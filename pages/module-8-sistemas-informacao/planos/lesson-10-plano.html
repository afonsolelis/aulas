About this session

Metrics and Telemetry · 09/12/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to instrument a DataApp with metrics, traces and structured logs linked by run, trace and span identifiers, follow a failure from the alert to the log line that explains it, and close the loop on one SLO with a controller that compares the indicator with its setpoint, acts within guardrails and escalates to a person when it cannot recover.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor builds the observability of the Lesson 7 package live with a coding agent and four CREATE prompts, injecting faults to follow a failure through the three signals and to watch the controller recover or escalate, and distinguishes observability, the sensor and display of the loop, from control, which compares and acts. In the second hour each group applies the method to its own DataApp with prompt 05.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. The three signals and their correlation (7 min)
  3. Step 01 — metrics in Prometheus (8 min)
  4. Step 02 — traces and structured logs with OpenTelemetry (8 min)
  5. Step 03 — the stack; from the alert to the log line (4 min)
  6. The closed loop, and why observability alone does not close it (4 min)
  7. Step 04 — the controller of the DataApp with injected faults (14 min)
  8. Second hour — the groups apply it to their DataApp (60 min)

2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to instrument a DataApp with metrics, traces and structured logs linked by run, trace and span identifiers, follow a failure from the alert to the log line that explains it, and close the loop on one SLO with a controller that compares the indicator with its setpoint, acts within guardrails and escalates to a person when it cannot recover.

Observable outcomes

  • Explain which question each signal answers: the metric says whether something is wrong, the trace says where, and the log says what the program knew at that moment.
  • Name metrics with the prefix of the application and the unit in base units, choose their type (counter, gauge, histogram or info) and keep identifiers out of the labels to control cardinality.
  • Explain why a batch run pushes its metrics to a Pushgateway and why the time of the last success is pushed only after a success.
  • Read the trace of a run as a tree of spans and locate the failing span by its status, its events and its recorded exception.
  • Write structured log lines that carry the run_id, trace_id and span_id of the span they describe.
  • Follow a failure in four hops, from the alert to the metric, to the trace and to the log lines of the failing span, using the identifiers that link them.
  • Map the elements of a closed loop (setpoint, sensor, comparator, controller, actuator, plant and feedback) to the DataApp and explain why observability alone leaves the loop open.
  • Specify guardrails for a controller (hysteresis, circuit breaker with backoff, cooldown, budget per window and a single escalation) and the failure mode that each one prevents.
  • Choose one SLO of the DataApp of the group, decide whether it has a safe actuator and prove with a test that its loop recovers or escalates once.

3. Session products

Observable reference

The package of Lesson 7 instrumented live in four steps, with telemetry.py, observability.py, the stack of six containers, control.py and 25 tests, compared at each step with lab/lesson-10/ and with the numbers of the rehearsal of 08/11/2026.

Three signals in the DataApp of the group

Metrics pushed with an info metric of the run and the trace, one span per run, task, call to the source and transformation, JSON log lines with the three identifiers, three alerting rules with promtool tests, and a failure rehearsed on purpose and followed from the alert to the log line.

One closed loop for one SLO

The setpoint and the dead band, the sensor, the actuator and what it changes, the cooldown, the budget and the condition to escalate, and a test that breaks the DataApp and proves the loop, recorded in docs/observability.md.

Continuity

The session closes the open risk of freshness left by Lesson 9, turning the check that nothing called on schedule into a metric with an alert and an SLO with an actuator. Lesson 11 turns the DataApp into technical infographics, with charts chosen for the data and dashboards built for the people who read them.

Students

  • Complete the self-studies on Adalove: Prometheus Metric Types Explained (30 min), Introduction to the Prometheus Monitoring System (video, 20 min) and Prometheus vs OpenTelemetry Metrics: A Complete Guide (40 min).
  • Read the Lesson 10 material, at least Parts I and V, on the three signals and on the closed loop.
  • Bring the DataApp of the group, the package of Lesson 7 or the flow of Lesson 8, with its virtual environment working and its tests passing.
  • Have Docker Compose installed and able to run six containers, a browser for Grafana and a coding agent ready.

Professor

  • Start from a copy of the package of Lesson 7 and keep the reference lab/lesson-10/ open for comparison at each step.
  • Pull in advance the images pinned in stack/compose.yaml (Prometheus 3.15.0, Pushgateway 1.11.2, collector 0.141.0, Tempo 2.10.10, Loki 3.6.3 and Grafana 13.2.4) and check whether port 3000 is free, using GRAFANA_PORT=3001 otherwise.

  • Prepare the injected faults TCG_FAULT=source_down and duplicate_prices, with the short guardrails of the rehearsal (a backoff of 20 s and ticks every 10 s).

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2 and present the agenda of slide 3, with the two hours and the six containers that the session uses.
  2. On slide 4, ask the class which of the three signals would have shown a failure of the DataApp at six in the morning, and present the cost of each one.
  3. Present the four hops of slide 5 and explain why the run_id lives in a single info metric and never as a label of every metric.
  4. Paste prompt 01 into the agent and, while it works, present the metric types and the push of slide 7; review the output against the rows 7, 1490, 657, 896, 1153, 1414 and 1831 listed on slide 6.
  5. Paste prompt 02 and present the tree of spans of slide 9; check that a failing task ends with status ERROR, its attempts as events, and a log line with the span_id of its task.
  6. Paste prompt 03, bring up the stack and run the DataApp with TCG_FAULT=source_down, following with the class the alert, the trace and the three log lines of the failing span; use slide 11 to show the queries of each store.
  7. Present the elements of the closed loop on slide 12 and contrast, on slide 13, the open loop, the loop closed by a person and the automatic closed loop, pointing out that the steps so far built the sensor and the display.
  8. Present the four guardrails of slide 14 and the failure mode that each one prevents.
  9. Paste prompt 04 and, while the agent writes the controller, present the table of actuators of slide 16 and the last known good state.
  10. Rehearse the loop with source_down and duplicate_prices, comparing each tick with slide 17, and check that the 25 tests pass, the 7 of the loop included.
  11. Use slide 18 to show that the same structure appears in the reconciliation loop of Kubernetes, in the Horizontal Pod Autoscaler, in a webhook of Alertmanager and in the error budget.
  12. At 15:00, present the expected result of slide 19 and launch prompt 05; circulate among the groups, checking first that no identifier became a label and that each chosen SLO states whether it has a safe actuator.
  13. Close with the self-study of slide 20 and the passage to Lesson 11.

7. Methodology

The first hour is a demonstration in which the professor builds the observability of a package that the class already knows, with a coding agent and four CREATE prompts, and uses the time in which the agent works to explain the concept that each step implements. Each prompt states the facts measured in the rehearsal of 08/11/2026, so that the class can compare the result of the agent with numbers known in advance and recognise when a step has gone wrong. The faults are injected on purpose, because a failure that the class follows from the alert to the log line shows the value of the shared identifiers more directly than a description of them.

The session presents observability as the sensor and the display of a loop and introduces control theory to show what is still missing: something that compares the measurement with the objective and acts on the process. The guardrails of the controller are treated as part of the design, since an automatic action that oscillates or repeats without limit can do more harm than the failure it addresses. In the second hour each group transfers the method to its own DataApp with prompt 05, and the measure of success is a failure rehearsed on purpose and followed in four hops, together with one SLO whose loop either recovers or escalates once.

8. Assessment of learning

Product of the second hour

The session has no graded activity. The learning is verified by the product that each group reaches at the end of the second hour: the changes in the repository of the group, the output of pytest and of promtool, and docs/observability.md with the SLO, the diagram of its loop and the path of the rehearsed failure. When an SLO has no safe actuator, the group states it, and that SLO stays as an alert to a person, with the reason in the README.

The checklist of the material serves as self-verification for each group.

  • Every metric has the prefix of the application and its unit in the name, and no label takes an identifier.
  • A run is one trace, with one span per task, per call to the source and per transformation, and a failure sets the status of its span.
  • Every log line is JSON and carries run_id, trace_id and span_id.
  • Every alert describes a symptom, has a reason for its threshold and a promtool test.
  • From an alert, a person reaches the log line of the failing span in four hops.
  • The loop has hysteresis, a cooldown, a budget per window and one escalation, and a test breaks the DataApp and proves that it recovers or escalates once.

10. Risks and contingencies

Port 3000 already taken

Grafana is brought up with GRAFANA_PORT=3001, as the prompt of step 03 foresees.

Links to Explore that do nothing

With anonymous access as Viewer, Grafana redirects the links to Explore back to the dashboard; the stack sets GF_USERS_VIEWERS_CAN_EDIT, acceptable on a local stack and not on a shared one.

Alert test that fails intermittently

The recording rules stay in the same group as the alerts that read them, before them, because in separate groups the order of evaluation is not fixed and the test of the alert failed about one run in three in the rehearsal.

Demonstration exceeding the first hour

The results of slides 11 and 17 can be shown from the rehearsal of 08/11/2026 instead of reproduced live, preserving the 60 minutes of the second hour for the groups.

SLO of the group without a safe actuator

The group states it, keeps that SLO as an alert to a person and records the reason in the README, as prompt 05 requires.

Credentials of the partner exposed

Prompt 05 forbids putting credentials of the partner in the repository, in the logs or in span attributes, and the log lines carry only counts and errors, never records of the source.
