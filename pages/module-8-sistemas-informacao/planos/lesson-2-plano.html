Module 8 · Information Systems · Lesson 2

Lesson plan · Advanced Exploratory Data Analysis

Flipped session in which the groups, after running Steps 1 and 2 of the starter notebook at home, conduct Steps 3 to 8 of the exploratory analysis on one partner table with an AI assistant that receives only the schema and aggregated outputs, record the findings in a data quality log linked to the Data Model Canvas, write an idempotent Parquet sample to the raw zone of the Data Lake and present what they achieved to the class.

About this session

Advanced Exploratory Data Analysis · 19/10/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to conduct the eight-step exploratory analysis on the partner data with an AI assistant, sending it only the schema and aggregated outputs and reproducing in the notebook every finding it proposes, record the findings in a data quality log, use the log to fill the blocks of the Data Model Canvas, store a sample in the Data Lake with an idempotent Parquet write, and present the findings, their volume and the canvas blocks they change to the class.

Session strategy

Flipped session. Before class, each member prepares the environment, runs Steps 1 and 2 of the starter notebook on synthetic data and reads the material on the first steps. The session opens with an oral entry check and a focused exposition of Steps 3 to 8 and of the discovery loop with an AI assistant, in which the assistant writes the checks and interprets aggregated outputs while the group runs the code and decides. The groups then conduct Steps 3 to 8 on the partner data with the prompts of the session, record the findings in the data quality log, turn them into canvas entries and write a Parquet sample, and each group presents what it achieved to the class, which asks one question per group.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. Entry check on the pre-class notebook — 5 min
  3. Focused exposition: steps 3 to 8 and the discovery loop with an AI assistant — 15 min
  4. Group practice on the partner data: AI-assisted steps 3 to 8, quality log, canvas and Parquet — 45 min
  5. Group presentations: what each group achieved — 30 min
  6. Sprint 1 deliverables and self-study — 10 min

2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to conduct the eight-step exploratory analysis on the partner data with an AI assistant, sending it only the schema and aggregated outputs and reproducing in the notebook every finding it proposes, record the findings in a data quality log, use the log to fill the blocks of the Data Model Canvas, store a sample in the Data Lake with an idempotent Parquet write, and present the findings, their volume and the canvas blocks they change to the class.

Observable outcomes

  • Explain what the grain of a table means and tell apart a repeated key caused by a duplicated extract from a repeated key that reveals a finer grain.
  • Apply Steps 3 to 7 in order (structure and grain, completeness, univariate profile, relationships and consistency across tables) and justify why the grain is tested before any distribution.
  • Conduct the discovery loop with an AI assistant, sending only the schema and aggregated outputs and keeping in the notebook the cell that reproduces each finding.
  • Record each verified finding as a row of the data quality log, with volume, probable cause, decision and the canvas block it changes.
  • Translate a pandas check into its PySpark equivalent for the Sprint 1 notebook.
  • Write a sample to the raw zone of the Data Lake as Parquet partitioned by ingestion date, with an idempotent write, and verify types and row count by reading it back.
  • Present three findings with their volume, one invariant violation, the canvas blocks affected, the Parquet sample and the next step.

3. Session products

Notebook with Steps 3 to 8

The starter notebook completed on the synthetic data and applied to one partner table that supports a question of block 2, with one cell per finding and a final cell that writes and reads back the Parquet sample.

Data quality log and canvas entries

One row per verified finding, kept under /docs/data_model_canvas/, with the canvas blocks it supports and the filled prompts saved in prompts.md.

Group presentation

About four minutes per group with the five elements of slide 23, followed by one question from the class about the evidence behind a finding, its decision or a cause suggested by the assistant.

Continuity

The notebook and the data quality log are the evidence of the Data Discovery with Data Model Canvas deliverable, presented at the Sprint 1 Review on 23/10. The zones of the Data Lake designed today are the starting point of Lesson 3, which reorganises them into the Bronze, Silver and Gold layers and decides the treatment in Silver of findings such as duplicated stock counts.

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2.
  2. Present the objective and structure on slide 3, recall the pre-class tasks on slides 4 and 5 and conduct the entry check of slide 6: each question is answered first by show of hands and then explained by a volunteer with the output of the notebook on screen.
  3. Use the answers to decide which of Steps 3 to 8 need more time in the focused exposition.
  4. Present the eight steps on slide 7 and the three panels of slide 8, contrasting a repeated key with identical rows and a repeated key with different rows, and stating that a finer grain can always be aggregated into a coarser one.
  5. Explain on slide 9 the division of work between the group and the assistant and the rule on what may be sent, and show the four prompts on slides 10 and 11.
  6. Go through Steps 3 to 7 on slides 12 to 15, stressing that the exploration measures and records findings and leaves corrections to the Silver layer.
  7. Present the data quality log on slide 16, recalling that its figures belong to the illustrative case.
  8. Play the animation of slide 17 to show how Spark plans, partitions, shuffles and returns the grain check, and show on slide 18 the PySpark equivalent of each step and on slide 19 the local alternatives for studying distributed processing.
  9. Start the 45-minute practice of slide 20, asking each group to run the cells first on the synthetic data and then on one partner table chosen for a question of block 2.
  10. Circulate among the groups checking that only aggregated outputs are pasted into the assistant and that each finding in the log has a cell that reproduces it.
  11. In the last fifteen minutes of the practice, point the groups to the bucket convention of slide 21 and the idempotent write of slide 22, and remind them to prepare the five elements of the presentation.
  12. Conduct the presentations of slide 23, inviting the class to ask one question per group and noting findings that recur across groups.
  13. Close with the Sprint 1 deliverables and Lesson 3 on slide 24 and the self-studies on slide 25.

7. Methodology

The session adopts a flipped format, because the preparation that each member can do alone (installing the environment, running the first two steps on synthetic data and reading the procedure) does not need the group or the professor. The time in class is reserved for the analysis of the partner data and the discussion of its findings, and the entry check at the start verifies that preparation and tells the professor which steps of the focused exposition need more detail. Running the cells first on synthetic data, whose defects are known, ensures that the code works before it meets the partner data and also tests the suggestions of the assistant against defects introduced on purpose.

The AI assistant writes the code of each check and interprets its aggregated outputs, while the group runs the code, reproduces each finding and decides. This division follows from what each side can verify: the assistant never sees the data and may propose causes that the output does not support, so every number of the log is read from a cell of the notebook and not from the reply of the assistant. The same rule keeps the partner data, subject to the policies of block 8 and to the LGPD, inside the control of the group. The presentations at the end expose the findings to the class while they can still change the canvas, before the Sprint Review, and force each group to select the findings that matter for the partner.

8. Assessment of learning

Formative check through the log and the presentation

The session has no graded activity. Learning is verified through the data quality log and the presentation of each group, which must show three findings with their volume, one invariant violation with its probable cause, the canvas blocks that the findings change, the Parquet path with the row count read back and the next step for the deliverable. The notebook and the log are later assessed as part of the Data Discovery with Data Model Canvas deliverable, with weight 4, at the Sprint 1 Review.

  • Every finding in the log is reproduced by a cell of the notebook.
  • Only the schema and aggregated outputs were sent to the assistant, never rows of the partner data.
  • Each log row has a volume, a probable cause, a decision and a canvas block.
  • The canvas text drafted with Prompt 4 cites the finding ID behind each line.
  • The Parquet sample is partitioned by ingestion date, written idempotently and read back with matching types and row count.

10. Risks and contingencies

Members without the environment working

The member works with a colleague whose environment runs, and completes the checklist of section 5 after the session, since the pre-class tasks are a condition for taking part in the practice.

Partner rows pasted into the assistant

The professor stops the conversation, recalls the table of what may be sent on slide 9 and asks the group to resend only the aggregates computed in the notebook.

Group correcting the data during exploration

The group restores the original cells and records the finding with its volume in the log, leaving the correction to a rule of the Silver layer.

Presentations running over thirty minutes

Each group shows only the five elements of slide 23, read from the log, and the steps not finished in class are completed before the Sprint Review.
