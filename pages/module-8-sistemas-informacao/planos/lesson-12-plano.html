Lesson plan · Cloud Infrastructure Pricing

A two-hour session in which the professor prices on AWS the DataApp of Lesson 7, in four steps with a coding agent, the AWS Pricing Calculator and the browser lab (measured workload, five candidate architectures, estimate in two regions and optimisation), after which each group estimates, compares and optimises the architecture of its own DataApp and delivers the documents by pull request.

About this session

Cloud Infrastructure Pricing · 16/12/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to estimate the monthly cost of the infrastructure of a DataApp from its measured workload, with the AWS Pricing Calculator and with the prices of the AWS Price List, compare alternative architectures, regions and pricing models, and propose an optimisation based on right-sizing, scheduling, commitments and Spot, applying it to the DataApp of the group.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor prices on AWS the DataApp of Lesson 7 in four steps with a coding agent, the AWS Pricing Calculator and the browser lab of the session: the measurement of the workload, five candidate architectures and their billing dimensions, the estimate in two regions and the optimisation. In the second hour each group estimates, compares and optimises the architecture of its own DataApp and delivers the documents and the exported estimates by pull request.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. Cost factors and pricing models: on-demand, commitments, Spot and free allowances — 10 min
  3. Step 01 · the measured workload of tcg-dataapp — 8 min
  4. Step 02 · five architectures and what each one bills — 10 min
  5. Step 03 · the estimate in the AWS Pricing Calculator and in the lab — 12 min
  6. Step 04 · optimisation: size, schedule, commitment, Spot and region — 10 min
  7. Second hour · each group estimates, compares and optimises its own architecture — 55 min

2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to estimate the monthly cost of the infrastructure of a DataApp from its measured workload, with the AWS Pricing Calculator and with the prices of the AWS Price List, compare alternative architectures, regions and pricing models, and propose an optimisation based on right-sizing, scheduling, commitments and Spot, applying it to the DataApp of the group.

Observable outcomes

  • Explain a cloud bill as the sum of counted quantities of billing dimensions multiplied by their unit prices, and distinguish dimensions counted by time from dimensions counted by use.
  • Compare on-demand, Compute Savings Plan, Reserved Instance or EC2 Instance Savings Plan and Spot prices for the same instance, and compute the utilisation of break-even of a commitment.
  • Measure the workload of a DataApp (wall time, CPU time, peak memory, data stored and downloaded) and record the origin of each quantity as measured, assumption or decision of the group.
  • Describe candidate architectures by what each one keeps billed while working and while idle, including volumes, public IPv4 addresses and databases.
  • State the limits that decide the choice of service, such as the 15 minutes of Lambda and the minimum of one minute billed by Fargate and EC2.
  • Fill the AWS Pricing Calculator for an architecture in São Paulo and in Virginia, save the estimate with a share link and export it as CSV.
  • Read unit prices from the AWS Price List with their offer, usage type and date, and check the calculator against a cost model in code.
  • Measure the saving of right-sizing, scheduling, commitments, Spot and region on the same workload, in USD per month and in percent.
  • Write a recommendation with its monthly total, shown with and without the free allowances, and the condition under which it stops being valid.

Continuity

The session prices the package built in Lesson 7 and scheduled in Lesson 8, and uses the idempotent load of Lesson 7 to decide whether Spot is acceptable. Lesson 13 closes the module with the security, authorizations and access controls of the DataApp.

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2 and confirm that each group has its DataApp running locally for the measurement of the second hour.
  2. Present the agenda of slide 3, stating that the first hour prices tcg-dataapp and the second prices the DataApp of each group with the same four steps.
  3. Go through the table of slide 4, asking the class which quantity of the workload drives each line, and use slide 5 to compute with the class the break-even of 0.0105 / 0.0168 = 62.5%.
  4. Paste prompt 01 into the agent and, while it runs the measurement, explain why the origin of each quantity is recorded; review the three questions of slide 6 and read slide 7, insisting that the peak memory, and not the average, sets the size.
  5. Paste prompt 02 and ask the class, before showing slide 9, what each architecture keeps billed while idle; confirm that the run of forty seconds fits the 15 minutes of Lambda.
  6. Paste prompt 03, fill the calculator live for one architecture in one region, save it with a share link and compare its total with the cost model and with the lab, as slide 11 describes.
  7. Read the five totals of slide 12 and the largest line of each, and explain why the totals are shown with and without the free allowances of the account.
  8. Paste prompt 04 and use slide 14 to measure each lever on architecture A, including the 96% saving of scheduling and the reason why Standard-IA would cost more for the marts.
  9. Read the sensitivity table and the recommendation of slide 15, stressing the condition under which architecture C stops being valid.
  10. Project slide 16, hand prompt 05 to the groups and go from group to group, checking first that the workload is the one of the group DataApp and that no resource billed while idle was forgotten.
  11. In the last minutes, point to the self-studies of slide 17 and confirm that each group knows what goes in the pull request.

7. Methodology

The session follows the pattern of demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor prices tcg-dataapp in four steps, each opened by a CREATE prompt to the coding agent and closed by a review with three questions, and the time in which the agent works is used to explain the concept of the step: the billing dimensions while the workload is measured, the limits of each service while the architectures are described, the two sources of prices while the estimate is built, and the levers of cost while the optimisation is written. Since every number of the demonstration was measured or read from the AWS Price List on 08/11/2026, the class can trace each total back to a quantity and a unit price.

The second hour transfers the method to the DataApp of each group, whose workload, architectures and requirements differ from those of the reference package. The calculator gives the format that a manager or a partner receives, the lab gives a second estimate in which the quantities and prices of the group can be edited, and the comparison between both exposes typed quantities and forgotten resources, which are the most common mistakes listed in section 21 of the material.

8. Assessment of learning

Evidence delivered by the groups

The session has no graded activity. Learning is verified on the documents and estimates that each group delivers by pull request, read against the list of what to produce and how to check on slide 16 and against the common mistakes of section 21 of the material.

  • The workload of the group DataApp is measured, and each quantity records its origin.
  • At least three architectures are described with a diagram and the table of their billing dimensions.
  • Every architecture is estimated in the calculator in São Paulo and in Virginia, with share links and CSV files in estimates/.
  • Every resource billed while idle appears in the estimate: volumes, addresses, databases, NAT gateways and load balancers.
  • Every unit price has a source and a date, and the totals are shown with and without the free allowances.
  • The optimisation measures each lever in USD per month and in percent, and the recommendation states its monthly total and the condition that would change it.

10. Risks and contingencies

Prices changed since the snapshot

The prices of the session were read on 08/11/2026 and may change; the group records the date of each price, and the AWS Pricing Calculator remains the reference for a real estimate.

Calculator and code disagreeing

The group checks the typed quantities and the forgotten resources first, and records in docs/cost_estimate.md the explanation of any difference that remains.

Group DataApp that does not run locally

The group estimates from the quantities it can document, marks them as assumptions and replaces them by measures as soon as the run works.

Demonstration exceeding the first hour

The theory slides of steps 02 and 04 can be presented only through their result tables, with reference to sections 7, 8 and 12 to 17 of the material, so that the groups keep their hour.

11. Closing

The session closes with the recommendation for tcg-dataapp, architecture C at less than one cent per month with the free allowances and $0.11 without them, valid while a run stays well below the 15 minutes of Lambda, and with the groups starting their own estimates. The ideas retained are that every line of a bill is a quantity times a unit price, that the largest lines of a DataApp that works 0.05% of the month are the resources billed while idle, and that a recommendation is presented with the condition under which it stops being valid. The professor records the forgotten resources found in the groups, to retake them in the review of the pull requests.
