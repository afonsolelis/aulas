Lesson plan · Data Security, Authorizations, and Access

The last session of the module, in which the professor applies six data protection controls to the DataApp of a fictitious card shop, live, with a coding agent and a PostgreSQL that runs inside the browser (inventory under the LGPD, roles of least privilege, row level security, pseudonymisation with k-anonymity, an append-only audit log and an automated access test), closes with a case study of how the portal protects its exams, and then has each group apply the same controls to the partner data in ClickHouse.

About this session

Data Security, Authorizations, and Access · 17/12/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to classify the personal data of a DataApp under the LGPD and implement, in the database, the access controls that follow from it (roles of least privilege, row level security, pseudonymisation with k-anonymity and an append-only audit log), verifying them with an automated access test, first in the card shop of the lab and then on the partner data in ClickHouse.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor applies six controls to the DataApp of a fictitious card shop, live, with a coding agent and six CREATE prompts, in a PostgreSQL (PGlite) that runs inside the browser, proving each control with SET ROLE and closing with a case study of how this portal protects its exams. In the second hour each group applies the same controls to the partner data in the ClickHouse of the class (shrek2) with the seventh prompt.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. The LGPD, data governance and security in layers (5 min)
  3. Steps 01–02: inventory of personal data; roles and least privilege (12 min)
  4. Steps 03–04: row level security; pseudonymisation and k-anonymity (14 min)
  5. Steps 05–06: audit log; the access policy as an automated test; case study of the exams of the portal (14 min)
  6. Second hour: the six controls on the partner data in ClickHouse (60 min)

2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to classify the personal data of a DataApp under the LGPD and implement, in the database, the access controls that follow from it (roles of least privilege, row level security, pseudonymisation with k-anonymity and an append-only audit log), verifying them with an automated access test, first in the card shop of the lab and then on the partner data in ClickHouse.

Observable outcomes

  • Distinguish personal, sensitive, pseudonymised and anonymised data under articles 5, 12 and 13 of the LGPD, and relate the principles of purpose, necessity, security, prevention and accountability to controls of a database.
  • Write an inventory of personal data as a table of the database, with category, type of identifier, purpose, legal basis, retention and analytics decision for each column, tested against the catalogue.
  • Map the jobs of data governance to roles of least privilege, granting privileges to jobs and revoking them from PUBLIC.
  • Enable and force row level security with a policy over the acting role, and explain why a policy over a setting that the user can change is an anti-pattern.
  • Build pseudonymised views with a peppered hash, generalise quasi-identifiers and publish an aggregate only for groups with at least five customers.
  • Write an append-only audit log that records the names of the changed columns and never their values, and serve reads of contact data through a function that demands a purpose.
  • Turn the access policy into an automated test that runs every probe as every role inside a subtransaction that is rolled back, and read a regression caused by a careless grant.
  • Explain, on the case of the exams of this portal, how deny by default, a single SECURITY DEFINER function, a secret stored as a hash and a log of attempts combine.
  • Apply the same controls in ClickHouse with roles, grants, row policies, hash functions and system.query_log.

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2 and confirm that each group can reach shrek2 for the second hour.
  2. Present the agenda of slide 3 and open the lab, stating that the first hour secures the card shop and the second secures the DataApp of the group.
  3. Use slide 4 to ask the class which columns of the shop are personal, which one is sensitive and why, and slide 5 to relate each job of governance to a role and to the layers of defence in depth.
  4. Show on slide 6 that the session is the superuser and that every proof runs under SET ROLE, recalling that the data is synthetic and must stay so in any DataApp.
  5. Paste prompt 01, run the script in the lab and review the three questions of slide 7; read the result of slide 8 and explain why quasi-identifiers are generalised.
  6. Paste prompt 02 and, while the agent works, explain on slide 10 group roles, the ETL that writes without reading and default privileges; review that no table keeps a privilege of PUBLIC.
  7. Paste prompt 03 and run the same query as manager_sp, manager_rj and the superuser, as on slide 12; show that app_owner sees no customer under FORCE and discuss the anti-pattern of the policy over a setting.
  8. Paste prompt 04, read the views as the analyst and use slide 14 to separate pseudonymised data, which is still personal, from the anonymised aggregate, and to explain where the pepper lives in production.
  9. Paste prompt 05, produce the three events and read them as the DPO on slide 16, insisting that the log keeps the names of the columns and never their values.
  10. Present the case study of slide 17, describing only the access mechanism of the exams (RLS with no policy, the single function, the token compared as a hash, the log of attempts and the secrets outside git), and ask the class how shop.lookup_customer could make its refusals visible to the DPO.
  11. Paste prompt 06, run the access matrix of slide 19 and then the careless grant, showing the two failures and the test green again after the REVOKE.
  12. Project slide 20, hand prompt 07 to the groups and go from group to group, checking first that the inventory precedes any grant and that no secret enters the repository.
  13. In the last minutes, read the closing table of slide 21 as the synthesis of the module and point to the self-studies of slide 22.

7. Methodology

The session follows the pattern of demonstration followed by application, in a lab that runs a real PostgreSQL in the browser so that nothing needs to be installed and every control can be proved by switching role and running the same query. Each of the six steps is opened by a CREATE prompt to the coding agent and closed by a review with three questions, and the time in which the agent writes the script is used to explain the article of the LGPD or the mechanism of PostgreSQL that the step implements. The order of the steps reproduces defence in depth, since each control adds a layer that still works when the previous one fails, and the last step turns the whole policy into a test that detects when a change weakens any layer.

The case study of the exams of this portal shows the same controls applied to a real problem of the course, a public repository that must not expose an exam before the session, and adds two decisions that the lab leaves open: returning a refusal as data so that the attempt is recorded, and checking the right token before the limit of attempts. The second hour transfers the controls to ClickHouse, whose syntax and behaviour differ, so that the groups must translate the ideas and confirm with a query as each role that the policy holds on the partner data.

8. Assessment of learning

Evidence delivered by the groups

The session has no graded activity. Learning is verified on the four documents that each group delivers in its repository, listed on slide 20 and in section 15 of the material, and read against the checklist of section 20.

  • Every field of the group ingestion table and of the gold views is classified, with the analytics decision of each.
  • The jobs of the DataApp are written with the expected access of each, as the table of step 06.
  • The security script creates roles and grants, a row policy when the partner has a regional or business cut, a pseudonymised view and an aggregate with k = 5, and ends with SHOW GRANTS.
  • The audit document lists the reads of the personal tables of the group in system.query_log.
  • The pepper and the credentials of the group never enter the repository.

10. Risks and contingencies

Lab replaying an old state

The lab replays from the localStorage every statement that changed the database; if the state diverges from the steps, the professor clears it and runs the scripts again in order.

Proof run as the superuser

The superuser bypasses every policy, so a proof that shows all rows usually means that SET ROLE was missing; every script ends with RESET ROLE, as section 18 of the material describes.

Group user without ACCESS MANAGEMENT

The group writes the statements of roles, grants and policies as a reference that the administrator of shrek2 runs, and confirms the result with SHOW GRANTS.

Demonstration exceeding the first hour

The theory of step 02 and the result of step 05 can be shown only through their tables, with reference to sections 8 and 11 of the material; the case study and step 06 are kept, because they close the argument of defence in depth.
