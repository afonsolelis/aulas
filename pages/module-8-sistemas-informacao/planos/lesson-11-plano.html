Module 8 · Information Systems · Lesson 11

Lesson plan · DataApp Technical Infographics

A two-hour session in which the professor adds to the DataApp of the TCGplayer prices, live and with a coding agent, a Streamlit app in the views layer, with a technical infographic generated from metadata, a ranking with drill-down checked against the cube and prices over time reviewed for accessibility and ethics, after which each group builds the same kind of app for the DataApp of the partner.

About this session

DataApp Technical Infographics · 10/12/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to choose the chart that fits each question of the DataApp, build a Streamlit app in its views layer with a technical infographic generated from metadata and analytical views with drill-down over the gold layer, and review the app for accessibility, time zones and the ethics of the chart, first with the TCGplayer prices and then with the partner data.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor builds the app live with a coding agent in four CREATE prompts (queries and metadata, technical infographic, ranking with drill-down, time and accessibility) over the package of Lesson 7, comparing each step with the reference package 0.5.0. In the second hour each group adds a Streamlit app to the DataApp of the partner with prompt 05.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. Chart selection and principles of perception — 10 min
  3. Step 01 · Queries of the view layer and metadata — 10 min
  4. Step 02 · Streamlit and the technical infographic — 10 min
  5. Step 03 · Ranking and drill-down checked against the cube — 10 min
  6. Step 04 · Prices over time, UTC, accessibility and ethics — 10 min
  7. Groups · Streamlit app for the DataApp of the partner — 60 min
  8. Self-study and next steps

2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to choose the chart that fits each question of the DataApp, build a Streamlit app in its views layer with a technical infographic generated from metadata and analytical views with drill-down over the gold layer, and review the app for accessibility, time zones and the ethics of the chart, first with the TCGplayer prices and then with the partner data.

Observable outcomes

  • Associate each question of the DataApp with the job of the data (comparing magnitudes, change over time, part of a whole, hierarchy, one headline number, how the system works) and with the form that the eye reads with the least effort.
  • Explain the order of perceptual precision measured by Cleveland and McGill and apply it through highlight and context, direct labels and a recessive frame.
  • Write the queries of the app in a module without Streamlit, over a read-only connection, with dimensions and measures taken from fixed lists and filter values passed as parameters.
  • Generate the lineage of the warehouse from duckdb_views() and from the comment of the frozen tables, and the graph of the run from ops.pipeline_tasks, without importing the pipeline into the view layer.
  • Explain the execution model of Streamlit and the role of st.cache_resource and st.session_state in the drill-down.
  • Check an analytical view against gold.agg_price_cube at the right grouping_id and state when the check does not apply because the cube has no card.
  • Convert Unix time to a calendar day in UTC only when drawing, and explain why a capture at 00:00 UTC would otherwise fall on the previous day in São Paulo.
  • Review a chart against the accessibility criteria of WCAG 2.2 adopted in the app and against the five ethical rules of the chart.
  • Test the queries alone and the whole app with AppTest, without a browser and without network.

3. Session products

Reference app 0.5.0

The Streamlit app built live over a copy of the Lesson 7 package, with three tabs (technical infographic, ranking and drill-down, prices over time) and 22 tests passing in about ten seconds, compared at each step with the reference package in lab/lesson-11/tcg_dataapp/.

App of the partner DataApp

In each group, a Streamlit app in the views layer of the partner DataApp, built with prompt 05: a tested module of queries, a tab that explains the DataApp and at least two analytical views opened by a sentence, one of them with drill-down.

Reviewed figures

Every figure of the group app carries an alternative text, a table view and the SQL behind it, with one y axis, one accent colour, bars from zero and dates in UTC, so that the review can be checked by someone outside the group.

Continuity

The app reads the gold layer, the cube and the frozen reports built in Lessons 6 and 7 and the run log of the pipeline. Lesson 12 estimates how much this DataApp, its pipeline and its app would cost to run in the cloud and compares the configurations that would serve the partner.

4. Preparation

Students

  • Complete the three self-studies assigned on Adalove for the week: the dev.to article on chart types in Streamlit, the Streamlit crash course video and the viso.ai text on building interfaces in Streamlit.
  • Read the Lesson 11 material, at least Parts I to III, before the session.
  • Prepare the lab: clone the repository, copy the Lesson 7 package to a working folder, create the virtual environment, install the package with Streamlit and Altair, and run tcg-dataapp run so that the warehouse read by the app exists.
  • Have the coding agent of the group working in the terminal, and the access to the partner data (ClickHouse of the class or the warehouse of the group package) available for the second hour, with the credentials kept in environment variables.

Professor

  • Rehearse the four prompts over a fresh copy of the Lesson 7 package and confirm the reference numbers of 08/11/2026: 7 sets, 1,273 cards, 5,951 prices, 5 captures, 524 cells, 13 objects and 20 edges in the lineage.
  • Keep the reference package 0.5.0 open to compare each step and to recover the session if the agent diverges.
  • Check that Streamlit 1.67.0, Altair 6.3.0, DuckDB 2.0.0 and Python 3.12 are the versions installed on the demonstration machine.
  • Keep prompt 05 and the checklist of the second hour ready to be projected while the groups work.

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2 and note the blockers that affect the second hour, such as access to the partner data.
  2. Present the agenda of slide 3, making clear that the first hour builds the app over a copy of the Lesson 7 package and the second hour applies the same pattern to the DataApp of the partner.
  3. Use the table of slide 4 to ask the class which form answers each question of the DataApp before showing the column of forms, and use slide 5 to explain the order of perceptual precision and the four choices that the app never makes.
  4. Paste prompt 01 into the agent and ask it to show the plan before editing; while it works, explain on slide 7 the catalogue, the comment of the frozen table and the run log as the sources of the infographic, and the fixed lists that make the explorer safe by construction.
  5. Review step 01 with the three questions of slide 6 before moving on, including the test that a typed value such as x' OR '1'='1 returns no rows.
  6. Paste prompt 02, start the app with tcg-dataapp app and, while the agent works, explain the pieces of Streamlit on slide 9; compare the first tab with the numbers of slide 10 and confirm that the status of each task is written inside its box.
  7. Paste prompt 03 and explain on slide 12 why the click is stored as a pending change and why the key of the chart includes the depth of the path; check on slide 13 that both levels agree with the cube.
  8. Paste prompt 04 and use slide 15 to show how 1788220800 becomes 01/09/2026 in UTC and 31/08/2026 in São Paulo; go through the accessibility and ethics tables of slide 16, insisting that the jump of ME: 30th Celebration reflects its launch on 16/09/2026.
  9. Run pytest and read the table of slide 17, so that the class sees that the app was tested without a browser and that the 9 tests of Lesson 7 still pass.
  10. Project slide 18, hand prompt 05 to the groups and go from group to group, checking first that the connection is read-only, that the credentials of ClickHouse stay in environment variables and that each view opens with a sentence.
  11. In the last minutes, point to the self-studies of slide 19 and announce that Lesson 12 will price the DataApp in the cloud.

7. Methodology

The session follows the pattern of demonstration followed by application that the module adopted from Lesson 4 onwards. In the first hour the professor builds the app with a coding agent in four CREATE prompts, and the time in which the agent writes code is used to explain the concept that the step implements, so that the theory arrives at the moment the class sees its effect on the screen. Each step closes with a review against numbers measured in the rehearsal of 08/11/2026, which allows the class to recognise when an agent produced something different from what was asked, as happened in the rehearsal with labels that disappeared, deprecated parameters, decimal values and the inversion of the direction of the layers.

The second hour transfers the same method to the DataApp of the partner, where the data, the questions and the hierarchies belong to the group. Since prompt 05 asks for the queries tested alone, a tab that explains the DataApp and two analytical views opened by a sentence, the group work exercises the three ideas of the session together: the chart is chosen by the question, the technical figure is generated from metadata, and every figure is reviewed for accessibility and ethics before it is shown.

8. Assessment of learning

Evidence produced by the groups

The session has no graded activity. Learning is verified on the app that each group adds to the DataApp of the partner, read against the list of what to build and what to review on slide 18 and against the checklist of section 24 of the material.

  • The queries live in a module without Streamlit, over a read-only connection, and are tested without network.
  • Dimensions and measures come from fixed lists and filter values are parameters.
  • The first tab explains the DataApp with the size of the model, the last run and the lineage, generated from metadata.
  • At least two analytical views answer questions of the partner, each opened by a sentence with the number that matters, one of them with drill-down.
  • Every figure has one y axis, one accent colour, bars from zero, an alternative text, a table view and the SQL behind it.
  • Dates are shown in UTC from Unix time, and the credentials of ClickHouse are read from environment variables.

10. Risks and contingencies

Agent diverging from the prompt

The professor compares the result with the reference package 0.5.0 and, if the step cannot be recovered within its ten minutes, copies the corresponding file of the package and continues the demonstration from it.

Bars without labels or deprecated parameters

These were the problems of the rehearsal; the professor checks that the height is passed to st.altair_chart with theme=None and that the app uses width="stretch", as section 21 of the material describes.

Agent importing the pipeline into the views

The professor rejects the change and points to ops.pipeline_tasks, because the import would make the view layer depend on the module that sits above all layers.

Group without access to the partner data

The group builds the module of queries and the first tab over the warehouse of its own package and connects to ClickHouse when the access is restored, keeping the credentials in environment variables.

Demonstration exceeding the first hour

The theory slides of steps 03 and 04 can be presented only through their result, with reference to sections 11 to 16 of the material, so that the group work keeps most of its time.

Credentials committed by mistake

The professor asks the group to remove them from the repository at once and to move them to environment variables, as prompt 05 requires.

11. Closing

The session closes with the groups showing the first state of their app and with the self-studies of the week. The ideas retained are that the question chooses the chart, that a technical figure generated from the catalogue and from the run log describes the DataApp that ran, that every analytical view is checked against the model and opened by the sentence it supports, and that accessibility and ethics are reviewed before the app is shown. The professor records the points of the review that most groups missed, to retake them when the app is priced in Lesson 12.
