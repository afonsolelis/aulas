About this session

Automated Testing · 03/12/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to derive the automated tests of a DataApp from its quality attributes with the ATAM, building the business drivers, a rated utility tree and quality attribute scenarios in six parts, implementing test cases traced to the scenarios as pytest tests in Arrange, Act, Assert, and reading the results back into the tree as risks, non-risks, sensitivity points and tradeoffs.

Session strategy

Short exposition followed by individual work graded in class. In 25 minutes the professor presents automated testing with pytest (levels of test, Arrange, Act, Assert, fixtures, parametrize and markers) and the ATAM, applied in full to the package of Lesson 7, whose first run failed 5 of 29 tests in three scenarios. Each student then applies the six steps alone to the DataApp of the group, with the CREATE prompts of the lab and a coding agent, while the professor circulates; this work is the graded DAG and Testing Activity on Adalove, delivered individually as a branch with a Prefect pipeline and the tests, documented in a pull request, by Friday.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. Automated tests, levels of test and pytest — 10 min
  3. The ATAM: drivers, utility tree, six-part scenarios and findings; the reference — 15 min
  4. Steps 01–02 alone: drivers, attributes and utility tree — 15 min
  5. Step 03 alone: scenarios in six parts — 15 min
  6. Steps 04–05 alone: test cases, tests and first run — 35 min
  7. Step 06 alone: findings, second run and delivery — 15 min

2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to derive the automated tests of a DataApp from its quality attributes with the ATAM, building the business drivers, a rated utility tree and quality attribute scenarios in six parts, implementing test cases traced to the scenarios as pytest tests in Arrange, Act, Assert, and reading the results back into the tree as risks, non-risks, sensitivity points and tradeoffs.

Observable outcomes

  • Explain the three roles of an automated test in a DataApp (regression, specification and constraint on the design) with examples from the package of Lesson 7.
  • Place a test at its level (unit, integration, contract, end to end or performance) according to the size of what it exercises.
  • Write a pytest test in Arrange, Act, Assert, using fixtures for the environment, parametrize for variations of the stimulus and a marker that ties the test to its scenario.
  • Write four to six business drivers for the DataApp of the group and derive from them the quality attributes, each citing the driver that justifies it.
  • Build a utility tree whose leaves are situations that can happen to the DataApp, each rated (importance, difficulty), and state the order of analysis.
  • Write the high-priority leaves as scenarios in six parts, with a response measure that is a number or a verifiable condition, measuring first any number taken from the real data.
  • Derive one to three test cases per scenario, each traced to exactly one scenario and running without the network.
  • Run the suite on the code as it is, keep the first run with its failures and classify the results as risks, non-risks, sensitivity points and tradeoffs.
  • Put the suite in a pipeline of git.inteli.edu.br that runs it on every push and blocks the merge when a scenario fails.

Continuity

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2 and present the agenda of slide 3, stating from the start that the work after 14:40 is individual and graded.
  2. Use slide 4 to ask which properties of the DataApp were checked once by reading a result, and present the three roles of a test with the examples of Lesson 7.
  3. Present the levels of test on slide 5 and the test of S04 on slide 6, pointing to the Arrange, Act and Assert of the code and to the marker that ties it to its scenario.
  4. Present the six steps of the ATAM on slide 7, explaining that the session replaces the analysis by discussion with execution, so that a scenario without a test remains a hypothesis.
  5. Read the utility tree of slide 8 with the class, asking why S04 and S05 are rated (H,H) and why a leaf rated (L,L) can wait.
  6. Walk through the six parts of S07 on slide 9 and show how its three conditions yield two test cases.
  7. Show on slide 10 the traceability table printed by pytest and the pieces that produce it, from docs/scenarios.toml to tools/traceability.py.
  8. Present the four kinds of finding on slide 11 and the first run of slide 12, emphasising that the nine tests of Lesson 7 passed throughout because none of them reproduced the stimuli of S03, S04 and S11.
  9. At 14:40, read slide 13: the green suite as starting point, one prompt per step, the reference as form and not as content, and the delivery of the graded activity on Adalove.
  10. Circulate during steps 01 to 03, checking first that each attribute cites a driver, that each leaf is a situation and not a wish, and that each measure is a number or a verifiable condition.
  11. At 15:10, announce the passage to the test cases; during step 05, check that no student changes an assertion to make a test pass and that the first run is kept with its failures.
  12. At 15:45, ask for the classification of the findings with each finding citing its scenarios, and at 15:55 present step 07 and the conditions under which the merge request is blocked.
  13. Close with the self-study of slide 21, the delivery of the link by Friday, 04/12, and the passage to Lesson 10, which addresses the open risk of freshness.

7. Methodology

The session concentrates the exposition in 25 minutes and devotes it to a single worked example: the package of Lesson 7, which the class already knows, evaluated with the method from the drivers to the findings. Because the reference was tested as it was, its first run failed 5 of 29 tests, and those failures show in class that a test derived from a quality attribute reaches risks that tests written from memory had not reached. The ATAM is condensed into six steps, each producing a file of the repository, and the analysis by discussion of the original method is replaced by execution, so that the result of a test becomes the evidence about the architecture.

The remaining time is individual work on the DataApp of the group, guided by one CREATE prompt per step and by a coding agent, with time boxes that keep the order of the method and a condition to check at the end of each step. The professor circulates and checks first the points where the material records recurring mistakes: leaves written as wishes, measures that cannot be checked, assertions changed until the test passes and tests that reach the network. The work done in class is the graded activity itself, which makes the application of the method the evidence of learning.

8. Assessment of learning

In-class graded activity

The work of the session is the DAG and Testing Activity on Adalove, done individually on a branch of the project repository named graded-activity/student-name. The branch holds a Prefect pipeline, as built in Lesson 8, and the tests derived in the six steps, and the implementation, the testing approach and the instructions of use are documented in a pull request, which on git.inteli.edu.br is a merge request with the pipeline of step 07. Only the link is submitted, by Friday, 04/12/2026. The statement and the criteria are those of the activity on Adalove and are not reproduced in this public repository.

During the session each student checks the work against the conditions that close each step on slides 14 to 20 and against the checklist of the material.

  • The suite was green before the first prompt.
  • Each quality attribute cites the business driver that justifies it.
  • Each leaf of the utility tree is a situation, rated (importance, difficulty).
  • The scenarios of highest priority have six parts and a measurable response.
  • Each test case traces to one scenario, and each test carries the marker of its scenario.
  • The suite runs without the network and prints the traceability table, and the first run was kept with its failures.
  • The findings are classified as risks, non-risks, sensitivity points and tradeoffs, and the open risks are recorded with the reason.

10. Risks and contingencies

Suite of the group red before the first prompt

The student fixes the installation with pip install -e '.[dev]' until pytest passes, because a failure on the first run must come from the scenarios and not from an environment already broken.

Coding agent changing assertions

The prompt of step 05 forbids changing the assertion or fixing the DataApp in that step; the professor checks the first run kept by each student, since a failure at that point is a finding about the architecture.

Scenarios still empty at 15:10

The leaves without six parts stay in the tree, rated, and are listed as such in step 06, so that the time boxes preserve the passage to the tests.

Tests that reach the network

The fixture that blocks the network turns such a test into an immediate failure, which shows where the fake source of the DataApp of the group still needs to be adapted.

Pipeline pending in GitLab

Without a runner available to the project, the pipeline stays pending; the student keeps .gitlab-ci.yml on the branch and records the local run of the suite until the runner is available.

Exposition exceeding 25 minutes

Slides 10 and 11 can be shortened with a reference to sections 9 and 11 of the material, preserving slide 12, which shows what the first run reveals and motivates the individual work.

11. Closing

The session closes with the delivery of the individual branch and with the passage to Lesson 10. The method of the session turns each quality attribute that the partner depends on into a scenario with a measurable response and each response into a test, so that the results of the suite, read back into the utility tree, separate the risks found from the tradeoffs accepted on purpose. The risk of freshness left open in the reference, a check that exists and that nothing calls on schedule, is the starting point of the next session, which turns it into a metric with an alert.
