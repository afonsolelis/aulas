About this session

Learning the Dataset with Data Model Canvas · 15/10/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to explain the five RM-ODP viewpoints and the concern each one separates, describe the folders and schemas of the partner Data Package, and write the business and analytical consumption blocks of the Data Model Canvas for the partner from the enterprise and information viewpoints.

Session strategy

Session with in-class flipping. A fifty-minute exposition presents the RM-ODP, the order in which the module covers the viewpoints and the enterprise and information viewpoints on an illustrative case, followed by a discussion of the folders and schemas of the partner Data Package. The groups then write blocks 1 to 4 of the Data Model Canvas for the partner and commit the exported JSON, with the professor going from group to group. The session closes with the pre-class task for Lesson 2: environment, starter notebook and reading.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. Why the problem is modelled before the data is queried — 5 min
  3. RM-ODP: the five viewpoints and the module roadmap — 15 min
  4. Enterprise and information viewpoints on an illustrative case — 15 min
  5. The partner Data Package and its schemas — 20 min
  6. Groups write blocks 1 to 4 of the canvas for the partner — 35 min
  7. Pre-class task for Lesson 2, deliverables, self-study and workshop preparation — 15 min

2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to explain the five RM-ODP viewpoints and the concern each one separates, describe the folders and schemas of the partner Data Package, and write the business and analytical consumption blocks of the Data Model Canvas for the partner from the enterprise and information viewpoints.

Observable outcomes

  • Explain why the partner problem is described before the dataset is queried, using the difference between an inventory of columns and a reading guided by a decision.
  • Name the five RM-ODP viewpoints, the question each one answers and the sprints of the module in which each one is worked.
  • Describe the enterprise viewpoint of a case through its community, objective, roles, process and policies, including the LGPD classification of health data.
  • Distinguish the invariant, static and dynamic schemas of the information viewpoint and state the grain of a fact table in one sentence.
  • Trace a KPI back to an objective through the chain of correspondences between the enterprise and information viewpoints.
  • Describe the role of each of the seven folders of the partner Data Package and the grain of the raw execution files, the curated panels and the documentary families.
  • Write verifiable entries for blocks 1 to 4 of the Data Model Canvas, with each KPI carrying a formula, a unit and a periodicity.

3. Session products

Canvas blocks 1 to 4 for the partner

First version of the business objectives, analytical questions, business processes and KPIs of the partner, written by each group in class and exported as JSON to /docs/data_model_canvas/.

Open questions in block 6

What the group could not yet establish about the partner, written as questions with an owner, which feed the list of questions that the group takes to the partner workshop.

Environment ready for Lesson 2

Python 3.12, Java 17, Poetry, Docker, Docker Compose and MkDocs working on every member's machine, with Steps 1 and 2 of the starter notebook executed before Monday.

Continuity

The canvas written in this session is the basis of the Data Discovery with Data Model Canvas deliverable, due at the Sprint 1 Review on 23/10. Lesson 2 checks the entries against the partner data through the eight-step exploratory analysis, and the remaining blocks of the canvas are revised as the module reaches the computational, engineering and technology viewpoints.

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2 and note which groups already have access to the partner documents.
  2. Present the objective and the structure on slide 3, explaining that the exposition is kept to fifty minutes so that the groups produce the first version of their canvas in class and that the Metabase queries of the material are not run in this session.
  3. Use slide 4 to contrast the two ways of opening a dataset and ask the class which decision of the partner it already knows.
  4. Present the RM-ODP on slide 5 and the five viewpoints on slide 6, and show on slide 7 that Sprint 1 belongs to the enterprise and information viewpoints, so that tool choices made this week remain provisional.
  5. Define on slides 8 and 9 the concepts of the enterprise viewpoint and the three schemas of the information viewpoint, stressing that the grain is an information decision on which every KPI depends.
  6. Apply both viewpoints to the fictitious pharmacy chain on slides 10 and 11, and read the chain of correspondences of slide 12 in both directions, pointing out that a KPI without an objective leaves the canvas.
  7. Show on slide 13 which canvas groups are completed in Sprint 1 and on slide 14 the criteria of a complete entry for blocks 1 to 9 and a to f.
  8. Walk through the seven folders of the partner Data Package on slides 15 to 20, stating which folders feed the pipeline and which serve as benchmark, reference or framing.
  9. Discuss with the class the four questions of slide 21 (grain, identity, stages and limits), pointing to the charter as the main source for the answers.
  10. Start the 35-minute group work of slide 22 and go from group to group asking for the evidence behind each entry, checking first that the objectives are verifiable and that each KPI has a formula, a unit and a periodicity.
  11. Five minutes before the end of the group work, remind the groups to export the JSON and commit it under /docs/data_model_canvas/.
  12. Present the Sprint 1 deliverables on slide 23, the toolchain on slide 24 and the pre-class task for Monday on slide 25.
  13. Close with the self-studies of slide 26 and the preparation for the partner workshop on slide 27, asking each group to record its questions in the repository ordered by their effect on the Sprint 1 deliverables.

7. Methodology

The session adopts in-class flipping. The exposition is limited to fifty minutes and follows the order of the reasoning that the module asks of each group: the reason for describing the problem before the data, the RM-ODP as the framework that organises that description into viewpoints, the enterprise and information viewpoints applied to a fictitious case and, finally, the structure of the real partner dataset. Since the pharmacy case is easy to grasp and contains decision-makers, a recurring decision, rules on the use of data and tables whose grain must be understood, it allows the concepts to be shown in full before the groups face the larger and less familiar Data Package of the partner.

The group work transfers the reasoning to the partner while the professor is still in the room. Each group writes the business and analytical consumption blocks, the ones that belong to the enterprise and information viewpoints covered in the session, and records what it cannot establish as open questions with an owner, which become the agenda of the partner workshop. Versioning the canvas as JSON from the first session keeps a dated record of how the description of the partner changes during the sprint, and the pre-class task at the end prepares the flipped format of Lesson 2.

8. Assessment of learning

Formative check of the canvas blocks

The session has no graded activity. Learning is verified through the canvas blocks that each group writes and commits, read against the criteria of slide 14 and of section 9 of the material, which define an entry as complete when another reader with access to the partner and to the data can verify it. The same blocks are later assessed as part of the Data Discovery with Data Model Canvas deliverable, with weight 4, at the Sprint 1 Review.

  • Objectives in block 1 are stated as verifiable results, preferably with a direction and a measure.
  • Analytical questions in block 2 are phrased as the decision-maker would ask them.
  • Business processes in block 3 are named by a verb and an object.
  • Each KPI in block 4 has a formula, a unit and a periodicity and traces to an objective of block 1.
  • Unknowns are recorded in block 6 as questions with an owner.

10. Risks and contingencies

Exposition running over fifty minutes

Slides 16 to 20 can be presented through their tables only, with reference to the Data Package README and to the charter, so that the group work keeps its 35 minutes.

Group filling block 4 with dataset columns

The professor points to the first recurring mistake of section 12 of the material and asks the group which objective of block 1 the column would serve and with what formula.

Objectives that cannot be verified

The group rewrites the objective with a direction and a measure, or records the missing information in block 6 as a question for the partner workshop.

Group without access to the canvas or the repository

One member shares the screen with the canvas open, the group writes the blocks in that session, and the JSON is exported and committed as soon as access is restored, before Lesson 2.
