Lesson plan · Data Cubes and Data Marts

Plan of the session in which the professor builds, live and with a coding agent, a star schema, a cube and a data mart as views over a landing table of TCGplayer prices inside the browser, freezes a monthly report next to a live one and answers five OLAP questions, after which each group repeats the model over the partner data in the ClickHouse of the class.

About this session

Data Cubes and Data Marts · 10/11/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to model a data cube with at least three dimensions over a landing table, write its gold layer and data mart as views, decide when a report must be materialized and frozen in time, and answer multidimensional questions over it, first with TCGplayer prices and then with the partner data in ClickHouse.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor builds the model live with a coding agent in five steps (dimensions, fact, cube and mart, frozen and live reports, and five questions), pasting each CREATE prompt and running the SQL it writes in a lab page with DuckDB inside the browser, over a landing table kept in the localStorage; while the agent works, the slides explain the concept of the step, and each step closes with a review against the numbers of the rehearsal. In the second hour each group repeats the steps over the partner data in the ClickHouse of the class, starting with a specification that is reviewed before any SQL.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. Case, landing table and lab in the browser — 5 min
  3. Step 01: grain and dimensions as views — 10 min
  4. Step 02: the fact as a view; additivity of measures — 6 min
  5. Step 03: cube and data mart, as views — 10 min
  6. Step 04: frozen and live reports — 7 min
  7. Step 05: five OLAP questions — 7 min
  8. Second hour: the partner data in ClickHouse, in groups — 60 min

2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to model a data cube with at least three dimensions over a landing table, write its gold layer and data mart as views, decide when a report must be materialized and frozen in time, and answer multidimensional questions over it, first with TCGplayer prices and then with the partner data in ClickHouse.

Observable outcomes

  • Declare the business process and the grain of a fact table in one sentence before writing any SQL.
  • Write the dimensions of a star schema as views over the JSON of a landing table, each with its hierarchy.
  • Recognise a natural key that repeats, such as the set code 30C shared by two sets, and keep the identifier guaranteed by the source as the key of the model.
  • Distinguish transaction, periodic snapshot and accumulating snapshot fact tables, and justify the periodic snapshot for prices captured on fixed dates.
  • Classify each measure as additive, semi-additive or non-additive and keep the time dimension in every grouping of a semi-additive measure.
  • Prove the grain with a query that compares the number of rows with the number of distinct keys.
  • Write a cube with CUBE over three dimensions, identify each level by grouping_id and explain when ROLLUP or GROUPING SETS fit better.
  • Decide whether a report must be materialized and frozen in time or kept as a view that follows the last capture.
  • Name the OLAP operation (roll-up, drill-down, slice, dice or pivot) that answers a business question and write its query over the cube or the mart.
  • Reproduce the model over the partner data in ClickHouse, with every object prefixed by the group.

3. Session products

Model of the demonstration

The five SQL files of the lab (dimensions, fact, cube and mart, reports and questions), the specification specs/dimensional_model.md and the reference numbers measured on 07/11/2026, such as 5,951 rows for 5,951 keys of the grain and 524 cells in eight levels.

Model of each group in ClickHouse

The landing table lab.gNN_db_ingestion loaded with the enriched partner data, the dimensions, the fact and the cube as views, one report of a closed month frozen in a MergeTree table and the same report as a live view.

Answers of the partner

The file docs/answers.md with five questions of the partner, each answered with a number, the query that produced it and the OLAP operation it performs.

Continuity

The specification and the cube enter the Data Ingestion Structure artifact of the sprint as the analytical layer that the DataApp will read, and the five questions become the first indicators of its dashboard. Lesson 7 turns the SQL of this session into package data of an installable DataApp that rebuilds the model every day.

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2.
  2. Present the agenda of slide 3, making clear that the first hour builds the model live in the browser and that the second hour repeats it in groups over the partner data in ClickHouse.
  3. Introduce the case of slide 4 through the three questions of the collector, the store and the investor, and state the question of the session: which cards of the seven sets of 2026 appreciated most between their first capture and 07/11/2026.
  4. Show on slide 5 the landing table with its three columns and the convention of Unix time, and open the lab of slide 6, explaining that the landing table and every statement that creates an object are kept in the localStorage.
  5. Paste prompt 01 into the coding agent, approve its plan and, while it writes, explain the four decisions of Kimball, the hierarchies of the dimensions and the trap of the set code 30C (slides 9 to 11).
  6. Run the file in the lab and review the counts of the final SELECT against the prompt before moving on.
  7. Paste prompt 02 and, while the agent writes the fact, present the three kinds of fact table and the classification of measures; run the grain test and read the coverage of prices per capture date on slide 16.
  8. Paste prompt 03 and explain the cube, the CUBE clause and grouping_id; on slide 21, ask the class why the sum of US$ 51,967.84 over the five dates has no meaning before giving the answer of the semi-additive measure.
  9. Present the data mart and the bus matrix of slide 22, showing that a second mart would reuse the same dimensions.
  10. Paste prompt 04, explain on slide 24 when a report must be frozen and when it must follow the source, and run the demonstration in which a simulated capture changes only the live report.
  11. Paste prompt 05, present the five operations of OLAP on slide 27 and read the results of Q1 to Q5, stressing on slide 29 that the jump of the 30th Celebration reflects coverage and on slide 31 that Q5 compares the same card and variant on two dates.
  12. At 15:00, present the steps of the second hour on slide 32 and prompt 06 on slide 33, insisting that the agent stops after the specification so that the group reviews grain, measures and questions before any SQL reaches the shared server.
  13. Circulate among the groups, checking first that every object carries the prefix of the group and that the grain test passes on the fact of the group.
  14. Close with the delivery checks of slide 34 and the self-study of slide 35.

7. Methodology

The first hour is a demonstration in which the professor builds the dimensional model with a coding agent while the class watches the specification, the SQL and the results appear. Each step starts with a CREATE prompt that carries the numbers measured in the rehearsal, and the time that the agent takes to plan and write is used to present the concept that the step implements, so that the theory reaches the class at the moment it is needed and is immediately checked against a result. Because the lab runs DuckDB compiled to WebAssembly inside the browser, nothing needs to be installed, and a reader who missed the session can reproduce every number with the reference SQL.

The second hour transfers the same decisions to the partner data, in the ClickHouse of the class, where the syntax changes and the reasoning stays the same. Prompt 06 asks the agent of the group for the specification first and stops there, which makes the group decide the grain, the classification of the measures and the five questions of the partner before any statement reaches the shared server. The delivery is verified by statements that the professor can run on shrek2, so that the evidence of the group work is the state of its objects in the database and the answers it records.

8. Assessment of learning

Delivery of the group (not graded)

The session has no graded activity. The evidence of learning is the repository of each group, with the files asked by prompt 06, and the objects of the group in the database lab of shrek2, verified with the statements of the delivery slide.

The professor checks the delivery against the following items, each tied to a statement that can be run on the server.

  • The landing table lab.gNN_db_ingestion exists, with one row per tag of the partner in the count by data_tag.
  • The grain holds: the number of rows of the fact equals the number of distinct keys of the grain.
  • The cube has every level, 2ⁿ for n dimensions, identified by grouping_id.
  • The closed month is frozen in one MergeTree table, and the same report exists as a view over the last period.
  • Every object of the model carries the prefix of the group, and no object of another group was touched.
  • The file docs/answers.md answers five questions of the partner, each with its query and its OLAP operation.

10. Risks and contingencies

Agent slower than the slot of the step

Each step has the reference SQL of the professor in the lab, which can be pasted and run so that the demonstration keeps its time; the agent output is then compared with the reference afterwards.

Agent writes SQL that does not run in the browser

The lab runs DuckDB 1.4, and SQL written for the 2.0 of the command line, or using read_json, COPY or files, fails. A failed statement stops the script while the earlier ones remain kept in the localStorage, so the script is corrected and run again from the failed statement.

Silent errors in the model

Errors such as reading extendedData as an object, grouping by set_code or leaving the date out of the cube raise no error. The review of each step compares the result with the numbers of the rehearsal written in the prompt, which reveals them.

Group without its enriched data

The group writes the specification of prompt 06, which needs no data, and loads the landing table as soon as the enriched data of Lesson 5 is available, so that the grain and the questions are reviewed during the session.

Load into ClickHouse blocked

The Play console does not upload files, so the load is done from the computer of the group with clickhouse-connect or with curl over HTTP, as shown in the material.

Conflicts on the shared server

Every object carries the prefix of the group, and the prompt forbids dropping objects of another group; the professor checks the list of objects of the database lab with system.tables when a conflict is reported.

11. Closing

The session closes with the delivery checks of the second hour and with the self-study of the week on OLAP, its storage models and the relation between warehouse and mart. The checklist of the material summarises what the class should retain: the grain is declared before any SQL, the gold layer and the mart are views over the landing table, a report of a closed period is materialized once while a report of the current state is a view, the cube keeps the time dimension in every grouping when a measure is semi-additive, and each question of the partner names its OLAP operation and its query.

The groups whose grain test or cube did not pass during the session complete them before Lesson 7, in which the gold layer of each group becomes package data of the DataApp.
