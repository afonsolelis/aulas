2. Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to justify the content and the treatment of each layer of a medallion architecture, choose architectural tactics by stating their gains and costs, and represent a data pipeline as a UML component diagram in the graded Architecture Activity.

Observable outcomes

  • Explain the passage from the information viewpoint, which states what the data must say, to the computational viewpoint, which states which functions transform it, and show that every invariant must be enforced by some component.
  • State the purpose, the typical content and the guarantee to the next layer of the Bronze, Silver and Gold layers.
  • Place each data quality check in the layer whose contract it tests and state the action taken when it fails.
  • Represent a pipeline as a UML component diagram with named components and provided and required interfaces, keeping servers and cloud services out of it.
  • Choose scalability tactics such as partitioning, incremental processing, idempotent reruns and decoupling through layers, stating the cost that each one brings.
  • Write a non-functional requirement as a quality attribute scenario with a response measure, using the five Vs as pressures on the platform.

3. Session products

Sketch of the project pipeline

A first sketch of the sources, layers and consumers of the group project, by hand or in PlantUML, brought to class to shorten the start of the graded activity.

Architecture proposal (graded)

The data architecture proposal of the graded Architecture Activity, with weight 4, written in class and submitted on Adalove by 16:00 on 22/10.

Diagrams as code

Component diagrams of the project written from the PlantUML listings of the material, with the components and interfaces renamed to those of the project.

Continuity

The layers and contracts designed in this session are implemented in the following lessons: Lesson 4 builds the ETL from object storage into the Silver layer of a warehouse, and Lessons 5 and 6 develop the Data Warehouse, dimensional modelling and data cubes over Silver and Gold. The engineering and technology views, including the deployment diagram, are developed in Sprints 4 and 5.

6. Facilitation script

  1. Run the daily with the timer of slide 2.
  2. Present the objective and structure on slide 3, recalling that the concepts were studied before class and that the exposition only consolidates them, and check on slide 4 who brought the sketch of the project pipeline.
  3. Use slide 5 to show that Lessons 1 and 2 described what the data must say and that this lesson describes the functions that transform it, stressing the correspondence by which each invariant becomes a check enforced by some component.
  4. Introduce on slide 6 the running example of the pharmacy chain: POS terminals that resend whole days, a nightly ERP extract with counts corrected up to three days later, supplier CSVs that change layout, and the constraints of the team.
  5. Present on slide 7 the three layers through their purpose, content and guarantee to the next layer, insisting that the layers do not prescribe tools.
  6. Show on slide 8 that each layer tests what its contract promises, and ask the class what happens when a completeness check is left to Gold.
  7. Read the component diagram of slide 9, explaining components, provided and required interfaces, dependencies and artefacts, and point out that servers belong to the deployment diagram and not to this one.
  8. Present the four tactics of slide 10 (partitioning, incremental processing, idempotent reruns and decoupling through layers), each with the cost the architecture must state.
  9. Open the graded activity on slide 11, recommending that the diagram and the text follow the same order and that each component explanation close with the cost of the chosen tactic.
  10. Circulate during the seventy minutes, checking that components are named, that the component diagram contains no servers and that each layer has a contract and a quality check.
  11. Five minutes before 16:00, ask each student to confirm the submission on Adalove and recall the self-studies and the Sprint 1 deliverables on slide 12.

7. Methodology

The session adopts a flipped format because the graded activity occupies most of the time in class. The reading material is organised as a reference wiki in five parts, from the RM-ODP and UML modelling to the medallion architecture, Big Data and the tactics catalogue, and it is studied with the three self-studies before the session. The thirty-minute exposition therefore consolidates only what the activity requires, using a single running example so that the layers, the checks, the component diagram and the tactics are seen acting on the same pipeline.

The activity asks for a blueprint, in the sense in which Fowler distinguishes UML as blueprint from UML as sketch: every element of the diagram must be named, explained in the text and consistent with the other views. Writing the diagrams in PlantUML allows students to start from the working listings of the material and to version the architecture with the code, provided that the components and interfaces are renamed to those of the project. The professor remains available in the room, which allows doubts about notation and about the contracts between layers to be resolved while the proposal is being written.

8. Assessment of learning

Graded Architecture Activity

The main assessment is the data architecture proposal of the graded Architecture Activity, with weight 4, whose statement and coverage checklist are on Adalove and are not reproduced in this plan. The material indicates how the content of the session supports the proposal: the medallion layers provide the structure of the processing, the quality checks provide the treatment of data quality, the five Vs provide the non-functional requirements against which the proposal is justified, and the tactics provide the arguments about scalability and efficiency.

  • Each layer is described by the guarantee it gives to the next one, and not only as a storage location.
  • The component diagram names every component and interface and keeps servers and cloud services out.
  • Each layer has at least one quality check with an action on failure.
  • Each tactic is chosen with its cost stated.
  • The text explains the components in the same order as the diagram.

10. Risks and contingencies

Students who did not study before class

The exposition does not repeat the material; the student starts the activity from sections 14, 15 and 19 of the material and from the listings of section 8, which cover what the proposal requires.

Exposition running over thirty minutes

Slides 8 and 10 are reduced to their headings with reference to sections 15 and 19 of the material, so that the activity keeps its seventy minutes.

Component diagram mixing servers and functions

The professor points to the second recurring mistake of section 22 and asks the student to move the servers to a deployment view and to keep only components and interfaces.

Problems rendering PlantUML

The student uses the official server in the browser or draws the diagram by hand and attaches the image, keeping the same components and interfaces in the text.
