1. How to read this material

This material accompanies Lesson 3, which opens with a team-based learning session on a medallion pipeline and continues with an exposition that organises the decisions discussed in class. It is meant to be read after the session, to consolidate what the discussion raised. Before the session, read the three self-studies.

The text follows the path of the data through the pipeline. It starts by placing the lesson in the computational viewpoint of the RM-ODP and by describing how the team-based learning session works. It then presents the medallion architecture layer by layer, the data quality checks that belong to each layer, the UML component diagram as the notation that documents the pipeline, and the tactics that allow the pipeline to grow. The last sections prepare the graded Architecture Activity, whose statement is on Adalove.

The examples continue the fictitious pharmacy chain used in Lessons 1 and 2. The chain must deliver a weekly stock-out rate of continuous-use medicines to its purchasing team, from three sources with very different behaviour: point-of-sale terminals that resend files, an ERP with a nightly extract and suppliers whose CSV files change without notice.

2. The computational viewpoint

Lessons 1 and 2 worked in the information viewpoint of the RM-ODP. They stated which information the partner needs, defined indicators and their grain, and checked those definitions against the data in an exploratory analysis. That work says what the platform must deliver, but it does not say which functions produce it. The computational viewpoint addresses that question by decomposing the system into objects that perform functions and interact through interfaces.

In a data platform, the computational objects are the components of the pipeline: connectors that extract data from the sources, jobs that conform and deduplicate it, models that organise it for analysis and services that deliver it to consumers. Each component offers an interface to the next one, and the interface is a contract. When the conformance job promises one stock position per product, store and day, the dimensional model can rely on that grain without checking it again.

The correspondence between the two viewpoints is direct. Every invariant written in the information viewpoint, such as stock on hand never being negative, must be enforced by some component, and the architecture must show which one. An invariant that no component enforces exists only on paper, and the indicator that depends on it will be wrong the first time the source violates it.

3. The team-based learning session

The session opens with a team-based learning activity built on a single case and five questions. Each question presents a decision of the pipeline and four tactics, and each tactic declares a gain and a cost. None of them is correct, because each one answers one requirement of the case and leaves another uncovered. The purpose of the activity is to make the class reason about trade-offs before the exposition names the concepts involved.

Every question follows the same cycle. In the first decision, which lasts three minutes, each student chooses a tactic on their phone and writes the assumption that must hold for it to work. In the discussion, which lasts four minutes, the anonymous justifications appear for everyone, and the professor asks first for the cost of the least chosen tactics. In the second decision, which lasts two minutes, the first distribution is visible and each student keeps or changes their choice. The synthesis then shows both distributions and the shifts between them. In one of the questions, new information about the case is released during the discussion and may change the criterion of the decision.

The quality of a justification depends on the assumption it declares. "It is the best practice" cannot be confirmed or refuted by the case, whereas "the purchasing team accepts a three-day delay in the indicator" can be checked with the partner. Writing verifiable assumptions is the same skill that the Architecture Activity asks for when it requires the role of each component to be explained.

Before the session

Read the three self-studies. The case study of the e-commerce company introduces the layers, the component diagram guide introduces the notation, and the article on data quality shows the problems specific to each layer. The TBL assumes this vocabulary.

4. The medallion architecture

The medallion architecture is a pattern for organising a lakehouse into layers of increasing refinement, usually called Bronze, Silver and Gold. The name was popularised by Databricks, but the idea is older than the term. It separates the preservation of what the sources sent from the conformance of the data into reliable entities, and both from the modelling of the data for a specific use. Each separation exists because the three activities change at different rates and for different reasons.

A layer is defined by the guarantee it gives to the next one. Bronze guarantees that what arrived is kept and can be traced to its delivery. Silver guarantees conformed entities with a declared grain and enforced invariants. Gold guarantees data modelled according to the business definitions of the indicators. When the guarantees are stated, a team can replace the technology of one layer without touching the others, and a failure can be located in the layer whose guarantee was broken.

SourcesPOS files · ERP extract · supplier CSVs
Bronzeas received, partitioned by source and date
Silvertyped, deduplicated, one row per declared grain
Goldfacts, dimensions and the weekly stock-out rate
ConsumptionMonday dashboard of the purchasing team

The pattern does not prescribe tools. Bronze can be a bucket of files in object storage, Silver a set of Parquet tables and Gold a schema in PostgreSQL, which is close to what the module builds over the next sprints. What the pattern prescribes is the order of the transformations and the existence of a contract at each boundary.

5. Bronze: keeping what arrived

The Bronze layer receives the data from the sources. Its main function is to allow any later step to be repeated, which requires keeping the data in a form close to what was delivered and recording when and from where it came. A Bronze layer that stores the original files, partitioned by source and ingestion date, lets the team rebuild Silver after discovering an error in a transformation, without asking the sources to send the data again.

This function conflicts with other concerns, and the first question of the TBL explores the conflict. Keeping every delivery as received means keeping every POS resend and every sensitive field in clear text. Typing and deduplicating on arrival reduces the volume, but a wrong rule applied at ingestion destroys information that cannot be recovered. Keeping only the latest snapshot is simple, but it erases the history of corrections that explains why an indicator changed. None of these options is free, and the architecture must state which cost it accepts.

Whatever the choice, Bronze needs metadata. Each record or file should carry at least the source, the ingestion timestamp and an identifier of the delivery, so that a row in Gold can be traced back to the file that produced it.

Text · Bronze path convention
s3://pharmacy-lake/bronze/source=pos/entity=sale_line/ingestion_date=2026-10-22/batch_1415.json
s3://pharmacy-lake/bronze/source=erp/entity=stock_position/ingestion_date=2026-10-22/extract.parquet
s3://pharmacy-lake/bronze/source=supplier/entity=delivery/ingestion_date=2026-10-22/acme_0930.csv

6. Silver: conforming the entities

The Silver layer turns the deliveries into entities that the whole organisation can trust. It applies types, removes duplicates, resolves keys between sources and enforces the invariants declared in the information viewpoint. At the end of Silver, a sale line exists once, a stock position exists once per product, store and day, and the fields that the LGPD protects have been removed or pseudonymised.

The hardest decisions of Silver concern data that changes after it arrives. In the case, POS terminals resend whole days after a connection loss, and stock counts are corrected up to three days later by inventory audits. Merging by key and keeping the latest version gives simple tables, but it silently changes past values of the indicator. Keeping every version with a validity interval preserves the history, at the price of more complex queries. Holding data in quarantine until a settling window has passed gives stable published days, but delays the indicator. The second question of the TBL places the class in front of this choice.

Silver is also the natural place for most data quality checks, because it is the first layer in which the grain and the invariants are known. Rows that break a rule can be rejected, corrected or sent to a quarantine area, and the volume of each outcome is itself a quality indicator that should be monitored.

PySpark · deduplicate resends keeping the latest delivery
from pyspark.sql import Window, functions as F

latest = Window.partitionBy("sale_id", "product_id").orderBy(F.col("ingested_at").desc(), F.col("batch_id").desc())

silver_sale_line = (
    bronze_sale_line
    .withColumn("rn", F.row_number().over(latest))
    .filter("rn = 1")
    .drop("rn", "cpf", "prescription_number")
)

The snippet implements one of the tactics of the second question. Keeping only the latest delivery per sale line is correct if a resend is always the most recent truth, and the case shows when that assumption holds. If the partner needs to reproduce past values of the indicator, the same window can be used to close and open validity intervals instead of discarding older rows.

7. Gold: modelling for consumption

The Gold layer organises the conformed data for a specific use. In the case, that use is the weekly stock-out rate read by the purchasing team every Monday, and later the questions that analysts will ask about it. The modelling choice determines how easily those later questions can be answered.

A star schema, with a periodic snapshot fact of stock positions and conformed dimensions of store, product and date, lets new questions reuse the same dimensions and computes the indicator from a single fact with a declared grain. A wide table built for the dashboard is faster to deliver, but each new question requires a new table, and the definitions of store or product drift apart. Keeping the daily detail and computing the weekly rate in the BI tool preserves traceability, but moves the formula into the reports, where it can diverge. Views over Silver avoid duplicating data, but recompute the indicator at every read. The fourth question of the TBL discusses these options, and Lessons 5 and 6 of the module develop dimensional modelling and data cubes in detail.

Whatever the model, Gold must match the definitions of the Data Model Canvas. The formula, unit and periodicity of the KPI written in block 4 are the specification that the Gold layer implements, and the reconciliation between them is the first check of this layer.

8. Data quality across the layers

Each layer tests what its contract promises. Placing a check in the wrong layer has a cost. A check in Bronze that depends on business rules lacks the context it needs, and a completeness check left to Gold detects a missing delivery only after the indicator has already been published with a gap. The table below organises the checks of the case by layer.

LayerQuestion the check answersExamples in the caseAction on failure
BronzeDid everything arrive?Files per store and period, expected schema of supplier CSVs, arrival time, volume compared with the same weekdayAlert the data custodian and hold the dependent loads
SilverDoes the data obey the model?Uniqueness of the grain, on_hand ≥ 0, every sale line referring to an existing product, share of rows in quarantineQuarantine the rows and record the volume
GoldDoes the indicator make sense?Totals reconciled with Silver, stock-out rate between 0 and 1, data freshness before the Monday dashboardMark the dashboard as stale and notify its owner
SQL · grain check before publishing Silver
SELECT store_id, product_id, date, COUNT(*) AS rows_per_key
FROM silver.stock_position
GROUP BY store_id, product_id, date
HAVING COUNT(*) > 1;  -- must return no rows

9. UML component diagrams

The component diagram of the UML shows the modular parts of a system and the interfaces through which they connect. A component is a replaceable unit with a defined responsibility. It provides interfaces, which are the services it offers, and requires interfaces, which are the services it needs. In the notation, a provided interface is drawn as a ball and a required interface as a socket, and the connection between a ball and a socket shows that one component satisfies what the other needs.

For a data pipeline, the diagram makes the contracts between layers visible. The connectors provide raw files, the Bronze store requires them and provides raw partitions, the conformance jobs require those partitions and provide conformed entities, and so on until the dashboard. Reading the diagram from left to right reproduces the path of the data, and each interface marks a point where a quality check can be placed. Dependencies, drawn as dashed arrows, show relationships that are not data flows, such as a job that depends on a configuration service.

The diagram belongs to the computational viewpoint. It shows functions and interfaces, not machines. Where each component runs, on which node and with which technology, is the concern of the engineering and technology viewpoints, which the module develops in Sprints 4 and 5 with deployment concerns.

PlantUML · component diagram of the case
@startuml
component "Source connectors" as C
component "Bronze store" as B
component "Conformance jobs" as S
component "Dimensional model" as G
component "Purchasing dashboard" as D
interface "raw files" as I1
interface "raw partitions" as I2
interface "conformed entities" as I3
interface "KPI views" as I4
C - I1
I1 )- B
B - I2
I2 )- S
S - I3
I3 )- G
G - I4
I4 )- D
@enduml

In PlantUML, the line C - I1 declares that the component provides the interface, and I1 )- B declares that the next component requires it. The same structure can be drawn in any UML tool, and what matters for the Architecture Activity is that each component and each interface is named and explained.

10. Scalability tactics

A pipeline scales when the work of each run grows with the new data, independently of the size of the history. In the case, thirty new stores will increase the POS volume by about a third, and the fifth question of the TBL asks which tactic carries that growth. The tactics below are the usual candidates, and each one has a cost that the architecture must state.

Partitioning

Data is split by date and store, so that each run reads and writes only the partitions that changed. Frequent small loads create many small files, and a compaction job becomes necessary.

Incremental processing

Each component records a watermark and reads only data newer than it. Late data, such as audited stock counts, arrives behind the watermark and needs a lookback window.

Idempotent reruns

Rerunning a day produces the same result as the first run, which makes reprocessing after a failure safe. The usual technique is to overwrite whole partitions instead of appending rows.

Decoupling through layers

Each layer can be recomputed from the previous one. A fix in a Silver rule is applied by recomputing Silver from Bronze, without new extractions from the sources.

PySpark · idempotent write of one day
spark.conf.set("spark.sql.sources.partitionOverwriteMode", "dynamic")

(silver_stock_position
    .filter(F.col("date") == run_date)
    .write.mode("overwrite")
    .partitionBy("date")
    .parquet("s3://pharmacy-lake/silver/stock_position/"))

With dynamic partition overwrite, the job replaces only the partition of the day it processed, so running it twice leaves the table in the same state. This property is what allows a late stock count to be absorbed by reprocessing the affected days.

11. Preparing the Architecture Activity

The graded Architecture Activity of this session is individual, has weight 4 and must be submitted on Adalove by 16:00 on Thursday, October 22. Its statement and its coverage checklist are on Adalove, together with a support reading on design patterns for modern data pipelines. This section does not repeat the statement. It explains how the content of the session helps to answer it.

The decisions of the TBL provide the raw material for the proposal. Each question corresponds to a component or a boundary of the architecture, and the justification written in class already contains the assumption and the cost that the explanation should state. The medallion layers provide the structure of the processing, the quality checks of section 8 provide the treatment of data quality, and the tactics of section 10 provide the arguments about scalability and efficiency.

A proposal is easier to assess when the diagram and the text follow the same order. Draw the components from the sources to the dashboard, then explain the role of each one in the same sequence, and close each explanation with the cost of the tactic chosen for it. A diagram whose boxes are not explained in the text, or a text that mentions components absent from the diagram, forces the reader to reconstruct the architecture.

12. Recurring mistakes

The most common mistake is to treat the layers as storage locations, without stating the contract of each one. A diagram that shows three boxes named Bronze, Silver and Gold without saying what each guarantees describes folders, and it does not explain why the data in Gold can be trusted.

The second mistake is to choose a tactic without stating its cost. Every tactic of the TBL had one, and the architecture is judged by whether the cost it accepted fits the case. The third is to place all the quality checks at the end of the pipeline, where a missing delivery is found only after the indicator has been published. The fourth, finally, is to mix viewpoints in the component diagram by drawing servers, cloud services and functions as if they were the same kind of element. The component diagram shows functions and interfaces, and the deployment of those functions belongs to another diagram.
