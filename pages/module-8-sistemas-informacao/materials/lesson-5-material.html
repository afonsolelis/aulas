2. The Olist case and the hybrid architecture

Olist connects small Brazilian shops to large online marketplaces, and it published an anonymised sample of its operation as an open dataset. The sample contains about one hundred thousand orders placed between 2016-09-04 and 2018-10-17, with their items, customers, sellers, products, payments and reviews, and it is distributed under the MIT licence in the repository github.com/olist/work-at-olist-data. The same data is published on Kaggle as the "Brazilian E-Commerce Public Dataset by Olist". The lab uses eight of its CSV files, packed in one archive of 28 MB, which also carries the licence and a file SOURCE.txt with the origin of the data. The file of geolocation, with 61 MB, was left out, because no step of the lesson needs it.

The data is real, and for this reason its defects were not planted by a generator but left by the systems that produced it. Some zip prefixes arrive with four digits, the leading zero already lost at the source; two headers of the products file are misspelt; one file starts with a byte order mark; and the timestamps were written in the local time of Brazil, which had daylight saving time until 2019, without any offset. Section 5 describes how the enrichment treats each defect, and section 14 lists the counts that load_bronze.py prints for them.

The architecture was decided by the professor before the session and is recorded by the agent in an ADR during Step 01. The script load_bronze.py uploads the eight CSV files, unchanged, to the Bronze layer of a local RustFS under bronze/source=olist/entity=<entity>/ingestion_date=2026-11-05/. Each load of the Silver layer reads the Bronze, enriches it with DuckDB and writes first a standardised lake in RustFS, one Parquet file per tag and per day under silver/enriched/data_tag=<tag>/ingestion_date=<D>/data.parquet, together with a manifest of the run under silver/_manifests/run_<id>.json that records the SHA-256 of every file. Only then does the load fill ClickHouse, and it does so by reading the files it has just written to the lake, never the Bronze. This order is what makes the warehouse auditable, since ClickHouse never holds a row that the lake does not hold and can be rebuilt from the lake at any moment. Over the enriched table of ClickHouse, Step 04 builds a star schema in a database gold, with dimensions as views and a fact and an aggregate as refreshable materialized views.

The two copies serve two kinds of question. ClickHouse keeps a short window, up to one year, removed automatically by a TTL, and answers the tactical questions of the operation, such as the number of delivered orders of the month by region. The lake keeps every version for years and is read with DuckDB for the strategic questions, for data science and for heavy manipulations such as training runs that last hours or days, which should not compete with the tactical queries for the resources of the warehouse. Section 8 explains why the length of the ClickHouse window belongs to the business and why, in this case, the clock that the window follows matters as much as its length.

The environment runs in two containers declared in docker-compose.yml. The object storage is RustFS, image rustfs/rustfs:1.0.4, which publishes the S3 API on port 9000 and the web console on port 9001, under the path /rustfs/console/. It replaces the MinIO of Lesson 4 because the official MinIO image is no longer published, and since it speaks the same S3 API, DuckDB and boto3 work against it unchanged. RustFS accepts only path-style addresses, which is why DuckDB is configured with URL_STYLE 'path'. ClickHouse uses the image clickhouse/clickhouse-server:26.8.15.10 and publishes only its HTTP interface, on port 8123, which is the one used by clickhouse-connect. The native port of ClickHouse is also 9000, and since RustFS already occupies that port on the host, the native port is not published. Inside the network of the compose file, ClickHouse reaches RustFS by the name of its service, at http://rustfs:9000, which is the value of S3_ENDPOINT_FROM_CLICKHOUSE in .env.

3. The Silver layer as a Data Warehouse

The term Data Warehouse was defined by William Inmon as a subject-oriented, integrated, time-variant and non-volatile collection of data in support of the decisions of management. Each of the four properties describes a guarantee that operational systems do not give, and together they explain why the Silver layer of the medallion architecture plays the role of the warehouse in this module.

Subject-oriented

The data is organised around the entities of the business, such as customer, seller, product, order and order item, whatever application produced them. In the enriched table, the entity is the middle segment of each tag, as in olist.order.v1.

Integrated

Codes, formats and units are made consistent. After the enrichment, every zip prefix of customers and sellers has five characters, every timestamp carries its offset and every category has a name in English, with the Portuguese name as fallback.

Time-variant

Every record is associated with a moment, and the history is kept. Here the moment is data_ingestao, and a second delivery of the same files, loaded with load_bronze.py --ingestion-date 2026-11-06, would be kept as another partition of the lake.

Non-volatile

Data is loaded and read, but not edited in place. A correction arrives as a new record or as a reload derived from the Bronze, which is why a rerun of the load must produce the same result.

The Silver layer combines and cleans the Bronze into a corporate view of the core entities of the business. Corporate view means that the definition of an order, of an order item or of a delivery is the same for every team that reads it, so that the commercial team and the finance team do not compute two different revenues from two different extractions. This shared definition is what makes self-service analytics possible, in which analysts query the Silver layer directly, without asking the engineering team to reconcile the sources for each question. Self-service depends on three conditions that the lab makes explicit: a written contract with the meaning of every field, stable names, and a way of filtering the data by kind and version, which is the role of data_tag.

The Silver layer is also the foundation of the Gold layer. This division corresponds to the two classic schools of the field. Inmon proposed an integrated enterprise warehouse, normalised, from which departmental data marts are derived. Ralph Kimball proposed building the warehouse as a set of dimensional marts that share conformed dimensions. In the medallion architecture, the Silver layer takes the integrating role of the Inmon warehouse and the Gold layer takes the dimensional role of the Kimball marts, so that the two approaches become successive layers. Step 04 of this lesson builds the first piece of the Gold layer, a star schema for one question, and Lesson 6 continues with cubes and data marts over the same Silver layer, so that every mart inherits the same cleaning and the same keys.

4. The enriched table

The description of the session asks for a table with three columns, and the lab keeps their names exactly as written, in Portuguese, because the contract of the Silver layer and the queries of every later lesson depend on them. The table stores one record per row, whatever the entity, and the entity and its version are declared by the tag.

The date and time of ingestion

The value of data_ingestao is derived from the Bronze, without reference to the clock of the load. Every object of the delivery lives under the partition ingestion_date=2026-11-05, so every record of the run receives 2026-11-05 00:00 in America/Sao_Paulo, written by ClickHouse as 2026-11-05 00:00:00.000 with the offset -03:00. The reason for this rule is reproducibility. If the load wrote the current time, two runs over the same Bronze would produce different values in every row, the files of the lake would differ from one run to the next and a reconciliation between runs would find differences that do not correspond to any change in the data. Deriving the moment from the Bronze makes reruns deterministic.

The moment of ingestion must not be confused with the moment of the business event. An order bought on 2018-03-14 entered the platform on 2026-11-05, and the date of the purchase lives inside data_linha, in the field purchased_at. The two clocks answer different questions, since data_ingestao says when the platform learned of a record and purchased_at says when the sale happened, and section 8 shows that the choice between them decides what the retention of ClickHouse keeps.

The record in JSON

The column data_linha holds the record as a JSON object. JSON accommodates structured data, such as an order with fixed fields, and semi-structured data, such as a record whose fields vary from one version of a source to another, in the same column. This flexibility would be a problem in a warehouse that stored the JSON as text, because every query would have to parse the text of every row. ClickHouse avoids it with the type JSON, which stores each path of the object as a separate subcolumn with its own type, so that a query on data_linha.customer_region reads only that subcolumn, as it would read an ordinary column. The format therefore remains compatible with the columnar storage of the warehouse, and a new field in a new version of the source becomes a new path without any change to the table.

In the rehearsal, ClickHouse kept the types written by the enrichment: purchased_at and zip_prefix stayed String, delivery_days and order_item_id became Int64, price and freight_value Float64, and delivered_late Bool. The path purchased_at stayed as text because every insert disabled the inference of dates, as section 17 explains, which preserves the offset written by the enrichment. The path zip_prefix stayed as text because the enrichment wrote it as a string of five characters, and a numeric type would remove the leading zero that the enrichment has just restored.

The flexibility of JSON has a cost that the design must pay elsewhere. The table does not enforce which fields each record has, so the schema of each kind of record moves from the DDL to the contract of the Silver layer, specs/silver_contract.md, where each tag lists its fields, its key of deduplication and its rules. The enrichment code and its tests are what enforce that contract.

The tags

The column data_tag classifies each record by source, entity and version. It serves three purposes. It versions the records, because a change in the format of a source produces a new tag, such as olist.order.v2, while the records of the previous version remain valid and the DDL of ClickHouse does not change. It categorises them, because the entity is part of the tag. And it filters them, because the table is ordered by (data_tag, data_ingestao), so that a query restricted to one tag reads a contiguous range of the table. The type LowCardinality(String) stores the few distinct values of the tag in a dictionary, which reduces both the size and the cost of comparing them. Five tags enter the Silver layer, plus quarantine. Payments and reviews stay in the Bronze, as material for the groups to treat on their own.

Part IIEnrichment and audit

5. Enrichment techniques

Enrichment is the set of transformations that turns a record of the Bronze into a record that the business can use without consulting other sources. The description of the session names three techniques, which are normalising formats and units, aggregating multiple sources and deriving new fields according to the requirements of the partner. The lab applies the three in the SQL of src/enrich.py, executed by DuckDB over the Bronze, together with the deduplication and the quarantine introduced in Lesson 4.

Normalising formats and units

Normalising means writing the same kind of value in the same way, whatever the state in which the source delivered it. The Olist files require six normalisations, and each one prevents a specific error downstream.

  • Every CSV column is read as text and converted explicitly, because automatic type detection reads a zip prefix such as 02728 as the integer 2728. In the source, 23,995 customer and 1,027 seller zip prefixes already have four digits, since the leading zero was lost before the export, and the enrichment pads them to five characters, so that the customer of the example has zip_prefix "02728".
  • The two misspelt headers of the products file, product_name_lenght and product_description_lenght, become name_length and description_length, so that the contract does not perpetuate the error of the source.
  • The translation file of the categories starts with a UTF-8 byte order mark, so its first header is read as ﻿product_category_name, with an invisible character in front, and a join on product_category_name fails to find the column. The reader must strip the mark, for instance with the encoding utf-8-sig in Python.
  • The categories receive their English name. The 610 products without a category receive 'unknown', and the 13 products whose category has no English translation, such as pc_gamer and portateis_cozinha_e_preparadores_de_alimentos, keep the Portuguese name.
  • The timestamps arrive without an offset, in the local time of Brazil, and they are written in ISO 8601 with the offset that America/Sao_Paulo had on each date. Brazil had daylight saving time until 2019, so the offset is -03:00 for most of the year and -02:00 in the summer, as in the shipping_limit_at "2017-12-08T13:30:29-02:00" of one item. A fixed offset of -03:00 would shift every summer timestamp by one hour, and an order placed in the last hour of a month would move to the next month in the answer of Step 04.
  • The CSV files mix quoted and unquoted fields, so the reader must follow the quoting rules of the format instead of splitting the lines on commas.

Aggregating multiple sources

Aggregating, in the sense of the description, means bringing into one record the attributes that live in different sources. An order of the orders file carries the identifier of the customer, but not the state where the customer lives, which comes from the customers file. The enrichment joins the customers and writes customer_state and customer_region inside the data_linha of each order. The record becomes self-contained, so that an analyst can group the orders by region without knowing where the states are defined, and the record keeps the attributes that were valid at the moment of its ingestion.

Deriving new fields

Deriving means computing a field that no source sends but that a requirement of the partner needs. The region is derived from the state, following the five regions of the IBGE, because the commercial area of Olist compares regions. The order receives delivery_days, the number of days between the purchase and the delivery to the customer, and delivered_late, which compares the delivery with the estimated date; the order of the example was bought on 2018-03-14, delivered on 2018-03-22 and estimated for 2018-04-06, so it has 8 days and was not late. The order item receives item_total, the sum of price and freight, which is 85.45 for an item of price 58.9 and freight 26.55. A derived field belongs to the Silver layer when the rule is stable and shared by several consumers, and it belongs to the Gold layer when it serves one indicator only.

Each tag is deduplicated by the key of the contract, which this delivery does not need, since it has no duplicates, but which the next delivery of the same files will need, because the rule keeps the latest ingestion date and then the latest file. Their 8 items follow them with the reason 'order in quarantine', so that no item of the Silver layer refers to an order that the Silver layer does not hold. The 14 orders delivered without order_approved_at are kept, with approved_at null, because the missing approval does not prevent any field that the business uses.

6. Auditability: the lake first

A warehouse is auditable when every number it returns can be traced to the data that produced it and when that data can be shown to be the same data that was loaded. The hybrid design obtains this property through the order of the load, which always writes the lake, then loads ClickHouse from the files of the lake, and writes the manifest last.

The column data_linha is declared as String in the structure passed to s3(), since Parquet stores it as text, and the insert converts it to the type JSON of the table.

The order has three consequences. The first is that ClickHouse never holds a row that the lake does not hold, because the only input of ClickHouse is the set of lake files of the run. If the load of ClickHouse fails, the manifest is written with the status error_clickhouse and the process exits with a non-zero code, so that the scheduler knows that the tactical copy is out of date while the lake remains complete. The second is that ClickHouse is disposable, in the sense that it can be rebuilt from the lake at any moment. In the rehearsal, rebuilding it from the lake took about 1.0 s for 347,578 rows. The third is that the integrity of the lake can be checked later, because the manifest keeps the SHA-256 of every file. The reconciliation of Step 03 recomputes the hash of each file listed in the last manifest, and any difference means that a file was changed after the load.

Since data_ingestao comes from the Bronze, the content of every record is also the same in both runs. In the rehearsal, the second run arrived at the same counts for every tag.

Part IIIWarehouses and lifecycle

7. Columnar warehouses: Redshift and ClickHouse

Operational databases store each row contiguously, because a transaction usually reads or writes a whole record, such as one order. Analytical queries behave differently, since they read a few columns of millions of rows, such as the price and the region of every order item of a month. A columnar warehouse stores each column contiguously, so that such a query reads only the columns it needs. Values of the same column are similar to one another, which makes compression much more effective, and the engine can process blocks of values of one column at a time, which is called vectorised execution. Amazon Redshift and ClickHouse, the two tools named in the description of the session, are columnar warehouses, and so is DuckDB, used in the lab as the engine of the lake.

ClickHouse and the MergeTree engine

ClickHouse is an open-source columnar database for analytical workloads, which can run in a container, as in the lab, on a cluster or as a managed cloud service. Its main family of table engines is MergeTree. Each insert writes an immutable part, with the rows sorted by the key of ORDER BY, and a background process merges small parts into larger ones. The table has a sparse primary index, with one entry for each granule of rows (8,192 rows by default), so that a filter on the first columns of the sorting key skips the granules that cannot contain matching rows. In the lab, the sorting key (data_tag, data_ingestao) makes a filter by tag read only the granules of that tag, which is what keeps the dimension views of Step 04 cheap even though they are recomputed at every query.

Two other clauses of the DDL organise the data in time. PARTITION BY toYYYYMM(data_ingestao) keeps the parts of each month separate, which allows the engine to drop or skip a whole month at once. TTL toDateTime(data_ingestao) + INTERVAL 1 YEAR declares that a row expires one year after its ingestion, and ClickHouse removes expired rows during merges. After Step 02 the table occupied about 32.5 MB on disk for 347,578 rows.

Amazon Redshift

Amazon Redshift is the managed warehouse of AWS, offered as provisioned clusters or as Redshift Serverless. It is a massively parallel processing (MPP) system, in which a leader node plans each query and compute nodes execute it in parallel over slices of the data, stored in columns. Two choices of the table design decide its performance. The distribution style (KEY, ALL, EVEN or AUTO) decides on which node each row lives, and a good choice puts the rows that are joined together on the same node, avoiding traffic between nodes; a small dimension such as dim_date is a typical candidate for ALL, which copies it to every node. The sort key decides the order of the rows on disk, and Redshift keeps the minimum and maximum of each block (zone maps) to skip blocks, playing a role similar to the sorting key of MergeTree. Redshift Spectrum reads files in S3 through external tables, so that a query can combine tables of the warehouse with Parquet files of a lake, and the type SUPER stores semi-structured values queried with PartiQL, which is the counterpart of the JSON type of ClickHouse.

8. Lifecycle and the clock of the TTL

Data has a lifecycle, and the questions asked about it change with its age. Recent data is read often, by many people, with short questions about the operation, and it must answer in a fraction of a second. Old data is read rarely, by few people, with long questions about trends, and it can take seconds or minutes. The hybrid design gives each phase a place, with ClickHouse keeping the tactical window and the lake, read with DuckDB, keeping the strategic history.

The question of Step 04 was answered in both places, which makes the difference measurable. ClickHouse answered it in 0.002 s from the stored aggregate and in 0.06 s from the fact with its joins, while DuckDB answered it in 0.47 s reading the Parquet files of the lake, with the same result in the three cases. The lake is small in this case, so the strategic copy is still fast. Its place is the lake for another reason, which is that the lake keeps every delivery for years, and that a training run of data science that reads all of them for hours would degrade the tactical queries if it ran on the warehouse.

The window is a business decision

The length of the ClickHouse window, one year in the lab, is a business decision. Technically, ClickHouse could keep a month or a decade, and changing the window is a single statement, ALTER TABLE silver.enriched MODIFY TTL .... What the window should be depends on questions that only the business can answer: how far back the tactical users look in their daily work, how much the partner is willing to pay to keep data in the fast copy, and which legal obligations or limits apply, such as fiscal retention periods and the principle of minimisation of the LGPD for personal data. The owner of the decision is the business area of the partner that uses the tactical queries, together with whoever answers for data governance. For this reason the contract and the ADR of the lab mark the one-year retention as a hypothesis to validate with the partner, and the engineering team implements whatever value the business decides.

Which clock the retention follows

The Olist case adds a second decision, which is the clock that the window follows. The TTL of the lab counts one year from data_ingestao, and since every record was ingested on 2026-11-05, ClickHouse keeps every row. A TTL that counted from purchased_at would keep 0 of the 99,433 orders, because the newest order of the dataset was placed on 2018-10-17T17:30:18-03:00, eight years before the lesson. Both readings are defensible. Counting from the ingestion keeps in ClickHouse whatever arrived recently, which suits a platform that reprocesses old data, while counting from the purchase keeps the recent sales, which suits an operation that only looks at the current year. Which clock the retention follows is therefore a business decision of the same kind as the length of the window, and Step 03 records it in the ADR as a question addressed to its owner.

Decision for the group

In the artifact Data Ingestion Structure, the group should state the retention of its warehouse as a hypothesis, say which date of the partner data the retention counts from, name who in the partner organisation decides it, and record the question that will validate it, for example how many months back the users of the DataApp compare their indicators.

The reconciliation inside the window

The reconciliation compares, for each tag, the rows of the lake, the rows of the lake that fall inside the window of ClickHouse and the rows of ClickHouse. The window must be read from the TTL of the table in ClickHouse, so that a change of the business decision does not require a change in the script, and a row in ClickHouse that is not in the lake is a difference whatever the window, because it would break the guarantee of section 6. In this delivery the three counts coincide for every tag, since nothing has expired.

Part IVDimensional modelling

9. Dimensional modelling

The enriched table records what arrived, with each record self-contained, and it is well suited to audit and to reprocessing. The commercial area of Olist, however, asks questions about sales by month, by region or by category, and answering them over the enriched table would require every analyst to know which tags to join, which paths to read and how to define a sale. Dimensional modelling, systematised by Ralph Kimball and Margy Ross in The Data Warehouse Toolkit, organises the data around the measurement of a business process, so that the questions of the business become simple queries and the definition of each measure is written once.

The four steps of the design

Kimball and Ross describe the design of a dimensional model in four steps, taken in order because each one depends on the previous. The first step selects the business process, which in this case is the sale of products through the marketplace, recorded by the orders and their items. The second step declares the grain, which is the exact meaning of one row of the fact table; here the grain is one order item, because price, freight, seller and product exist only at the level of the item, and a fact at the level of the order would have to choose one seller and one product for orders that have several. The third step identifies the dimensions, which are the contexts in which the measurements are read: who bought (customer), who sold (seller), what was sold (product) and when (date). The fourth step identifies the facts, which are the numerical measurements of the process at the declared grain, here price and freight_value of each item.

Fact table

One row per measurement event at the declared grain, with foreign keys to the dimensions and the numerical measures. It is long and narrow, since it grows with the operation; in the case, 112,642 rows.

Dimension table

One row per member of a context, with descriptive attributes used to filter and group, such as city, state and region of the customer. It is short and wide, and it changes slowly.

Conformed dimensions

A dimension is conformed when it has the same keys, the same attributes and the same meaning in every fact table that uses it. If the payments and the reviews, which this lesson leaves in the Bronze, later receive their own fact tables, they should reuse dim_customer and dim_date instead of building new ones, so that a question that crosses sales and reviews by region of the customer compares the same regions on both sides. Conformed dimensions are what allows a set of marts, built one at a time, to behave as one warehouse, which is the central argument of Kimball against the integrated warehouse built in one piece.

Additive measures and distinct counts

Kimball classifies the measures by how they can be summed. An additive measure can be summed across every dimension, as is the case of price: the revenue of a month is the sum of the revenue of its days, and the revenue of a region is the sum of the revenue of its states. A semi-additive measure can be summed across some dimensions but not across time, as a stock balance, and a non-additive measure cannot be summed at all, as a ratio. The number of sales of the question belongs to a fourth kind, the distinct count, because a sale is an order and the fact has one row per item. Counting the rows of the fact would count items, 112,642 in total for 99,433 orders, and the question asks for orders, so the measure must be uniqExact(order_id). A distinct count is not additive in general, since the same order could be counted in two groups, but in this case the sum across regions and across months is safe, because each order has exactly one customer and one date of purchase, so it belongs to exactly one region and one month.

The star and its alternatives

A star schema places the fact table at the centre and connects it to one table per dimension, with the dimensions denormalised: the region is an attribute of dim_customer, repeated for every customer of the region. This redundancy is deliberate, because it keeps every question at one join per dimension and makes the model readable by analysts. Third Normal Form, which stores each fact in one place to prevent the anomalies of updates, and Data Vault, which separates identity, relationships and history into hubs, links and satellites for environments with many changing sources, remain valid alternatives for the integrated Silver layer of other projects, and a group whose partner has many sources may prefer one of them before the Gold layer. This lesson keeps the enriched table as the Silver layer and builds the star directly over it.

12. Views and materialized views in ClickHouse

The objects of the star can be computed at query time or stored, and ClickHouse offers three mechanisms with different costs. The choice depends on how much the computation costs, how often the result is read and how the source table changes, which in the lab is by truncating and reloading silver.enriched at every run.

Plain views for the dimensions

A plain view stores only its query, and ClickHouse substitutes it into every query that reads the view. The dimensions are plain views because their computation is a filter on the first column of the sorting key followed by casts, which reads only the granules of one tag, and because a view can never be out of date: after a reload of silver.enriched, the next query already sees the new customers.

Why the incremental materialized view does not fit

An incremental materialized view of ClickHouse is an insert trigger. When a block of rows is inserted into its source table, ClickHouse runs the query of the view over that block only and appends the result to the target table, which makes the view cheap for aggregations over a table that only grows. Two properties of the lab break this mechanism. The first is the join: the trigger is attached to one table, and in a query with a join only the inserts into the left table fire it, so an item that arrives before its order, or an order that changes, never updates the target. The second is the reload: the load of Step 02 truncates silver.enriched and inserts everything again, and a truncate of the source does not reach the target of an incremental view, so the target would keep the rows of the previous run and receive the same rows again, doubling every count at each run.

Refreshable materialized views for the fact and the aggregate

A refreshable materialized view recomputes its whole query on a schedule, writes the result into a new table and then swaps it for the previous one in one atomic operation, so that a query never sees a half-written result. The fact uses REFRESH EVERY 1 HOUR, which ClickHouse aligns to the clock hour, and the aggregate declares DEPENDS ON gold.fact_order_item, which makes its scheduled refresh wait for the refresh of the fact. The cost of the mechanism is that the stored result can be up to one interval old, which is acceptable for a commercial question asked once a day, and the benefit is that the answer is read from 107 stored rows instead of being recomputed from 347,578.

17. Recurring mistakes of agents

The traps below were found while the lab was rehearsed with the same prompts on 05/11/2026, and the prompts already carry the corresponding adjustments. Most of them pass a superficial test and are caught only by a count, by the type of a column or by one specific row, which is why each review box asks for a number.

Reading the files

The first group of mistakes happens before any transformation. An agent that lets DuckDB or pandas detect the types of the CSV columns reads the zip prefixes as integers, so the 23,995 customer prefixes and 1,027 seller prefixes without the leading zero stay wrong and the ones that had it lose it; for this reason prompt 01 asks to read every column as text and to keep zip_prefix as a string of five characters. An agent that opens the translation file with the plain encoding utf-8 receives the header ﻿product_category_name, with the byte order mark in front, and the join of the categories either fails or silently leaves every product untranslated. An agent that splits lines on commas breaks on the fields that the source quoted.

Time zones and offsets

The timestamps of Olist have no offset, and the tempting conversion appends -03:00 to every one of them. The conversion is wrong for the summer months of 2016 to 2018, when Brazil was at -02:00, and the error moves the orders of the last hour of a month into the next month of the answer. The correct conversion interprets each timestamp in the zone America/Sao_Paulo, which knows the history of daylight saving time, and writes the offset of that date, as in "2017-12-08T13:30:29-02:00". DuckDB returns values of type TIMESTAMPTZ to Python only when the package pytz is installed, and without it the load fails when it converts data_ingestao. In ClickHouse, the JSON type infers dates from strings by default, so every insert must use input_format_try_infer_dates = 0 and input_format_try_infer_datetimes = 0, or purchased_at becomes a timestamp without its offset.

Versions and connections

In DuckDB 2.0 the relations returned by con.sql(...) no longer have the method fetchone(), which agents trained on older code still write, and the call must be con.execute(...).fetchone(). ClickHouse 26.8 enables async_insert by default, which makes an insert return before the rows are visible, so a count right after the load can return fewer rows than were sent, and async_insert = 0 makes each insert synchronous. The s3() function runs inside the ClickHouse container, where localhost:9000 is the container itself, so the agent that copies the endpoint of Python into the SQL receives a connection error; the address from inside the container is http://rustfs:9000, the value of S3_ENDPOINT_FROM_CLICKHOUSE. RustFS and the native port of ClickHouse both use port 9000, so only the HTTP port 8123 of ClickHouse is published, and an agent that tries the native protocol on the host finds RustFS instead. DuckDB reaches RustFS only with URL_STYLE 'path' and USE_SSL false, and the secret must be created at the start of each session with CREATE OR REPLACE SECRET, never with CREATE PERSISTENT SECRET, which would store the password in the profile of the user.

Reading the TTL and parsing the paths

The reconciliation must read the window from the TTL of the table, and SHOW CREATE TABLE writes it as toIntervalYear(1), not as INTERVAL 1 YEAR, so a regular expression written for the text of the DDL finds no window. In a query over the whole of silver.enriched, parseDateTimeBestEffort(data_linha.purchased_at) fails on the rows of the other tags, where the path is empty; the query must filter the tag in a subquery before parsing, or use the variant parseDateTimeBestEffortOrNull.

The star

An agent that refreshes the fact with SYSTEM REFRESH VIEW gold.fact_order_item and then queries the aggregate reads the previous result, because DEPENDS ON orders only the scheduled refreshes; the sequence of section 12 refreshes and waits for each view in order. An agent that answers the question with count() over the fact counts items, 112,642 for 99,433 orders, and overstates every month; a sale is an order, counted with uniqExact(order_id). An agent that proposes an incremental materialized view for the fact produces a target that doubles at each run, for the reasons of section 12.

19. Glossary

Additive measure
Measure that can be summed across every dimension, such as the price of the order items.
Columnar storage
Storage in which the values of each column are kept together, so that analytical queries read only the columns they use.
Conformed dimension
Dimension with the same keys, attributes and meaning in every fact table that uses it, which allows separate marts to be compared.
data_ingestao
Column of the enriched table with the moment at which the record entered the platform, derived from the partition of the Bronze.
data_linha
Column of the enriched table with the normalised and derived fields of one record, in JSON.
data_tag
Column of the enriched table with the source, the entity and the version of the record, or quarantine.v1.
Dimension
Table of descriptive attributes of a context of the business process, used to filter and group the facts.
Distinct count
Count of different values of a key, such as uniqExact(order_id), which is not additive in general.
Fact table
Table with one row per measurement of a business process at the declared grain, with keys to the dimensions and numerical measures.
Grain
Exact meaning of one row of a fact table; in the case, one order item.
Incremental materialized view
Insert trigger of ClickHouse that runs a query over each block inserted into one source table and appends the result to a target table.
Manifest
JSON file written at the end of each load with the keys, rows, bytes and SHA-256 of every lake file, the rows per tag, the times and the status.
MergeTree
Family of table engines of ClickHouse that writes immutable sorted parts and merges them in the background.
MPP
Massively parallel processing: execution of a query in parallel across nodes that each hold a slice of the data, as in Redshift.
Reconciliation
Comparison of the counts of the lake inside the window with the counts of ClickHouse, per tag, plus the check of the SHA-256 of the lake files.
Refreshable materialized view
View of ClickHouse that recomputes its whole query on a schedule and swaps the stored result atomically.
Self-service analytics
Analysis done directly by business users over documented data, without a request to the engineering team for each question.
Star schema
Dimensional model with a fact table at the centre and one denormalised table per dimension around it.
TTL
Time to live: rule of a ClickHouse table that removes rows after a declared interval, counted from a declared column.
View
Stored query that the database substitutes into every query that reads it, computed at query time.
