1. Partner

The partner is the Brazilian Army, represented by the Southeastern Military Command (CMSE). The main business area of the project is National Defense and Public Administration, specifically the management of public data applied to the Brazilian Army Health System (SSEx).

The Brazilian Army is a permanent national institution of the Brazilian State, present throughout the national territory, and the Land Force is organised into eight Military Area Commands. The pilot involves two of them. The Southeastern Military Command, headquartered in São Paulo, is linked to the 2nd Military Region, whose jurisdiction covers the State of São Paulo. The Southern Military Command (CMS), headquartered in Porto Alegre, is responsible for Paraná, Santa Catarina and Rio Grande do Sul, and within its area the 5th Military Region has jurisdiction over Paraná and Santa Catarina.

Health follows a functional axis that crosses this territorial organisation. The General Department of Personnel (DGP) is a sectoral direction body, and the Health Directorate (D Sau), subordinate to it, directs the Brazilian Army Health System technically and managerially at the national level. Military Regions are territorial structures of management and administrative support, and the 2nd and 5th Military Regions form the regional scope of the MVP.

3. Challenge

Public data on the execution of the Brazilian Army Health System are voluminous, longitudinal and heterogeneous. They are distributed across execution files, budget classifications, institutional registries and transactional documents.

The time series spans changes in codes, in Management Units, in institutional links and in validity periods. These changes make consistent comparisons difficult, both over time and across management levels. Turning the corpus into reproducible management information still requires reconciliation, treatment of data quality and domain knowledge, none of which is yet consolidated in a common analytical pipeline.

The challenge is therefore to integrate and govern these sources without losing their semantics, their provenance or their temporality, so that they become a reliable basis for analyses from three perspectives: National/Directorate, Regional and Local.

4. Objective

The objective is to develop an MVP of a reproducible, auditable and scalable Big Data pipeline. The pipeline ingests and prepares the public corpus provided by the partner, organises it in a data lake or a data warehouse and produces validated descriptive statistical analyses.

In the MVP, the National/Directorate perspective integrates the 2nd and 5th Military Regions. The Regional perspective allows the 2nd Military Region, with 29 UGE, and the 5th Military Region, with 36 UGE, to be analysed separately. The Local perspective segments the analysis by Hospital, by Military Health Organization (OMS) or Medical Post, and by Management Unit (UG).

The solution must be parameterisable so that it can later be extended to all 12 Military Regions, and it must be able to reconcile the national longitudinal universe, in which 563 base UGs were observed across the historical series.

5. Expected benefits for the partner

The partner expects the project to bring the following benefits:

  • comparing independent solutions to the same real-world challenge, including alternatives in data architecture, modelling and analysis;
  • establishing reproducible standards for ingestion, quality, temporality and traceability;
  • reducing manual effort in future updates;
  • validating which management questions of the Brazilian Army Health System Indicator Panel (PI-SSEx) can be answered with public data;
  • identifying new descriptive perspectives on financial and documentary execution;
  • generating reusable code and documentation, preferably with open-source components and open formats, as a basis for future evolution.

6. Study materials provided

The partner attaches a versioned Data Package to the charter. It contains:

  • previously acquired raw public data;
  • a domain guide and a glossary;
  • a reference registry of the 65 UGE and the 9 OMS of the pilot;
  • a data dictionary and the rules of identity and validity;
  • manifests, provenance records, control totals and audited samples;
  • the standard EB30-N-10.006, 2nd edition, 2023 (DGP/C Ex Ordinance No. 444/2023);
  • a matrix relating the 13 PI-SSEx management reports to their actual availability in the corpus;
  • the partner's TRL 3 proof of concept, with reference components and simple reference solutions.
How to use the reference material

Standardised tables and previously developed artefacts serve as anchor points and quality benchmarks. The charter states explicitly that they are not a mandatory final architecture or solution, so each group remains responsible for its own design decisions.

7. Macro scope

The project builds an end-to-end Big Data pipeline within the infrastructure provided for by the module. Teams are free to decide on architecture, modelling and technologies compatible with that environment. The partner provides the corpus, domain knowledge, controls, automations and simple solutions already developed, in order to reduce operational work and raise the starting point, without prescribing the final solution. The scope is divided into four parts.

Data ingestion

Batch ingestion of the previously acquired and versioned public corpus, with a volume greater than 1 GB that combines structured, documentary and textual data. The solution records origin, version, coverage and failures, and demonstrates that incremental updates can be incorporated without repeating manual collection.

Data preparation

ETL or ELT with quality controls that preserve identifiers, source semantics, institutional identity and validity, physical codes and base UGs, as well as documentary relationships whose cardinality may not be one-to-one. The partner provides factual rules, references and control totals. It does not provide a final analytical schema, cube, warehouse or closed set of indicators.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive and comparative analyses consistent with the module. As each team justifies, they may explore materiality, composition, distribution, concentration, temporal variation, relationships among dimensions and differences among analytical cuts. Analyses distinguish the National/Directorate, Regional and Local perspectives and state explicitly their coverage, source, definition and limits of interpretation.

Infographic creation

Validated statistical results are communicated in two complementary sets. Set A, the known institutional view, reproduces only the PI-SSEx questions, filters and comparisons that the public corpus supports, with emphasis on expenditure comparisons, and records explicitly the gaps in reports that depend on internal data. Set B, management innovation, proposes new visualisations, measures or descriptive relationships that add information for managers, explaining the information gain and the limitations of each proposal.

8. MVP and the three perspectives

The MVP is developed iteratively throughout the sprints. The partner reduces preparatory friction by delivering files already acquired, domain documentation, reference registries, audited samples, controls, automations and simple reference solutions. The decisions of higher technical and intellectual value remain with the teams: integration, ingestion strategy, transformation, modelling, organisation of the data lake or warehouse, quality, analysis, validation, scalability and communication. In the words of the charter, the aim is to raise the starting floor without lowering the ceiling of possible performance.

At the start of the project the partner provides a proof of concept at TRL 3. The module is expected to produce integrated and functionally validated prototypes consistent with progression to TRL 4. Solutions that show greater maturity may be invited by the partner to an additional validation in a relevant environment, including experiments with managers and expansion tests for further Military Regions.

The MVP demonstrates three perspectives of aggregation and consumption of information, together with an explicit path to scale. The three perspectives do not require three independent applications or front ends.

PerspectiveWhat it covers in the MVP
National/DirectorateJoint analysis of the 2nd and 5th Military Regions. Together they form the entry baseline and provide a supra-regional perspective from the outset, serving as a pilot for the future integrated view of the 12 Military Regions.
RegionalIndependent analyses of the 2nd Military Region, with 29 UGE, and of the 5th Military Region, with 36 UGE, using the same parameterisable logic.
LocalAnalytical segmentation by Hospital, by OMS or Medical Post, or by UG, according to institutional relationships and data availability.

Expansion must not depend on hard-coded territorial identifiers. In any extended validation of the most mature solutions, the 2nd Military Region is the preferred environment for experiments with managers, because of its institutional and geographic proximity to the execution of the project. The 11th Military Region may be used as an additional expansion set to test generalisation and scalability through configuration and reference data, without rewriting the core logic.

9. Required infrastructure

The infrastructure is aligned with Inteli within the scenarios the module allows, which are AWS or on-premises. The partner provides the Data Package, domain documentation and validation materials, without access to corporate production systems. Where there is freedom of choice, open-source components, open formats and portable artefacts are preferred, so as to avoid unnecessary dependence on a specific vendor.

10. Restrictions: what the project does not include

  • Production integrations with SIAFI, AGHUse, SIRSAU or other corporate systems of the Brazilian Army are not required. The project works with the snapshot provided by the partner and, when proposed by the team and authorised within the scope, with reproducible public sources.
  • Machine learning, neural networks, forecasting and other advanced analyses are not part of the mandatory scope. Differences among teams should come from the quality of integration, modelling, quality treatment, choice of questions, descriptive analysis, validation and communication.
  • Access to infrastructure and materials follows the rules defined by Inteli and the partner. Credentials, keys and non-public documents must not be included in open repositories or in deliverables.
  • The partner does not require a front end, an operational application or a production dashboard. If Inteli uses a DataApp or an analytical interface as a pedagogical extension of the module, it consumes the same data layer and remains subordinate to the pipeline and analyses defined in the charter.
  • Complementary analytical reports are not a mandatory partner deliverable. Final communication follows the curricular format defined by Inteli, and technical documentation, tests and reproduction instructions are part of the data-engineering solution.
  • The corpus consists of public data. No identifiable clinical data, sensitive personal data or classified data are used.
Limit of interpretation

Financial execution, documentary values, materiality or concentration must not be interpreted as clinical efficiency, quality of care, irregularity, cost per patient or causality without appropriate additional evidence. Every analysis presented to the partner must respect this limit.

11. Premises

  • The deadline for implementing the Big Data pipeline is 70 calendar days, or 10 weeks.
  • A dataset larger than 1 GB is provided in batches, including a longitudinal financial series and a documentary and textual volume. The handoff baseline covers January 2014 to June 2026, which amounts to 150 monthly periods, and preserves the raw public files and the validation references.
  • The corpus includes a variety of formats and semantics. The 2026 documentary layer covers the 65 UGE of the pilot and is prepared mainly from official batch files, with selective enrichment through the API only when there is a demonstrable information gain.
  • The partner provides reference registries, domain rules, fixtures, manifests and control totals. Standardised tables and governed results are quality benchmarks, not a mandatory path. Teams must show the reconciliation of their results against the controls and state coverage and discrepancies explicitly.

Semantic premise

Financial-flow values, document values and relationships among events are distinct concepts. A relationship such as liquidation to commitment may have N:N cardinality, and it does not replace the financial flows without reconciliation. The model must distinguish Military Region (RM), Management Unit (UG), OMS or Medical Post, and Hospital. It must preserve validity periods and support the expansion from 2 to 12 Military Regions and to the longitudinal series in which 563 base UGs were observed. The 2nd and 5th Military Regions form the entry baseline of the challenge, and any incorporation of the 11th Military Region is an additional expansion test that is not part of the baseline development.

13. Restricted content

The charter adopts a publication principle under which publicly sourced data, code and technical documentation are treated as publishable unless a specific restriction applies. Credentials and keys, personal, sensitive or classified data, internal documents not intended for the class, partner working notes and any material whose disclosure has not been authorised must remain outside open repositories.

15. Operational impact and alignment with the SDGs

The charter records the potential contribution of the project on two fronts, operational impact on SSEx management and, where applicable, alignment with the Sustainable Development Goals of the UN 2030 Agenda. Only connections directly supported by the scope of the challenge are considered.

On the operational side, the project contributes to integrating, documenting, making traceable and analysing public data relevant to management, with a focus on reproducibility, quality and the reduction of manual work. It also develops competencies in software project practices and data engineering consistent with the module. The expected gain is to reduce manual data processing, improve reproducibility and quality and expand the capacity to produce management information at different levels, without replacing the institutional production systems.

16. Reading the charter with the canvas

The sections of the charter correspond to blocks of the Data Model Canvas, which makes the charter the first source for the Sprint 1 blocks. The challenge and the objective feed block 1, the three perspectives and the PI-SSEx reports feed the analytical questions of block 2, and the stakeholders give the community and roles of the enterprise viewpoint. The restrictions and the limit of interpretation belong to the policies of block 8, and the semantic premise is a statement of the information viewpoint, since it fixes which concepts the model must keep apart.

Where the canvas states something that the charter does not support, the group either rewrites the block or turns the difference into a question for the partner workshop.
