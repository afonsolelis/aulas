0:15 · The law
What the LGPD protects and what it asks of a DataApp

The Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados (Law 13,709/2018) applies to every operation on personal data done in Brazil or about people in Brazil. A DataApp that collects, stores, combines or publishes such data is processing it, and its team answers for how.

ConceptArticleIn the card shop
personal data: identifies a person or makes the person identifiableart. 5, Iname, e-mail, CPF, phone, birth date, the customer id
sensitive personal data: health, religion, ethnicity, biometrics and othersart. 5, IIthe accessibility need of 5 customers
anonymised data: can no longer be linked to a person by reasonable meansart. 5, III; art. 12sales by store and age band, with at least 5 customers per group
principles: purpose, necessity, security, prevention, accountabilityart. 6each column with a purpose; analytics without what it does not need
legal bases: consent, legal obligation, contract, legitimate interestart. 7; art. 11the CPF by legal obligation; the health datum only with specific consent
security measures and a record of the operationsarts. 37 and 46roles, row policies, an audit log
Who answersThe controller decides why and how data is processed, the operator processes on its behalf, and the encarregado (art. 41) is the channel between the controller, the people and the ANPD, the national authority, which may fine up to 2 % of the revenue in Brazil, limited to R$ 50 million per infraction (art. 52).

Step 02 · Theory
GRANT decides which tables a job reaches

PostgreSQL checks the privilege of the schema first and of the table after it, and a new function is executable by PUBLIC by default. A grant left to PUBLIC is a grant to every user, present and future.

Jobs, not people

Group roles

CREATE ROLE manager_sp NOLOGIN IN ROLE store_manager: the privileges belong to store_manager, and replacing a manager is one membership, not a review of every grant.

The ETL is blind

Write without read

The ETL inserts customers and orders but cannot select them. A leaked password of the ETL then exposes no customer, which is the point of least privilege.

Future objects

Default privileges

ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES FOR ROLE analytics_owner IN SCHEMA analytics GRANT SELECT ON TABLES TO analyst: every view created later is readable by the analysts, without a forgotten grant.

Step 04 · Theory
Pseudonymised is still personal; anonymised is not

The LGPD draws the line by the possibility of linking the data back to the person. A key that a secret can reverse is pseudonymisation (art. 13, §4) and the law still applies; an aggregate that no reasonable means links to a person is anonymised (art. 12).

Pseudonymise

A peppered key

sha256(pepper || ':' || customer_id), cut to 16 hex characters. Without the pepper, a hash of the id alone is reversed by computing the hash of the 60 possible ids.

Generalise

Fewer details

The birth date becomes 18-24, 25-34, 35-49 or 50+; the moment of the order becomes its day, ordered_unix / 86400 * 86400; the e-mail becomes a***@example.com.

Aggregate

k-anonymity, k = 5

A store and age band with fewer than five customers is not published. Of 11 segments, 7 are published and 4 are suppressed, with 7 customers in them.

Where the pepper livesIn the lab it is a table that only analytics_owner reads. In production it is a secret of the server or an environment variable outside the repository, because whoever holds the pepper and the keys can reverse every pseudonym.

The log is not a copy

Writing the old and new values would make the log a second store of e-mails and CPFs, with its own retention and its own leaks. It keeps the key of the row and the names of the columns.

Append-only

A trigger refuses every UPDATE and DELETE of audit.events, the owner included. The superuser could drop the trigger, and that DDL is itself an event to watch.

Case study
How this portal keeps the exams away from the class

The repository of these lessons is public, so any exam written in it would be readable days before the class. The statement lives in the PostgreSQL of the course, behind PostgREST, with the same controls of the lab.

Deny by default

RLS with no policy

The tables of the exam have RLS enabled and no policy at all, plus REVOKE ALL from the public roles of the API. No call reads a row of them directly.

One door

prova_abrir(slug, token)

A SECURITY DEFINER function with a fixed search_path compares the SHA-256 of the token typed by the professor with the hash it keeps. The token in clear exists in no table, no file and no page.

Every attempt logged

Refusal as data

Each attempt, right or wrong, is a row of a log. A refusal returns {"ok": false} instead of raising an error, because an error rolls back the transaction and the record of the attempt with it.

Secrets outside

Nothing in git

The SQL that loads each exam sits in a folder that git ignores, and the database password in the environment file of the infrastructure. After eight wrong tokens in five minutes the function refuses, but never the right token.

Back to step 05shop.lookup_customer raises an error when it refuses, so a refused lookup leaves no event. Making refusals visible to the DPO is the exercise the case suggests.

About this session

Data Security, Authorizations, and Access · 17/12/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to classify the personal data of a DataApp under the LGPD and implement, in the database, the access controls that follow from it (roles of least privilege, row level security, pseudonymisation with k-anonymity and an append-only audit log), verifying them with an automated access test, first in the card shop of the lab and then on the partner data in ClickHouse.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor applies six controls to the DataApp of a fictitious card shop, live, with a coding agent and six CREATE prompts, in a PostgreSQL (PGlite) that runs inside the browser, proving each control with SET ROLE and closing with a case study of how this portal protects its exams. In the second hour each group applies the same controls to the partner data in the ClickHouse of the class (shrek2) with the seventh prompt.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. The LGPD, data governance and security in layers (5 min)
  3. Steps 01–02: inventory of personal data; roles and least privilege (12 min)
  4. Steps 03–04: row level security; pseudonymisation and k-anonymity (14 min)
  5. Steps 05–06: audit log; the access policy as an automated test; case study of the exams of the portal (14 min)
  6. Second hour: the six controls on the partner data in ClickHouse (60 min)
