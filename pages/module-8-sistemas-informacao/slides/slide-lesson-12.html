0:15 · Cost factors
Every line of a bill is a quantity times a unit price

AWS does not sell servers by the month: each service counts one or more billing dimensions, and the bill multiplies each counted quantity by its unit price. Estimating a cost is therefore estimating the quantities of the workload first.

0:20 · Pricing models
The same hour of t4g.small at four prices

The unit price depends on how the capacity is bought. A commitment lowers the rate in exchange for paying it every hour of the year, used or not; Spot lowers it further in exchange for the right of AWS to reclaim the instance.

Modelt4g.small, us-east-1, per hourCondition
On-demand$0.0168no commitment, billed per second with a minimum of one minute
Compute Savings Plan, 1 year, no upfront$0.0121a commitment in USD per hour, valid for EC2, Fargate and Lambda in any region
Reserved Instance or EC2 Instance Savings Plan, 1 year, no upfront$0.0105tied to the instance family and the region
Spotabout $0.007257% below on-demand on average, by the Spot Instance Advisor; interruption with two minutes of notice
Free allowances

Lambda: 1 million requests and 400,000 GB-seconds per month. Scheduler: 14 million invocations. Egress: 100 GB per month, across the account.

Break-even

A commitment of $0.0105 against $0.0168 pays only if the instance runs more than 0.0105 / 0.0168 = 62.5% of the hours.

Region

The same t4g.small costs $0.0268 per hour in São Paulo, 1.6 times the price in Virginia.

Step 02 · Theory
What each architecture keeps billed while idle

The architectures differ less in the price of a working minute than in what they keep billed during the 99.95% of the month in which nothing runs.

ArchitectureBilled while workingBilled while idle
A · EC2 always oninstance hoursthe instance, the volume and the public IPv4, 730 hours
B · EC2 started for each runinstance and IPv4 for the run and the bootthe EBS volume only
C · Lambda + Scheduler + S3GB-seconds and one request per run; S3 requeststhe bytes kept in S3
D · Fargate task + Scheduler + S3vCPU and memory from the image pull to the end, minimum one minutethe image in ECR and the bytes in S3
E · D + RDS for PostgreSQLas Dthe database instance and its storage, 730 hours
Two limits that decide the choiceLambda stops any run at 15 minutes, which the 40 s of tcg-dataapp respect. Fargate and EC2 have no such limit, so a DataApp whose run grows past it needs one of them.

Step 03 · Theory
Two sources of prices for one estimate

The AWS Pricing Calculator is the reference that AWS offers to customers and the format that a manager receives. The Price List is the same catalogue in machine-readable files, which a program can read again whenever the estimate must be repeated.

calculator.aws

An estimate to share

Service by service, with the region and the configuration chosen in forms; the total per month and per year, a share link and the export in CSV and PDF. Estimates for one group of services are kept together in one link.

AWS Price List

The catalogue as data

One JSON or CSV file per service and region at pricing.us-east-1.amazonaws.com, without a key. Each price has an offer, a usage type such as BoxUsage:t4g.small, a unit and the date of publication. The EC2 file of a region has about 480 MB.

The lab of the sessionThe lab prices the five architectures in the browser from a 17 KB snapshot of the Price List. The same rules are in cost_model.py, and a test proves that the page and Python agree on 180 totals.

About this session

Cloud Infrastructure Pricing · 16/12/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to estimate the monthly cost of the infrastructure of a DataApp from its measured workload, with the AWS Pricing Calculator and with the prices of the AWS Price List, compare alternative architectures, regions and pricing models, and propose an optimisation based on right-sizing, scheduling, commitments and Spot, applying it to the DataApp of the group.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor prices on AWS the DataApp of Lesson 7 in four steps with a coding agent, the AWS Pricing Calculator and the browser lab of the session: the measurement of the workload, five candidate architectures and their billing dimensions, the estimate in two regions and the optimisation. In the second hour each group estimates, compares and optimises the architecture of its own DataApp and delivers the documents and the exported estimates by pull request.
