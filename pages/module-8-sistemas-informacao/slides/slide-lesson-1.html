Why the problem is modelled before the data is queried

A dataset opened without a question produces a description of its columns. The question comes from the organisation, and it determines which tables matter, at which grain and under which rules.

Starting from the tables

An inventory that answers no decision

The group lists tables and columns, counts rows and draws charts. At the end of the sprint it knows the shape of the data, but it cannot say which of the findings matters to the partner.

Starting from the problem

A reading guided by the decision

The group first states who decides, which decision is at stake and which rules constrain it. The dataset is then read to find the facts that support that decision and to expose the facts that are missing.

What the module asks

A single line of reasoning, recorded in parts

Each artefact of the module records one part of this reasoning. The Data Model Canvas is the document in which those parts are written next to each other and kept consistent.

The RM-ODP supplies the order of that reasoning. Purpose and rules are described first, information second, and processing, distribution and technology afterwards.
RM-ODP as a framework for separating concerns

The Reference Model of Open Distributed Processing is the standard ISO/IEC 10746, also published by ITU-T as Recommendations X.901 to X.904. It describes a distributed system through five viewpoints, each with its own set of concerns and its own vocabulary.

Viewpoints

Each concern is specified by those responsible for it

A single model that mixes business rules, data structures, components and servers grows unreadable, and every change touches all of it. Separate viewpoints let each concern be written, reviewed and revised on its own.

Correspondences

The viewpoints describe the same system

The standard requires explicit correspondences between viewpoints. A KPI in the information viewpoint traces to an objective in the enterprise viewpoint, and a component in the computational viewpoint processes objects that the information viewpoint defines.

Structure of the standard

Four parts and a separate enterprise language

Part 1 is an overview, Part 2 sets the foundations, Part 3 defines the architecture and the viewpoints, and Part 4 gives their formal semantics. The enterprise language was later detailed in ISO/IEC 15414.

Fit with the module

A data platform is an open distributed system

Several areas of an organisation produce and consume shared information under rules, through components that run on distributed infrastructure. These are precisely the concerns that the five viewpoints separate.

Enterprise viewpoint: purpose, roles and policies

The enterprise viewpoint describes the system as part of an organisation. It names the community that uses it, the objective it serves, the roles people play and the policies that constrain their behaviour.

Community

A group of actors formed to achieve an objective. In the project, the partner areas that produce and consume the data form the community.

Objective

The practical result the community expects, stated so that it can be verified, such as reducing stock-outs of a product line.

Role

A position defined by behaviour and independent of the person who holds it. Data owner, data steward and analyst are roles.

Process

The sequence of steps through which the objective is pursued, such as the replenishment of a store's stock.

Policy

A rule stated as an obligation, a permission, a prohibition or an authorisation. LGPD requirements and internal sharing rules are policies.

Questions for the partner

Who decides? Which decision, and how often? Which rule cannot be broken? Who is accountable for each dataset?

Information viewpoint: objects, schemas and constraints

The information viewpoint describes the information the system handles, independently of how it is stored or processed. Part 3 of the standard organises that description into three kinds of schema.

Invariant schema

What holds at every moment

Constraints that no state of the system may violate. Stock on hand is never negative, and every sale line refers to an existing product.

Static schema

The state at a given instant

The values of the information objects at one point in time, such as the stock position of each product in each store at midnight.

Dynamic schema

The changes allowed over time

The permitted transitions between states. A purchase order moves from created to approved and then to received, or it is cancelled.

Grain is an information decision. Stating that a fact table holds one row per product, store and day is a choice made in this viewpoint, and every KPI computed later depends on it.
Illustrative case: the enterprise viewpoint

A regional pharmacy chain reports that continuous-use medicines are often missing from its stores. The case is fictitious and serves only to show the reasoning that the groups apply to the partner.

Community and roles

Store managers, the purchasing team, pharmacists and the regional director. The purchasing director is the data owner, and an analyst of the purchasing team acts as data steward.

Objective and decision

Reduce stock-outs of continuous-use medicines. The decision at stake is the weekly order quantity for each product in each store.

Process

Sales reduce stock, the system suggests an order, purchasing approves it, the supplier delivers and the store records the receipt.

Policies

Health data is sensitive personal data under the LGPD (art. 5, II, and art. 11), and the chain's policy forbids using identifiable prescription data for marketing. Store managers are permitted to see only their own store.

Illustrative case: the information viewpoint

The decision about order quantities needs information about sales, stock and orders. The entities below are what the group would expect to find in the dataset, and the reading of the data confirms or corrects them.

KPI definition

Stock-out rate

Product-store-days with on_hand = 0 divided by product-store-days observed, computed weekly for continuous-use products.

Schemas

Invariant: on_hand ≥ 0. Static: the daily stock position. Dynamic: the status of a purchase order.

What a complete entry looks like in the Sprint 1 blocks

A block is complete when another reader can verify it against the partner and the data. The criteria below apply to the blocks that belong to the enterprise and information viewpoints.

Business · 1–3

Objectives are stated as verifiable results. Analytical questions are phrased as the decision-maker would ask them. Processes are named by a verb and an object, such as "replenish store stock".

Analytical consumption · 4–6

Each KPI has a formula, a unit and a periodicity. Each user is linked to the channel through which the data reaches them. Open decisions are written as questions with an owner.

Corporate governance · 7–9

Owner, steward and custodian are named people or areas. Personal and sensitive data are classified under the LGPD. The location of the data catalogue is stated.

First cube · a–f

The grain fits in one sentence. Facts and metrics are listed with their units. Each dimension answers at least one analytical question from block 2.

04 · Reference tables for institutional identity

The monthly files say which management unit spent. The reference tables say which organisation that unit is, to which region it belongs and since when, which is what the Regional and Local perspectives of the charter require.

Who executes

UGE and reconciliation

01_uge_correntes lists the 65 current UGE, 29 in the 2nd and 36 in the 5th Military Region, with their physical codes. 02_reconciliacao relates them to the 69 historical executors and flags which ones enter the current totals.

Which organisation

Organisations and bridges

03_dim_om describes 118 organisations keyed by om_id, preferably the CODOM. Three bridges link region to OM, brigade to OM and UGE to supported OM, each with validity dates and evidence status.

How to read the values

Special cases, IPCA, dictionary

07_situacoes_especiais records five transformed or predecessor units. 08_ipca gives the deflation factor of each month to December 2025, and 09_dicionario defines 17 analytical fields.

The bridges carry no financial values, and their allocation rule says so explicitly. The financial fact stays at the executing UGE, so copying a UGE total to every organisation it supports would count the same money several times.

Questions for discussing the partner data

The README of the package states six rules of use. Each one points to a decision that the information viewpoint of the canvas has to record before any query is written.

Grain

The raw file, the curated panel and the documentary families describe the same expenditure at three different grains. Which grain does each analytical question of block 2 need?

Identity

A unit appears under a 160xxx and a 167xxx code, and the package counts 65 current UGE against 69 historical executors. Which key should the model use, and how is the difference explained to a manager?

Stages

Commitment, liquidation and payment are distinct concepts, and a payment may settle a commitment of an earlier year. Which stage answers the question the partner asks?

Limits

The package does not measure clinical efficiency or quality of care. Which KPIs written in block 4 would cross that limit, and how should they be rewritten?

The charter is available as a page on the module home, linked from the TAPI entry of the sidebar. Its semantic premise and its restrictions are the main source for these answers.
