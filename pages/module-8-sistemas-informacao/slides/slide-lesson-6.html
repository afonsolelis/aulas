0:15 · Running case
The market of Pokémon cards on TCGplayer

TCGplayer is the largest marketplace of collectible cards in the United States. For every product it publishes a market price per printing, computed from recent sales, and the public mirror tcgcsv.com keeps one copy of those prices per day.

Collector

What is a card worth today?

The price of one card, in one printing, on one date: a Pikachu ex numbered 276/217 in Holofoil cost US$ 571.17 on 07/11/2026.

Store

Which rarities and printings pay off?

The average price by rarity and the premium of a Reverse Holofoil over the Normal print of the same card decide what a store keeps in stock.

Investor

Which cards gained value during the year?

A card that appreciates is compared with itself on two dates. The sum of a whole set changes for other reasons, such as cards that only start to be priced later.

Question of the sessionAmong the cards of the seven Pokémon TCG sets released in 2026, which ones appreciated most between their first price capture and 07/11/2026, in percentage and in dollars?

Step 01 · Theory 1
Dimensional modelling in four decisions

Kimball designs a model from a business process and its questions, not from the tables of the source. Each decision constrains the next, and the grain is the one that costs most to change later.

1 · Process

Pricing a product

The process measured is the market price that TCGplayer publishes for each product, recomputed every day from recent sales.

2 · Grain

Product × variant × date

One row is one product, in one printing, on one capture date. The same card in Holofoil and in Reverse Holofoil has two prices.

3 · Dimensions

Set, product, variant, date

They describe the context of each price: which set, which card and rarity, which printing and which day.

4 · Facts

Market, low, mid, high

The four prices of the record, plus the days since the release of the set, computed when the fact is built.

The grain decides the questionsA grain of one set per date would answer how much a set is worth, but never which card appreciated. The finer grain answers both, because the value of a set is the sum of its cards on one date.

Step 02 · Theory 1
Three kinds of fact table

Kimball distinguishes facts by what one row records. The kind decides how rows arrive and which sums are valid.

Transaction

One event

One row per event, inserted once and never updated, such as one item of one order in the star of Lesson 5. Its measures usually add up in every direction.

Periodic snapshot · this lesson

One state per period

One row per entity per period, even when nothing happened, such as the price of a card on each capture date. It shows how a state evolves.

Accumulating snapshot

One process with milestones

One row per process, updated as it advances, such as an order with the dates of approval, shipping and delivery and the days between them.

Why a periodic snapshot hereThe question compares the price of the same card on different dates. TCGplayer does not publish each sale, only the price of the day, so the state on each capture date is the finest fact available.
Step 02 · Theory 2
Additive, semi-additive and non-additive measures

A measure is classified by the dimensions across which its sum still means something. The classification decides how the cube and every report may aggregate it.

KindAdds up acrossExample in this lessonExample in Lesson 5
additiveevery dimensionnumber of price recordsprice and freight of the items
semi-additiveevery dimension except timemarket price: the cards of a set on one date add up to the cost of the set; one card over five dates does notopen orders on a day
non-additiveno dimensionaverage price of a rarity; percentage of appreciationdistinct count of orders
Rule for semi-additive

Every aggregation keeps the time dimension, or takes one point of it (the last date, the first date), never the sum over it.

Rule for non-additive

The value is recomputed from the fact at each level. An average of averages, or a sum of distinct counts, gives a wrong number.

Step 03 · Theory 1
What a data cube is

A cube organises a measure by several dimensions at once. Each cell is the measure for one combination of members, and the totals along each dimension are cells too.

Dimensions of the cube

Set × rarity × variant

Three axes, with the date fixed in each slice. A cell answers: on this date, the cards of this set, of this rarity, in this printing.

Levels

2³ = 8 combinations

All three dimensions, each pair, each one alone and none. The level where a dimension is aggregated shows all in its place.

Storage

MOLAP, ROLAP, HOLAP

A cube can live in a dedicated engine (MOLAP), as summary tables of a relational warehouse (ROLAP), or both. This lesson builds the relational kind.

Summarised fact tableThe cube stored as a table is an aggregate fact: a fact table with a coarser grain, computed from the base fact, which answers the frequent questions without reading 5,951 rows and joining four dimensions every time.
Step 03 · Theory 2
CUBE, ROLLUP and GROUPING SETS

The three extensions of GROUP BY compute several groupings in one statement. They differ in which combinations they generate, and grouping() tells each row apart.

ClauseGeneratesFor three columns
CUBE (a, b, c)every combination8 groupings
ROLLUP (a, b, c)the hierarchy from left to right4: (a,b,c), (a,b), (a), ()
GROUPING SETS (...)only the listed onesas many as listed
grouping(a, b, c)a bit per aggregated column0 = finest, 7 = total
the cube of the session
SELECT f.date_key,
       grouping(s.set_name, p.rarity, f.variant)
                                   AS grouping_id,
       coalesce(s.set_name, 'all') AS set_name,
       ...
       sum(f.market_price)         AS market_value
FROM gold.fact_price AS f ...
GROUP BY f.date_key,
         CUBE (s.set_name, p.rarity, f.variant);
ROLLUP for a hierarchyWhen the columns form a hierarchy, such as year, quarter and month, ROLLUP generates only the meaningful levels. CUBE over a hierarchy would also produce month without year, which repeats the same month of different years.

Step 03 · Theory 3
Views, tables and materialisation

An optimised view is a stored result that spares the repeated cost of a query. The engines differ in how that result is kept up to date.

ObjectKeeps dataWhen it is computedIn this lesson
view (DuckDB and ClickHouse)no, only the queryat every readdimensions and mart
table from CREATE TABLE AS (DuckDB)yeswhen the statement runs; refreshing means running it againfact and cube
incremental materialized view (ClickHouse)yes, in a target tableat every INSERT, over the inserted blocksecond hour, if the group loads in batches
refreshable materialized view (ClickHouse)yes, replaced at onceon a schedule, over the whole sourcesecond hour: fact and cube
The choice is a trade-offA view is always current and pays the full cost at each read. A stored result is cheap to read and stale until the next refresh. A dimension that is small and changes little fits a view; a cube read by a dashboard fits a stored result.

Step 04 · Theory
The five operations of OLAP

Every multidimensional question is a movement over the cube: along a hierarchy, inside a dimension or across the axes.

Roll-upClimb a hierarchy

From the card to its rarity, from the day to the month. Fewer cells, higher level.

Drill-downDescend a hierarchy

From the rarity to its cards, from the year to the quarters.

SliceFix one member

Only Ascended Heroes: the cube loses one dimension.

DiceFix several members

Four rarities, three variants, one date: a smaller cube.

PivotTurn the axes

Dates as columns, sets as rows: the same cells in another layout.

About this session

Data Cubes and Data Marts · 10/11/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to model a data cube with at least three dimensions over a landing table, declaring its grain, its dimensions with their hierarchies and the additivity of its measures, to build a summarised fact table with CUBE and a data mart with views over conformed dimensions, and to answer multidimensional questions with roll-up, slice, dice and pivot, first over the prices of the Pokémon TCG sets of 2026 and then over the partner data in ClickHouse.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor builds the model live with a coding agent in four steps (dimensions, fact, cube and mart, and five questions), pasting each CREATE prompt and running the SQL it writes in a lab page with DuckDB inside the browser, over a landing table kept in the localStorage; while the agent works, the slides explain the concept of the step, and each step closes with a review against the numbers of the rehearsal. In the second hour each group repeats the four steps over the partner data in the ClickHouse of the class, starting with a specification that is reviewed before any SQL.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. Running case, landing table db_ingestion and lab in the browser — 7 min
  3. Step 01: grain, dimensions and hierarchies; a natural key that repeats — 12 min
  4. Step 02: the fact of prices as a periodic snapshot; additive, semi-additive and non-additive measures — 8 min
  5. Step 03: the cube with CUBE and ROLLUP as a summarised fact table; views and materialisation; data mart and conformed dimensions — 12 min
  6. Step 04: the five operations of OLAP and the five questions — 10 min
  7. Second hour: each group models the partner data in ClickHouse (shrek2), from the specification to the cube and five answers — 60 min
