The running example: three sources, one stock-out rate

The fictitious pharmacy chain of Lesson 1 now needs its indicator in production. The data team must turn three heterogeneous sources into a weekly stock-out rate that the purchasing team reads every Monday, and the exposition uses this example throughout.

Sources

POS terminals send JSON files every 15 minutes and resend the whole day after a lost connection. The ERP delivers a nightly extract, and suppliers send CSV files whose columns change without notice.

What the analysis found

Duplicated sale lines in 6% of store-days, stock counts arriving up to three days late, and prescription numbers linked to the customer's CPF inside the sale lines.

Constraints

A team of four, a lake on object storage and a PostgreSQL warehouse, a first version in eight weeks, and 30 new stores planned for next year.

The three layers of the medallion architecture

The medallion architecture organises a lakehouse into layers of increasing refinement. Each layer has a contract with the next one, and the contract states what the data guarantees when it leaves the layer.

LayerPurposeTypical content in the caseGuarantee to the next layer
BronzeKeep what the sources sent, so that any later step can be repeatedPOS JSON files, nightly ERP extracts and supplier CSVs, partitioned by source and ingestion dateCompleteness and traceability to the original delivery
SilverOffer conformed, deduplicated and typed entities that the whole organisation can trustOne sale line per sale and one stock position per product, store and day, with sensitive fields treatedDeclared grain, enforced invariants and stable keys
GoldDeliver data modelled for a specific consumptionThe stock-position fact with store, product and date dimensions, and the weekly stock-out rateBusiness definitions that match the KPIs of the canvas
The layers do not prescribe tools. Bronze can be a bucket of files and Gold a schema in PostgreSQL, as long as each contract is stated and checked.
Data quality checks change from layer to layer

Each layer tests what its contract promises. A check placed in the wrong layer either arrives too late to stop bad data or too early to have the context it needs.

Bronze

Did everything arrive?

File counts per source and period, expected schema, arrival time against the schedule and volume compared with the same day of the previous week.

Silver

Does the data obey the model?

Uniqueness of the declared grain, invariants such as stock never negative, referential integrity and the share of rows sent to quarantine.

Gold

Does the indicator make sense?

Reconciliation of totals with Silver, KPI values within plausible ranges and freshness of the data that the Monday dashboard reads.

The pipeline as a UML component diagram

A component diagram shows the replaceable parts of a system and the interfaces through which they connect. Each component provides an interface that the next one requires, which makes the contracts between layers explicit.

Component

A modular part with a defined responsibility that can be replaced without changing its neighbours.

Provided and required interfaces

Drawn as a ball and a socket. They state what one component offers and what the next one needs.

Dependencies and artefacts

Dashed arrows show dependencies, and artefacts such as Parquet files or tables show what is deployed.

Tactics that let the pipeline scale

A pipeline scales when the work of each run grows with the new data, independently of the size of the history. In the example, thirty new stores will increase the POS volume by about a third. Each tactic below absorbs that growth at a cost that the architecture must state.

Partitioning

Data is split by date and store, so each run touches only the slices that changed. Many small files require compaction.

Incremental processing

A watermark records what was processed. Late data needs a lookback window, or it is skipped.

Idempotent reruns

Rerunning a day produces the same result, which makes reprocessing after a failure safe. It requires overwriting partitions rather than appending.

Decoupling through layers

Each layer can be recomputed from the previous one, so a fix in Silver does not require new extractions from the sources.
