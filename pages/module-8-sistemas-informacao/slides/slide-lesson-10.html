0:15 · The three signals
Each signal answers a different question

A metric is a number kept over time, cheap to store and to evaluate, which says that something changed. A trace is the path of one execution as a tree of timed spans, which says where. A log is a record of a discrete event, which says what the program knew at that moment.

Metrics

Is something wrong?

tcg_run_success 0, tcg:price_freshness_seconds. Aggregated by design, so the cost does not grow with the number of runs, only with the number of series. Prometheus evaluates them every 15 s and fires the alerts.

Traces

Where did it go wrong?

One run of 30.6 s is a trace of 38 spans: the run, 13 tasks, 20 requests to the source and 4 SQL files. The failing span carries the status ERROR, the attempts and the exception. Stored in Tempo.

Logs

What exactly happened?

JSON lines such as task.fail, with the attempt and the error text, written inside the span they describe. Stored in Loki and in out/logs/, and searched by any field.

The common errorThree tools that do not share identifiers give three partial views. The value of the signals is in the path from one to the other, which the next slide shows.

Step 01 · Theory
Metric types, and why a batch job pushes

The type says how the number may change and which functions make sense over it. Prometheus pulls from its targets every 15 s; a run of 30 s has no endpoint after it ends, so it pushes to a Pushgateway, which is scraped in its place.

0:42 · Control theory
The elements of a closed loop

A closed loop measures the output of a process, compares it with a reference and acts on the process to reduce the difference. Each element of the diagram has a counterpart in the DataApp, and the observability of the previous steps is only one of them: the sensor.

Comparator

error = SLI − SLO. A positive error is a violation: data older than 36 h, a source failing above 50 %, a grain broken.

Controller

decide(), a pure function of the SLIs, the state and the clock, which returns one action per tick.

Feedback

The next tick reads the effect of the last action. Without it, the system acts blindly, which is an open loop.

0:44 · Observability is not control
Observability alone does not close the loop

Metrics, traces and logs measure the process and show it. A dashboard and an alert give the measurement to a person, who then decides and acts. The loop closes only where something compares the measurement with the setpoint and acts on the process.

Open loopLoop closed by a personAutomatic closed loop
In the DataAppcron runs the DataApp at 06:00, whatever happenedthe alert calls the person on call, who reruns or fixestcg-dataapp control reruns, opens the breaker or quarantines

0:45 · Stability
A loop that acts must not oscillate

An automatic action changes the process that the sensor reads next. A rerun against a source that is down fails again; a threshold crossed back and forth turns the action on and off at every tick. Guardrails bound what the controller may do, so its failure modes are slow and visible.

Hysteresis

A dead band on the freshness

The data becomes stale above 36 h and fresh again only below 30 h. Between them, the previous state holds, so a value near the threshold does not flap.

Backoff

A circuit breaker on the source

When the source fails, the controller stops calling it. It probes after 300 s, then 600 s, then 1,200 s, and escalates at the fourth opening.

Budget and cooldown

At most 3 reruns in 6 h

Reruns are at least 600 s apart. When the budget is spent, the problem is not transient, and the controller escalates.

Escalation once

Then it holds

After escalating, the controller holds every automatic action until the SLIs recover or a person runs control --acknowledge.

0:58 · The same idea at scale
Closed loops in the tools you will meet

The controller of the DataApp is small, but its structure is the one of the control loops that run most production systems: a declared desired state, a measurement of the current state, and an action that reduces the difference.

Kubernetes

Reconciliation loop

Each controller compares the desired state of a resource with the observed state and acts until they match: a Deployment of three replicas recreates a pod that died.

A setpoint of CPU or of a custom metric, a sensor in the metrics API, and an actuator on the number of replicas, with a stabilisation window against oscillation.

Alertmanager

Webhook to remediation

An alert sent to a webhook that runs a playbook closes the loop for a known failure; routing, grouping and silences are its guardrails.

SRE

Error budget

The SLO allows a measured amount of failure. While budget remains, changes go out; when it is spent, releases stop. It is a feedback loop on how a team works.

Three signals and one loop for your DataApp

Each group applies the method to its own DataApp, the package of Lesson 7 or the flow of Lesson 8, with prompt 05. The measure of success is a failure rehearsed on purpose and followed from the alert to the log line, and one SLO whose loop either recovers or escalates once.

The three signals
  • metrics of each run, pushed, with an info metric of the run and the trace
  • one span per run, task, call to the source and transformation
  • JSON lines with run_id, trace_id and span_id
  • three alerting rules, each with a promtool test
One closed loop
  • one SLO with its setpoint and dead band
  • the sensor, the actuator and what it changes
  • cooldown, budget and the condition to escalate
  • a test that breaks the DataApp and proves the loop

About this session

Metrics and Telemetry · 09/12/2026 · Prof. Afonso

Learning objective

By the end of the session, students should be able to instrument a DataApp with metrics, traces and structured logs linked by run, trace and span identifiers, follow a failure from the alert to the log line that explains it, and close the loop on one SLO with a controller that compares the indicator with its setpoint, acts within guardrails and escalates to a person when it cannot recover.

Session strategy

Demonstration followed by application. In the first hour the professor builds the observability of the Lesson 7 package live with a coding agent and four CREATE prompts, injecting faults to follow a failure through the three signals and to watch the controller recover or escalate, and distinguishes observability, the sensor and display of the loop, from control, which compares and acts. In the second hour each group applies the method to its own DataApp with prompt 05.

Session structure

  1. Daily — 15 min
  2. The three signals and their correlation (7 min)
  3. Step 01 — metrics in Prometheus (8 min)
  4. Step 02 — traces and structured logs with OpenTelemetry (8 min)
  5. Step 03 — the stack; from the alert to the log line (4 min)
  6. The closed loop, and why observability alone does not close it (4 min)
  7. Step 04 — the controller of the DataApp with injected faults (14 min)
  8. Second hour — the groups apply it to their DataApp (60 min)
