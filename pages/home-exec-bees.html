BEES Functional Trainings

Executive programme designed by Inteli for BEES on Global Product Scalability and Variability. It is taught online and entirely in English to the global senior leadership of the company, in four tracks organised by function. This page gathers the three sessions taught by Professor Afonso Brandão, with the slides, the reading material and the lab kit of each one.

Sessions

Each session lasts two hours and follows the standard structure of the programme: an opening with a retrieval question, a concept briefing, a worked example, an applied lab in breakout groups, a debrief and the transfer to the next session. The labs use Tradeline, a fictional B2B ordering marketplace with synthetic data, prepared for teaching.

The programme

Every session of the programme is organised around one challenge shared by the four functions: scaling a global software product to meet the distinct needs of customers and markets while preserving a common core and keeping development effort, operating cost, technical complexity and long-term maintenance under control. Meeting each demand in isolation expands reach in the short term, but it tends to multiply code, configurations, tests and special operations. For that reason, every track treats the effect of a variation decision on the business and on the end user as part of the analysis.

The tracks are connected by one cycle. Data & Analytics makes the signals from customers and markets comparable, Product turns them into needs and capabilities, Engineering & Platform chooses the mechanisms that absorb variation with reuse and isolation, and Program makes capacity, dependencies, risk and lifecycle cost explicit. After launch, telemetry measures adoption, impact and operational load and feeds the next decision. The sessions on this page sit at two points of that cycle: the choice and verification of variation mechanisms, and the telemetry foundation that makes markets comparable.

TrackQuestion the track works on
Engineering & PlatformHow to design and evolve a platform that accommodates variation across customers and markets, maximising reuse and minimising complexity, effort and operational load, with a clear view of the risk each change poses to the business and to the people who use the product.
ProductHow to use data to decide which needs become global capabilities, which become configurable features and which remain outside the core roadmap.
ProgramHow to prioritise and coordinate competing demands, dependencies, resources and risks, with visibility of the financial and operational impact of each choice.
Data & AnalyticsHow to use data to distinguish local requests from recurring global needs and to express the evidence as impact for the business and its customers.

Shared language

The sessions use the terms below with a fixed meaning, so that a decision discussed in one track can be read in the others.

TermMeaning in the sessions
Global coreCapabilities and rules that stay consistent for all customers and markets and evolve as part of the common product.
ConfigurationAnticipated variation, activated by parameters, policies, tiers or metadata, without per-customer code changes.
EntitlementCommercial or contractual rule that defines who can use a capability. It is a different mechanism from a feature flag.
Extension and integrationMechanisms that meet needs outside the core, such as APIs, adapters, events or plugins.
ExceptionLogic or artefact specific to one customer or market, justifiable when it has an explicit cost, an owner, a lifecycle and an exit path.
Feature flagMechanism for release, experimentation and progressive rollout, which should not become permanent product policy without governance.
Variability debtAccumulated cost of branches, conditionals, forks, configurations, versions, schema differences and exceptions that are hard to test and operate.
Cost-to-serveMarginal and recurring cost of operating, supporting, observing and evolving a customer, segment or capability, including the cost of incidents.

Format

The sessions are taught live on Zoom between the end of February and July 2027, and the dates of each class will be added here once the calendar is confirmed. Classes of 40 to 50 participants are split into groups of five or six for the labs, with four rotating roles: one participant leads the discussion, one records the deliverable, one keeps time and one acts as the sceptic who challenges assumptions. Every lab has a no-login alternative, based on shared documents and pre-generated outputs, for the case in which a tool or repository is not accessible during the session.

Each session has a pre-work of 15 minutes, sent one week before, and a post-work of 20 minutes that applies the artefact of the session to a real situation of the participant's team, without confidential names. The facilitator notes and the answer keys of the labs are kept in the professor area, which is encrypted because this site is public.
