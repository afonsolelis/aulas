By the end of the programme, participants should be able to formulate the business question behind a decision, judge how far an analysis produced with data and AI tools can be trusted, and defend an executive recommendation, using the Maré Alta case as the common thread.

1. The case and the programme

AI for Business: Data-Driven Decisions is a three-day programme of Inteli Executive Education for senior decision-makers. A single case, set in the fictional company Maré Alta Beverages, runs through its three mornings and is the thread that connects the modules and the professors.

The case places the participants in the working group that advises the chief financial officer of a brewer and distributor on how to allocate next year's commercial investment. Three executives of the company defend three different uses of the same budget, each with numbers taken from the same data. The participants formulate the question that the group should answer on the first morning, analyse the data with Databricks, Genie and Microsoft Copilot on the second and present an executive recommendation on the third.

Nobody builds a model or writes a query during the programme. The case was designed to exercise what falls to the person who decides: asking the right question, judging how far an analysis can be trusted and choosing between initiatives. The company, its brands, its executives and all the data are fictional and were created for the programme.

4. Using the data in the tools

In Databricks, each CSV file becomes a table with the name of the file. Loading the two releases into separate schemas, for example mare_alta_raw and mare_alta_curated, keeps the work of the first day apart from that of the following days. A Genie space created over each schema answers better when the description of each column, taken from the data dictionary below, is registered in the table.

In Excel, the workbook opens with a README sheet followed by the five tables. The sales table has about 100,000 rows, which Excel handles, although questions to Copilot are answered faster and more reliably on the smaller tables. In either tool, a question that states the period, the metric and the grouping gets a better answer than one that leaves them open, and the fiscal year of the company, from October to September, has to be stated in the question.

5. Structure of the extract

The extract has five tables that describe 1,150 points of sale of Maré Alta Beverages over 36 months, from October 2023 to September 2026. All the data is synthetic and was generated for the programme.

The fiscal year runs from October to September, so FY24 covers October 2023 to September 2024, FY25 the following twelve months and FY26 the twelve months up to September 2026. Volumes are expressed in hectolitres (1 HL = 100 litres) and monetary values in Brazilian reais. Months are stored as the first day of the month, in the format YYYY-MM-DD.

The tables sales_monthly, trade_spend and churn_scores relate to pos through pos_id, and sales_monthly relates to products through product_id.

The two releases

The extract is distributed in two releases with the same structure. The raw release is the extract as it left the source systems and is the one examined on the first day. The curated release is the version that the data team delivered after the working group reviewed the raw one, and it is used on the second and third days. The data team corrected in it only what could be corrected without a business decision, and a note delivered with it lists what was changed and what was left as it was.

ColumnTypeDescription

9. Table trade_spend

ColumnTypeDescription
monthdateFirst day of the month in which the spend was booked.
pos_idtextPoint of sale that received the investment.
spend_typetextCooler installation (one-off, at the start of the programme in the outlet), Cooler maintenance (monthly), POS activation (promotional actions at the outlet), Merchandising materials or Listing fee (quarterly payments to supermarkets).
amount_brlnumberAmount booked.

Invoice discounts are recorded in sales_monthly and are not repeated in this table, so the commercial investment of a period is the sum of the two.
