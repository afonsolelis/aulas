Analytical skills, mindset and organisations

Analytical thinking operates at three levels, and a decision improves only when the three are present, because a skilled person in an organisation that ignores evidence produces analyses that nobody uses.

Skills

What the person can do

Break a problem into parts, compare like with like, read a number together with its denominator and its period, and separate an observation from the interpretation given to it.

Mindset

How the person approaches a claim

Ask what evidence would change the opinion, treat a first result as a hypothesis and accept that the answer may contradict the proposal of a colleague or one's own.

Organisation

How the company decides

Davenport and Harris describe companies that compete on analytics as those in which leaders require evidence, data are shared across areas and decisions are reviewed against results.

Provost and Fawcett call this data-analytic thinking: the manager does not need to build the analysis, but needs to understand it well enough to ask whether it answers the decision at hand and how it could be wrong.

Davenport and Harris (2007); Provost and Fawcett (2013)

Four kinds of analytical question

Questions differ in what they ask of the data, and naming the kind of question tells the partner what evidence an answer requires and how far it can be trusted.

KindWhat it asksBeverage exampleWhat the answer requires
DescriptiveWhat happened?How did can volume in the off-trade evolve over the last three summers?Consistent definitions and complete data for the period
DiagnosticWhy did it happen?Why did a region lose share in convenience stores after a price change?A comparison group that differs only in the suspected cause
PredictiveWhat is likely to happen?Which bars are likely to stop ordering in the next quarter?A model evaluated on cases it did not see, with the right metric
PrescriptiveWhat should we do?Where should next year's trade budget go?The three answers above and an explicit criterion of value
Helena's request is prescriptive, but each of the three executives supports a prescription with a descriptive, diagnostic or predictive claim. The question the class writes later decides which of these claims has to be tested first.

From business question to decision and data

A business question is useful when it is attached to a decision someone will take, because only then is it possible to say which unknown matters and which data would resolve it.

1Decision

Which choice will be made, by whom and by when, and what the alternatives are.

2Unknown

What the decision-maker does not know today and that would change the choice if known.

3Metric

How the unknown is measured: the indicator, its unit, its period and the comparison.

4Data

Which tables and columns hold the metric, at what level of detail, and with what known gaps.

5Action

What the decision-maker does for each plausible answer, written before the analysis.

Writing step 5 before the analysis protects the group against a question whose every answer leads to the same action. In that case the analysis has no value for the decision, however interesting the result.

Adapted from Provost and Fawcett (2013), ch. 2, and from the business understanding phase of CRISP-DM 1.0 (Chapman et al., 2000)

Template for the business question

The template forces the five elements of the chain into a single sentence, so that a missing element becomes visible as an empty slot.

To decide <decision> by <date>, we need to know <unknown>, measured by <metric>, using <data>.
Example outside the case

To decide whether to keep the second weekly delivery to mini-markets in the interior of the state by March 2027, we need to know how much volume these outlets would lose with a single weekly delivery, measured by the change in monthly hectolitres per outlet against comparable outlets already on one delivery, using two years of invoices by outlet and the route calendar.

Why it works

The decision has an owner and a date, the unknown is the only thing that would change the choice, the metric names its comparison group, and the data exist in systems the company already has.

The example is deliberately outside Maré Alta. The question of the case is written by the class, and no slide of this deck contains it.
Narrowing a question that is too broad

A broad question cannot be answered with one extract in one morning of analysis, and the group narrows it by fixing one element of the template at each step.

Too broadHow do we reduce the cost of returnable bottles?
Decision fixedShould we raise the deposit charged to bars on returnable crates in the 2027 price list?
Unknown fixedWould bars return crates faster if the deposit were higher?
Decision-readyTo decide whether to raise the crate deposit before the 2027 price list is published, we need to know whether return times differ between bars paying different deposits, measured by the share of crates returned within 30 days at each deposit level, using crate movement records by outlet.
At each step, ask which element of the template is still open and which proposal on the table the answer would confirm or reject. When the group cannot say, the question is still too broad.
Five tests for a business question

The group applies the five tests to its draft before reading it to the class, and rewrites the element that fails instead of adding words to the sentence.

TestThe question passes whenA sign that it fails
DecisionIt names a choice that Helena takes to the board within the R$ 22 million envelopeIt describes a topic, such as "understand the margin"
ConsequenceDifferent answers lead to different allocations of the budgetEvery plausible answer leads to the same action
MeasurabilityThe unknown has a metric, a period and a comparisonIt uses words such as "impact" or "effectiveness" without a measure
DataThe five tables of the extract can, in principle, produce the metricIt requires data the company does not have, such as competitor prices
ScopeIt can be analysed in one morning by the working groupIt contains "and" joining two decisions

Where the data of a commercial decision come from

Every number reaches the partner after passing through systems, files and people, and data governance defines who owns each source, who may change it and which version is the official one.

Transactional

Corporate ERP

Invoices, discounts and costs recorded at the moment of sale. In the case, the outlets managed in ERP_CORE.

Inherited

Acquired systems

Data of an acquired distributor, sent in a monthly file with its own codes. In the case, LEGACY_NE.

External

Bought data

Indicators from a provider, such as the neighbourhood income index, with coverage the company does not control.

Derived

Model outputs

Scores and predictions produced by the analytics team, such as the churn score, which inherit the quality of their inputs.

Raw and curated

Platforms such as Databricks usually keep a raw layer, as received from the sources, and a curated layer, corrected by the data team. Day 1 uses the raw release so that the class sees what the curation has to decide.

Roles

The data owner, usually a business area, decides what a field means and what is correct. The data steward or data team applies the rules, and the analyst reports what could not be corrected.

Dimensions of data quality

Data are of good quality when they are fit for the decision they serve, and each dimension below corresponds to a check that can be made by looking at the tables.

DimensionQuestion to askHow to look at it without code
CompletenessAre values missing, and are they missing at random or in one group?Filter blanks in each column and compare their share by source system or region
ConsistencyIs the same thing written the same way everywhere?List the distinct values of each text column and compare them with the dictionary
ValidityAre values within the range and unit the dictionary states?Sort numeric columns and read the largest, the smallest and the negative values
UniquenessDoes each key appear only once?Count rows by month and region and look for a month that jumps without reason
IntegrityDoes every key in one table exist in the table it refers to?Check that identifiers in the other tables appear in pos
TimelinessDo the data cover the period the question needs?Read the first and last month and the snapshot date of each table

Wang and Strong (1996); DAMA International, DAMA-DMBOK, 2nd ed. (2017)

Correctable by rule

A problem whose correct value is known without judgement, such as a label written in two ways for the same thing.

Needs a business decision

A problem whose treatment changes the result and has no single correct answer, such as whether to exclude records or fill missing values.

System 1, System 2 and the decision process

Kahneman describes two modes of thinking, a fast and automatic one and a slow and deliberate one, and most biases appear when a decision that needs the second is taken by the first.

System 1

Fast, associative and effortless. It builds a coherent story from the information available and does not signal what is missing, which Kahneman summarises as "what you see is all there is".

System 2

Slow, effortful and capable of checking. It is activated when the stakes are made explicit or when a procedure requires it, as a checklist or a written question does.

Typical process in a commercial meetingIdeal process
The first proposal presented sets the reference for the discussionThe problem is defined before any proposal is discussed
Evidence is gathered to support the preferred optionCriteria are set before the alternatives are evaluated
Alternatives are compared by the confidence of their sponsorsSeveral alternatives are generated and compared on the same criteria
The decision is not reviewed against resultsThe decision states in advance what result would reverse it

Kahneman (2011); Bazerman and Moore (2013), ch. 1

Five biases that shape commercial proposals

Each bias is a systematic deviation, and for each there is a question that brings System 2 back into the meeting.

BiasWhat happensHow it appears in a commercial meetingQuestion that counters it
ConfirmationEvidence that supports the belief is sought and weighed moreOnly the outlets where an initiative worked are shownWhat result would show that the proposal is wrong?
AnchoringThe first number heard pulls later estimates towards itNext year's budget starts from this year's figureWould we reach the same number starting from zero?
SurvivorshipOnly the cases that remained are observedSuccess factors are read from the customers still activeWho is missing from the data, and why?
AvailabilityRecent or vivid cases are judged more frequentA large customer lost last month dominates the planHow often does it happen, counted over the full base?
OverconfidencePrecision of one's own estimates is overratedA single headline metric is presented as settledCompared with what, and how could the metric mislead?

Tversky and Kahneman (1974); Kahneman (2011); Kahneman, Lovallo and Sibony (2011)
