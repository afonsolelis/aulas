Five types of question and the chart each one calls for

A chart is chosen after the question is stated, because each type of question asks the reader to make a different comparison and each chart makes one comparison easier than the others.

Question typeWhat the reader comparesUsual chartMaré Alta example
ComparisonSize of items at one momentBar chart sorted by value, axis from zeroVolume by region in FY26
TrendChange of a measure over timeLine chart, time on the horizontal axisGross margin from FY24 to FY26
CompositionParts of a totalStacked bar or a single 100% barHow the FY26 envelope was spent
DistributionHow values spread across unitsHistogram or box plotVolume growth across the 1,150 outlets
RelationshipWhether two measures move togetherScatter plot, coloured by segmentDiscount depth against volume by outlet
Cleveland and McGill (1984) showed that readers judge position on a common scale more accurately than length, and length more accurately than angle or area. That ranking explains why bars and lines usually serve a decision better than pies, gauges and bubbles.
Trend: gross margin from FY24 to FY26

Both charts use the three public values of the case table (50.1%, 48.4% and 46.6%), and only the second lets the reader judge the size of the fall and the distance to the board's 48%.

Weaker choice

The bars start at 46%, so a fall of 3.5 points looks like a collapse of more than 80%. Bar length is read as size, and a bar must therefore start at zero.

Better choice

A line encodes change by its slope, so its axis may start above zero if the scale is labelled. The reference line places the target on the same scale, and the title can then state the message.

Comparison: measures with different scales

Volume is measured in thousand hectolitres and margin in percentage points, and the way the two are put on one chart decides what the reader believes about their relationship.

Weaker choice

With two vertical scales, the author chooses where the line crosses the bars. The crossing suggests that volume pushed margin down, a relationship the chart does not test.

Better choice

Indexing every series to FY24 = 100 puts them on one scale. Gross profit is net revenue times gross margin, computed from the public table, and the chart shows how each measure grew without asking why.

Composition: the parts of the commercial envelope

A composition chart answers what a total is made of at one moment, and a pie used for a series of years answers a trend question with the least accurate visual encoding.

Weaker choice

Years are not parts of a whole, the order of time is lost around the circle and the reader must compare angles without values. The question here is a trend, and a line or bars would answer it.

Better choice

One bar with direct labels shows the parts and the total together. The values come from the case document: R$ 22.2 million invested, R$ 5.2 million of trade spend and R$ 2.7 million for the Cooler Programme.

Distribution and relationship: looking behind an average

The three executives argue with averages and totals, and the outlet-level tables of the extract allow the group to see how those numbers spread and what else moves with them. The shapes below are illustrative and contain no case data.

Distribution · histogram

A few outlets with very high growth pull the mean above the typical outlet. Ask how many outlets produce the average before the average justifies a budget.

Relationship · scatter by segment

The overall line looks steep, yet inside each segment the points barely rise. Colour the points by segment and ask whether the comparison holds among outlets of the same tier.

Building the chart with Copilot in Excel

Copilot answers better when the prompt names the table, the period, the metric and the grouping, and when it states the fiscal year of the company, which runs from October to September.

Prompt 1 · build the chart (curated workbook)
In the table sales_monthly, add a column fiscal_year: months from October to September belong to the fiscal year that ends in that September (October 2025 to September 2026 is FY26).
Then create a PivotTable with fiscal_year in rows and the sums of net_revenue_brl and cogs_brl.
Add gross margin % = (net_revenue_brl − cogs_brl) / net_revenue_brl.
Insert a line chart of gross margin % by fiscal year, with the vertical axis labelled in %.
Write the chart title as one sentence that states what the line shows.
Prompt 2 · check before using
Show in a table the totals of net revenue and COGS for each fiscal year and the formula used for gross margin %, so that I can compare them with the case document.
Change the question, change the chart

For a comparison, ask for a bar chart sorted by value with the axis from zero. For a composition, ask for one stacked bar. For a relationship, ask for a scatter plot coloured by tier.

Copilot in Excel works on data formatted as an Excel table, which the workbook of the case already provides. The sales table has about 100,000 rows, so a question asked on a PivotTable or on a smaller table is answered faster, and every number Copilot returns is checked against a total the group already knows.

Strategic, tactical and operational dashboards

A dashboard is designed for one audience and one rhythm of decision, since the board and a sales supervisor decide at different rhythms and need different levels of detail (Few, 2006; Eckerson, 2010).

TypeWho reads itRhythmDecision it supportsMaré Alta example
StrategicBoard, CFO, executive committeeMonthly or quarterlyWhether the strategy and the budget are on courseGross margin against the 48% path; use of the R$ 22 million envelope
TacticalHeads of sales, revenue management, dataWeeklyWhich programme, region or channel needs correctionDiscount depth by channel; results of the outlets that received an action
OperationalSupervisors and sales representativesDailyWhat to do today at which outletOutlets without orders in the last 30 days, by route

A strategic dashboard has few indicators, each with a target and a trend, because the board reads it to decide whether to intervene, and detail at that level only delays the decision.

A tactical dashboard allows the reader to break an indicator down by region, channel or tier, because its reader has to locate where a deviation comes from before acting on it.

What makes an indicator a KPI

An indicator becomes a key performance indicator when it is tied to a decision and written so precisely that two analysts computing it from the same data arrive at the same number.

FieldExample: gross margin
Definition(Net revenue − COGS) / net revenue, excluding trade spend
Sourcesales_monthly, curated release
Current value46.6% in FY26 (case table)
TargetPath back to 48%, as signalled by the board
FrequencyMonthly, read in fiscal-year cumulative terms
OwnerThe executive who answers for the deviation
Lagging and leading

Gross margin is a lagging indicator, which confirms the result after the year. A leading indicator, such as discount depth or active outlets per month, moves earlier and allows correction while the year is still open.

When the target distorts the measure

A measure turned into a target invites behaviour that improves the number without improving the business (Strathern, 1997). For each KPI, ask how it could be met in a way the company would not want.

OKRs: objectives and key results

The method, which Andy Grove applied at Intel and John Doerr described in Measure What Matters (2018), links a qualitative objective to three to five measurable key results, so that progress can be verified at the end of the period.

Objective

Describes what the area wants to achieve in the period, in words that are significant, concrete and oriented to action. It is not measured directly.

Key results

Specify how the objective will be verified: each one has a number, a deadline and a source, and at the end of the period it is either met or not.

KPI and OKR

KPIs monitor the health of the business continuously, while an OKR concentrates effort on a change for one period. A KPI that deviates is often the reason an OKR is written.

Example outside the case · logistics of a beverage distributor
Objective  Make deliveries in the Northeast as reliable as in the Southeast before the summer season.
KR1  On-time, in-full deliveries in the Northeast rise from the current value to the Southeast level by March.
KR2  Outlets of the acquired distributor managed in the corporate system reach 100% by January.
KR3  Returns caused by delivery errors fall by half against the same quarter of the previous year.
Doerr distinguishes committed OKRs, which the area must meet in full, from aspirational ones, which are set above what the area expects to reach. The board needs to know which kind it is reading.
Practice 2: the board view of the FY27 decision

Whatever the group recommends, the board will want to follow it month by month, and choosing the indicators now forces the group to say what success would look like.

Each group writes

  • One leading indicator that would show within 90 days whether the recommendation is working.
  • One OKR for FY27 that the recommended initiative would serve, with no more than three key results.
Questions to test the choice

  • Would the indicator move if the recommendation failed, or does it move for other reasons?
  • How could a team meet the target without improving the margin?

The Pyramid Principle: answer first, then the support

Barbara Minto developed the principle at McKinsey from a simple observation about readers: they grasp a set of ideas faster when the idea that summarises them comes first and each level below answers the question the level above raises.

Governing thoughtThe single recommendation the reader must retain, stated as a complete sentence.
Key line 1Reason or step that supports it
Key line 2Reason or step that supports it
Key line 3Reason or step that supports it
EvidenceData, chart, source
EvidenceData, chart, source
EvidenceData, chart, source
Vertical logic

Each statement raises a question in the reader (why, how, which), and the level below answers it. The board can stop reading at any level and still leave with the decision.

Horizontal logic

Ideas in one group are of the same kind and do not overlap, and they follow an order: time, structure or importance. Deductive chains are kept short.

SCQA: the introduction that leads to the answer

Minto opens a recommendation with a short story the reader already accepts, so that the question arises naturally and the governing thought arrives as its answer.

SSituation

A fact the reader knows and does not dispute, which sets the context.

CComplication

What changed or went wrong in that situation and makes a decision necessary.

QQuestion

The question the complication raises in the reader's mind, often implicit.

AAnswer

The governing thought, which the rest of the document supports.

Example outside the case · returnable bottles
S  Returnable bottles carry most of the on-trade volume and their float is planned once a year.
C  Two regions ran short of bottles in the last summer peak, while a third held stock above plan.
Q  How should the float be allocated before the next peak?
A  Rebalance the float between regions by December and review it monthly in the peak, because total stock covered demand and the shortage came from its allocation.
Knaflic (2015) applies the same discipline to slides: the title of each slide states its message, and a reader who scans only the titles should be able to repeat the recommendation.

Practice 3: governing thought and SCQA

The group writes the top of its pyramid before building the page, because a governing thought that does not fit in one sentence usually means the decision has not yet been made.

1Answer

Write the governing thought in one sentence, with what is recommended and the main reason.

2SCQA

Write S, C and Q so that the question leads to that answer and S contains nothing Helena would dispute.

3Key line

List the reasons, check that they do not overlap and order them by importance to the board.

4Test

Read the top aloud to another group and ask which question it raises first.

The three executive proposals can appear in the recommendation as options considered, as part of the answer or as alternatives set aside, and in each case the page explains the reason. The group may also recommend a combination or a sequence, provided it fits the envelope.

Case closing: from insight to executive recommendation

For the next hour the groups produce the document Helena asked for and test it against the questions she and the board would ask, which is the work the case document assigns to the working group.

The page contains
  • Governing thought and SCQA from Practice 3.
  • Key line with the evidence of Day 2 and its source.
  • One chart, chosen by the type of question, with a title that states the message.
  • The conclusion the group discarded and why.
  • The KPI to follow and the condition to change course.
The constraints that apply
  • Envelope of R$ 22 million for invoice discounts and trade spend in FY27.
  • A credible path back to a gross margin of 48%.
  • Installed coolers stay for the year.
  • Off-trade discounts are decided before the January renegotiation.
  • One recommendation, which may combine elements of the three proposals.
There is no winning recommendation and no grade. The class compares how each group reasoned, and two groups that reach different decisions can both be right if each one can show why it believes what it recommends.
