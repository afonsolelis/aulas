Why AI governance belongs to leadership

An AI initiative moves data, automates part of a decision and exposes the company to new failure modes, and each of these effects carries a risk that someone with authority has to accept, reduce or refuse.

Data

Whose data enters the system

Customer, outlet and employee data are used for purposes the company has to justify, and the legal basis is a decision of the business owner of the process before it is a task for the legal team.

Decision

Who answers for the output

When a score or a generated text shapes a commercial decision, the company remains responsible for that decision, so the leader has to define where a person reviews the output and who can contest it.

Exposure

What can go wrong in use

Generative AI adds risks that a dashboard does not have, such as instructions hidden in documents and confidential text pasted into tools outside the company's control.

Governance in this module means the set of decisions, roles and controls that define which AI uses the area accepts, under what conditions and with whose approval. Its frameworks help the partner ask the right questions, and the answers remain with the area, the data protection officer and the legal and security teams.
LGPD and the data of customers and outlets

Lei 13.709/2018 protects natural persons, and a business-to-business operation still processes personal data, because behind each outlet there are owners, contacts, delivery staff and sales representatives.

Data in a commercial operationPersonal data?
Outlet registered as a company: volume, channel, tierGenerally no, since it describes a legal entity.
Name, phone and tax number of the owner of a small outlet or sole proprietorYes, it identifies a natural person.
Performance and route of a sales representativeYes, and it bears on employment decisions.
Consumer data from apps, promotions or loyalty programmesYes, often at large scale.
What the law asks
  • A legal basis for each purpose, among the ten listed in art. 7, such as contract or legitimate interest.
  • The principles of art. 6, among them purpose, necessity, transparency, security and accountability.
  • Records of processing (art. 37) and, when requested, a data protection impact report (art. 38).
Data collected to deliver beer and invoice an outlet does not automatically authorise its use to train a model or to profile the owner, because the principle of purpose limits later uses to those compatible with the original one.

Brasil, Lei nº 13.709/2018 (LGPD), arts. 5, 6, 7, 37 and 38

LGPD, AI outputs and the role of the ANPD

An AI output can itself be personal data or the basis of a decision about a person, and the LGPD gives that person rights that the area has to be able to honour.

Art. 20

Review of automated decisions

The data subject may request the review of decisions taken solely on the basis of automated processing that affect his or her interests, including profiling, and the controller must explain the criteria used, subject to trade secrets.

Arts. 46 and 48

Security and incidents

The controller adopts security measures and communicates to the ANPD and to the data subjects any incident that may cause relevant risk or damage, such as a leak of personal data through an AI tool.

ANPD

The national authority

The Autoridade Nacional de Proteção de Dados regulates, supervises and sanctions under the LGPD. Fines reach 2% of the group's revenue in Brazil, limited to R$ 50 million per infraction (art. 52).

The ANPD already acts on AI. In July 2024 it ordered, as a preventive measure, the suspension of Meta's use of personal data of Brazilian users to train generative AI models, and the Senate version of PL 2338/2023 assigns to it the coordination of the national AI regulation system.

LGPD, arts. 20, 46, 48 and 52; ANPD, Despacho Decisório nº 20/2024/PR/ANPD; Lei nº 14.460/2022

The EU AI Act and its risk tiers

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 classifies AI systems by the risk their use creates, and it reaches companies outside the EU when their systems or the outputs of those systems are used in the Union.

Unacceptable riskPractices prohibited by art. 5Emotion recognition of employees in the workplace; social scoringProhibited since 2 February 2025

The Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 with staggered dates: obligations for general-purpose AI models since 2 August 2025 and most high-risk obligations from 2 August 2026. The Commission proposed in November 2025 to adjust the high-risk timetable, so the dates in force should be checked before they are used in a plan. AI literacy of staff is required since February 2025 (art. 4).

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, arts. 2, 4, 5, 6, 50, 113 and Annex III

Brazil: PL 2338/2023 is still a bill

Brazil has no general AI law in force, and the main proposal, PL 2338/2023, follows the risk-based logic of the European regulation, so a company that organises its AI uses by risk today is preparing for either outcome.

Legislative status
  • Introduced in the Federal Senate in May 2023.
  • Approved by the Senate, in a substitute text, in December 2024.
  • Sent to the Chamber of Deputies, where it was under examination at the time this material was written.
  • Text and status can change; check camara.leg.br before quoting it.
What the Senate text proposes
  • Classification of systems by risk, with uses of excessive risk prohibited and high-risk uses subject to stricter duties.
  • A preliminary assessment by the developer or deployer and an algorithmic impact assessment for high-risk systems.
  • Rights of people affected by AI decisions, such as explanation and human review.
  • A national system of AI regulation coordinated by the ANPD.
Until a law is approved, the LGPD already applies to every AI use that processes personal data, and sectoral rules, consumer law and labour law continue to apply to decisions supported by AI.

Senado Federal, PL 2338/2023, text approved in plenary in December 2024

NIST AI RMF: four functions to manage AI risk

The framework of the US National Institute of Standards and Technology is voluntary and sector-neutral, and its four functions translate into questions that a leader can ask about any initiative.

GovernCulture, roles and policies

Who is accountable for the system, which policy applies and how the organisation decides which risks it accepts. Govern runs across the other three functions.

MapContext and impact

What the system is for, who is affected by its outputs, which data it uses and what happens if it is wrong.

MeasureEvidence about the risk

Which metrics show whether the system works and stays reliable, fair and secure, and who tests it before and after launch.

ManageTreatment and response

Which risks are reduced, transferred or accepted, how incidents are handled and when the system is stopped.

The Generative AI Profile (NIST AI 600-1, 2024) applies the same functions to generative systems and names risks such as confabulation, the term NIST uses for confident but false outputs, data privacy and information security.

NIST AI 100-1 (2023); NIST AI 600-1 (2024)

Typical risks of generative AI in daily use

These four risks appear as soon as a team uses Copilot, Genie or an agent in its routine, and each of them has a control that leadership can require.

RiskHow it happensWhat leadership requires
Prompt injectionInstructions hidden in an email, a document or a web page are read by the assistant or agent as if they came from the user, and change what it does.Agents act only with the permissions they need, and actions that send, pay or delete require human confirmation.
Data leakageConfidential prices, contracts or personal data are pasted into a tool outside the company environment, or an assistant exposes documents to people without access.A data classification that says what may enter each tool, and access permissions reviewed before an assistant is switched on.
HallucinationThe model produces a figure, a source or a conclusion that sounds right and is false, as discussed on Day 2.Verification of every number and source before the output reaches a decision, and the name of the person who checked it.
Shadow AITeams adopt tools that nobody approved because the approved ones are slow to reach them.An approved list that is easy to use, a fast route to request new tools and a policy that people know.

OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications (2025): LLM01 Prompt Injection, LLM02 Sensitive Information Disclosure, LLM09 Misinformation; NIST AI 600-1 (2024)

What leadership owns and what it delegates

Technical controls are delegated to data, security and legal teams, while the decisions about purpose, acceptable risk and accountability stay with the partner who sponsors the initiative.

The partner decides
  • The purpose of the initiative and the decision it supports.
  • Which risks the area accepts and under which conditions it stops.
  • Where a person reviews the output before it affects a customer, an outlet or an employee.
  • Who in the area is accountable for the system after launch.
The partner delegates and checks
  • The legal basis and the impact report, to the data protection officer and the legal team.
  • Access, logging and security controls, to IT and security.
  • Model metrics and monitoring, to the data team.
  • Training and communication, to the area's managers.
In the case, Daniel proposes to fund a retention offer for every outlet that the churn model flags. Before any discussion of value, a leader would ask who approved the use of the score in a commercial routine, what data about the outlet owners the model uses, how an outlet could contest the decision and who monitors the model once the offer is running.

Prioritising by impact and feasibility

The matrix compares initiatives on two axes defined before scoring, which forces the area to state why one initiative comes before another instead of following the loudest sponsor.

Impact on the decision
High impact, high feasibility

Start now, with a pilot and a KPI agreed in advance.

High impact, low feasibility

Plan for a later horizon and start with what removes the barrier, such as the data.

Low impact, high feasibility

Do only if it is cheap and builds capability for larger initiatives.

Low impact, low feasibility

Set aside and record why, so the discussion does not return every quarter.

Feasibility, from high (left) to low (right)
Criteria for each axis

Impact covers value in money or time, the number of decisions affected and the alignment with the area's targets.

Feasibility covers whether the data exists and can be trusted, whether the tools are available in the company, the effort of people, the regulatory risk seen in the first block and whether the team will adopt the result.

The scores are estimates, and two partners scoring the same initiative will disagree. That disagreement is useful when the criteria are written down, because it shows which premise each one is making.
Feasibility includes data, risk and adoption

Initiatives tend to be scored as feasible when the technology works, and the Day 2 discussion of where projects go wrong shows that data, privacy and adoption fail more often than the model.

Data

Is the data there and reliable?

Day 1 showed how a raw extract can mislead. An initiative that depends on reconciling two systems should be scored lower until the reconciliation is done.

Risk

Does it fall into a regulated use?

An initiative that profiles people or evaluates employees needs a legal basis, human review and documentation, which adds time and cost to the plan.

Adoption

Will a routine use the output?

The case document records that the churn model of Maré Alta has been running since early 2026 without being used in any commercial routine, which is an adoption problem before it is a model problem.

The Caderno of Day 2 already holds most of this information: the premise of the value, the risks of data, privacy and adoption, and the effort. The matrix uses it to compare the Day 2 initiative with the other ideas of the area.
A data and AI roadmap in three horizons

The roadmap sequences the initiatives over time, so that the first horizon delivers results with what the area already has and prepares the conditions that the later horizons depend on.

H1 · 0–6 monthsUse what exists

Initiatives of high impact and high feasibility, with approved tools and available data. Examples are a Genie space over trusted tables or a Copilot routine for a recurring report.

H2 · 6–18 monthsBuild the conditions

Data quality, integration of sources, governance and skills that the larger initiatives need, together with the pilots that test them.

H3 · 18 months or moreChange the decision

Initiatives of high impact that are not yet feasible, such as predictive models embedded in commercial routines or agents that act on behalf of the team.

The horizons adapt the three-horizon model of Baghai, Coley and White, written for growth portfolios, to a data and AI portfolio. The 30-60-90 plan of the Caderno is the first part of H1 seen at the scale of what the partner can start personally.

Baghai, Coley and White (1999)

Caderno do Sócio, Day 3: my 30-60-90 plan

The next hour belongs to the Caderno. Each partner writes the plan of the area in the template, starting from the Day 2 initiative and from the H1 initiatives of Practice 2.

What to fill in
  • First 30 days, up to 60 days and up to 90 days: three milestones each, with who and how I know it happened.
  • What I need to unblock: what, with whom and when I will talk.
  • The commitments come later, in the closing block.
Rules written in the Caderno
  • The plan is of your area, not of the company.
  • Avoid milestones that depend on approval from others or on new budget.
  • Write what you can start on your own.
  • Write little and be specific: an indicator with a name and a number helps more than a paragraph of intention.
The Caderno is yours. It is not graded and is not handed to anyone, and the professor will only ask, at the end of the hour, who wants to read one milestone aloud.

The thread of the three days

The case and the Caderno followed the same sequence, from a question to an analysis and from an analysis to a decision, and the closing returns to it so that each partner can name what will be done differently.

Day 1 · QuestionFrom the question to the decision

In the case, a business question with the decision it serves and the data it needs. In the Caderno, the recurring decisions of the area and where data would change one of them.

Day 2 · AnalysisFrom the analysis to the insight

In the case, results interpreted and at least one conclusion discarded after checking. In the Caderno, one initiative with value, premise, KPI, risks and a stop condition.

Day 3 · RecommendationFrom the insight to the action

In the case, a recommendation to the board told as a narrative. In the Caderno, a 30-60-90 plan and personal commitments.

The question for each partner is which habit of the last three days will survive Monday: asking for the decision behind a request, asking how a number was checked, or asking for the stop condition of an initiative before approving it.

About this session

Module 6 — Leading an AI-Driven Organization · 09/12/2026 · Prof. Egon

Learning objective

By the end of the session, participants should be able to identify the obligations that the LGPD, the EU AI Act, Brazil's PL 2338/2023 and the NIST AI RMF place on a data or AI initiative of their area, state which risk decisions belong to leadership, prioritise initiatives on an impact and feasibility matrix and in a roadmap of three horizons, and write in the Caderno do Sócio a 30-60-90 plan with milestones, owners and evidence, together with three dated commitments, one thing to stop doing and one team member to involve.

Session strategy

Closing afternoon of the programme, in four blocks of the official plan. The first block covers governance and risk: LGPD and the ANPD, the EU AI Act risk tiers, the status of PL 2338/2023 as a bill, the NIST AI RMF and the typical risks of generative AI. It ends with a risk review of each partner's Day 2 initiative in pairs. The second block presents the impact and feasibility matrix and a roadmap in three horizons, with a practice on three initiatives of the area. After the break, an hour of protected time is reserved for the Day 3 section of the Caderno: individual writing of the 30-60-90 plan, with optional English prompts for Microsoft Copilot, then review in pairs. The integrated closing goes back over the thread of the three days, from question to analysis to recommendation and from Caderno Day 1 to Day 3, collects the commitments and ends with a short round. The Maré Alta case serves only as a shared example, and there is no grade.
