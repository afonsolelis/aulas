Variability: what an average hides

Every business number moves even when nothing has changed, and a difference is only informative when it is large compared with that ordinary movement.

Season

Months are not comparable

At Maré Alta a summer month can sell 40% more than a winter month, so a comparison between January and July measures the season. Full fiscal years, or the same months of two years, remove that effect.

Spread

Groups overlap

Two groups of outlets can have different averages while most outlets of one group sit inside the range of the other. The spread around each average says how much the difference between them means.

Size

Small groups swing

The growth of ten outlets moves a lot with one large customer, whereas the growth of a thousand moves little. The number of outlets behind each percentage is part of the result.

A partner who receives "group A grew more than group B" asks three things before discussing the cause: over which months, how many outlets are in each group, and how much the outlets inside each group differ from one another.
Confidence interval: the range a result is compatible with

An estimate drawn from a sample comes with a range of values that the data cannot rule out, and the decision should hold across that range rather than only at the central value.

Illustrative beverage example

A pilot of a new 1-litre returnable bottle in 40 bars

The pilot reports an average volume increase of 6%, with a 95% confidence interval from −2% to +14%. The data is compatible with no effect at all and also with a large one, because 40 bars with very different volumes do not narrow the range enough.

The defensible decision is to extend the pilot or enlarge it before a national rollout, since the investment case would be positive only in part of the interval.

How to read it
  • The interval is wider when the sample is small or the outlets differ a lot.
  • An interval that contains zero does not prove that there is no effect; it says the data cannot tell.
  • The question for the team is whether the decision changes at each end of the interval.

Numbers of the example are illustrative and do not come from the case. Spiegelhalter (2019), ch. 7.

Correlation vs causation: four alternative explanations

When a group that received an action performs better, the action is one explanation among several, and each alternative suggests a specific comparison that can rule it in or out.

Selection

Who chose the units

If the sales team picked the outlets, it may have picked those it already expected to grow. The comparison has to take into account how the group was formed.

Confounding

A third factor

Location, channel or outlet potential can drive both joining the programme and growing. Comparing outlets that share that factor removes its effect.

Prior trend

Growth before the action

A group that was already growing faster before the action will look better afterwards. The period before the action shows whether the gap is new.

Reverse direction

Effect causes the cause

Outlets that sell more may be the ones that ask for a cooler, so volume would explain the cooler and not the opposite.

The strongest evidence of cause comes from assigning the action by a rule that does not depend on the expected result, such as a random draw or a staggered rollout. When the 400 new outlets are discussed, the board can ask how they would be chosen.

Pearl and Mackenzie (2018); Angrist and Pischke (2014)

Statistical significance and practical significance

A significant result says that a difference is unlikely to be produced by chance alone, and it says nothing about whether the difference is large enough to pay for the decision.

What a p-value is

The probability of observing a difference at least as large as the one found if there were no real effect. It is not the probability that the effect is real, and the 0.05 threshold is a convention adopted by the field.

Large data, small effects

With 100,000 sales rows almost any difference becomes significant. The question for the decision is the size of the effect in hectolitres or reais and the range around it.

Many cuts

When a team tests twenty segments, about one of them will look significant at 5% by chance alone. Ask how many cuts were tried before the one presented.

A significant difference can still be biased: significance measures chance, and the four alternative explanations of the previous slide are not chance. A result can pass the test and still fail the comparison of like with like.

Wasserstein and Lazar (2016), ASA Statement on p-values

Databricks, Genie and Copilot in the case

The three tools read the same curated release, and they differ in who writes the query and in how easily the partner can see what was calculated.

Databricks

The platform where the tables live

The five CSV files are loaded as tables in a schema such as mare_alta_curated, with the column descriptions of the data dictionary registered. Analysts query them in SQL notebooks.

Genie

Questions in natural language

Genie, the AI/BI assistant of Databricks, answers questions over a space set up on those tables. It writes the SQL, runs it and shows the query, which the partner can open and read.

Copilot in Excel

Analysis inside the workbook

Copilot works on data formatted as an Excel table and proposes formulas, pivots and charts. The curated workbook already has one table per sheet; the smaller tables answer faster.

In the case, Genie suits questions that join sales to outlets over 100,000 rows, and Copilot suits the smaller tables, such as pos and churn_scores. Using both on the same question is also a way of checking one against the other.

A verification habit for every number

An answer from Genie or Copilot becomes evidence for the group only after three checks, which take a few minutes and catch most errors of period, filter and definition.

1Ask for the query or formula

Read the SQL or the Excel formula and check the period, the filters and the column used. A missing filter or a calendar year shows up in the text of the query.

2Reconcile a known total

Ask for a total that the case document already gives, such as net revenue per fiscal year (R$ 145.0, 167.9 and 194.9 million), and compare it before trusting a finer cut.

3Re-ask differently

Ask the same question in other words, in the other tool, or from the opposite direction. A result that changes when the wording changes is not yet a result.

If the reconciled total does not match, the group stops and finds out why before going further. The difference can come from returns, from the release used or from the fiscal year, and each of these also affects the finer cuts.

Types of machine learning and the decisions they support

The type of learning follows from the question the business asks, and naming it tells the partner what the model can deliver and how it should be judged.

TypeWhat it learnsBeverage exampleHow it is judged

Supervised regressionA quantity from labelled past casesHectolitres an outlet will buy next quarterSize of the error in hectolitres

Unsupervised clusteringGroups without a labelSegments of outlets with similar buying patternsWhether the segments are stable and usable
Daniel's churn model is a supervised classifier: it learned from outlets that did or did not stop buying and gives each outlet a score from 0 to 1, which becomes a yes or no at the threshold of 0.5 chosen by the analytics team.

Provost and Fawcett (2013), ch. 2 and 7

Confusion matrix: a generic beverage example

The matrix counts the four possible outcomes of a yes-or-no prediction, and every metric the team reports is a ratio between these four cells.

Illustrative example, not from the case

Credit risk in 1,000 bars

A distributor predicts which of its 1,000 bars on 30-day credit will fall more than 60 days behind next quarter. In reality 50 do. The model flags 100 bars, and 30 of them are among the 50.

Really fell behindPaid on time
Flagged30true positive70false positive
Not flagged20false negative880true negative
What each error costs

A false positive is a good customer whose credit is cut, which costs sales and relationship. A false negative is a default the company did not see coming, which costs the unpaid invoices.

The two errors rarely cost the same, so the choice of which one to tolerate belongs to the business, which makes it together with the data team.

Accuracy, precision and recall in the same example

Each metric answers a different business question, and accuracy can look good on a rare event even when the model finds few of the cases that matter.

MetricCalculationExampleQuestion it answers
Accuracy(TP + TN) / all(30 + 880) / 1,000 = 91%How often is the model right overall?
PrecisionTP / flagged30 / 100 = 30%Of the bars we act on, how many needed it?
RecallTP / real cases30 / 50 = 60%Of the bars that fell behind, how many did we catch?
Base rateReal cases / all50 / 1,000 = 5%How rare is the event?
The trivial model

A rule that flags no bar at all is right on the 950 that paid, so its accuracy is 95%, higher than the model's. Accuracy should always be compared with this trivial rule.

Cost per case found

If each flagged bar costs a R$ 300 visit, the action costs R$ 30,000 and finds 30 real cases, or R$ 1,000 per case found. That is the number the decision weighs.

Reliability criteria and the analysis log

A conclusion enters the recommendation only when it meets the criteria below, and the log keeps the conclusions that failed so that the group can explain to Helena why it set them aside.

Reliability criteria
  • Provenance: release, tables and columns are named.
  • Reconciliation: a known total was matched first.
  • Reproducibility: the same answer came back when re-asked.
  • Comparability: like was compared with like, before and after.
  • Size: each group has enough outlets for its percentage.
Analysis log, one row per conclusion
  • Claim, in one sentence.
  • Source of the number: prompt, tool and query.
  • Check performed: reconciliation, re-asking, comparison.
  • What the check showed, described in words.
  • Status: kept, qualified or discarded.
A discarded conclusion is a result of the analysis. It tells the board which reading of the data the group examined and why it does not support the budget, which is part of what Helena asked for.
