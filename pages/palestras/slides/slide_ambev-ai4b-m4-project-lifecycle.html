Five stages of analytical maturity

Davenport and Harris describe how organisations move from isolated reports to analytics as a basis for competing, and the stage of an area determines which projects it can absorb today.

StageWhat characterises itWhat a project usually needs at this stage
1 · Analytically impairedData is missing or of poor quality, and decisions rest on experience and intuition.Basic data quality and agreement on a few definitions before any model.
2 · Localised analyticsIsolated reports and analyses inside functions, without coordination between them.A shared metric and a sponsor who can align two or more areas.
3 · Analytical aspirationsLeadership wants analytics, but data, people and processes are still being built.A first project with visible value that justifies the investment in data.
4 · Analytical companiesAnalytics is used across the business with enterprise data, but not yet as a distinctive capability.Portfolio management: prioritising, scaling and stopping initiatives.
5 · Analytical competitorsAnalytics underpins the strategy, and the organisation competes on it.Continuous renewal of models and governance of their risks.

Davenport and Harris, Competing on Analytics (2007; updated edition 2017)

The DELTA elements and the four analytical questions

An area rarely sits at the same stage in every dimension, and DELTA separates the elements so that the weakest one, which usually limits the next project, becomes visible.

DELTA · Davenport, Harris and Morison (2010)
  • Data that is accessible, integrated and of known quality.
  • Enterprise orientation, so that data and analysts serve more than one function.
  • Leadership that asks for evidence and acts on it.
  • Targets, meaning a few decisions where analytics is concentrated.
  • Analysts with the skills to frame and answer business questions.
Four questions, in increasing difficulty
  • Descriptive: what happened to volume and margin in FY26?
  • Diagnostic: why did the margin fall while volume grew?
  • Predictive: which outlets are likely to stop buying next quarter?
  • Prescriptive: where should the R$ 22 million envelope go to reach a 48% margin?
Each step up the ladder depends on the one below it, so a prescriptive question asked in an area whose descriptive numbers are disputed tends to produce a sophisticated answer that nobody trusts. Later versions of the model add technology and analytical techniques to the five elements.
CRISP-DM: the six phases of a data project

The Cross-Industry Standard Process for Data Mining was published in 2000 by a consortium of companies and remains the reference cycle for analytics projects, because it begins and ends with the business and allows returns between phases.

1Business understanding

Objective, decision to be improved, success criterion and constraints.

2Data understanding

Which data exists, where it comes from and what its quality is.

3Data preparation

Cleaning, joining and reconciling sources, usually the longest phase.

4Modelling

Choice and training of techniques, from a simple rule to machine learning.

5Evaluation

Checking the result against the business criterion set in phase 1.

6Deployment

Putting the result into a routine, with an owner, monitoring and review.

Where the partner acts

Phases 1, 5 and 6 depend on business decisions: what counts as success, whether the evidence is good enough and who will change their routine. The technical team cannot settle them alone.

Returns are part of the method

Data understanding often sends the team back to the business question, and evaluation can send it back to the start, which is why a plan without time for returns underestimates the project.

Chapman et al., CRISP-DM 1.0: Step-by-step data mining guide (2000)

Five points where data and AI projects go wrong

The five points rarely appear in the technical report, because they sit between the business and the data team, and each one has a question that the sponsor can ask before the money is committed.

1Goal alignment

The project optimises a metric that is different from the one by which the business will judge it.

2Data integration

Sources with different keys, units and definitions are joined as if they meant the same thing.

3Privacy

Personal data is used beyond the purpose for which it was collected, or without a legal basis.

4Ethics

The model reproduces an old classification or treats groups differently without anyone deciding so.

5ROI measurement

No baseline or comparison group exists, so the gain cannot be separated from what would have happened anyway.

Question the sponsor asks

Which number will the board look at in twelve months, what is its value today, and how will we know the change came from the project?

Why the technical team misses them

Sculley and colleagues showed that the model code is a small part of a real machine learning system, surrounded by data pipelines, configuration and monitoring that accumulate maintenance cost.

Goal alignment and ROI in the three proposals

The three executives of Maré Alta defend their proposals with three different metrics, while the board has stated the one it will use, and this gap is a goal-alignment problem before it is a data problem.

ProposalMetric used to defend itQuestion about the return
Rafael · Cooler Programme to 400 more outletsVolume growth of programme outlets against the othersHow would the 400 outlets be chosen, and would they resemble the first 236?
Beatriz · discounts back to FY25 levelMargin recovered on the current volumeWhat does the estimate assume about how volume reacts to the change?
Daniel · retention offer for flagged outletsMargin of the outlets that churned and model accuracyHow many of the flagged outlets would have stayed without the offer?
Board · Helena's constraintGross margin back to 48% within a R$ 22 million envelopeWhich of the three metrics translates into this one, and through which premise?
A return is measured against what would have happened without the project. Without a baseline and a comparison group decided before the start, any growth after the project tends to be attributed to it.
Data integration after an acquisition

Maré Alta bought a distributor in the Northeast in 2023, and its outlets still live in the LEGACY_NE system, which reaches the corporate platform through a monthly file that the data team has not fully reconciled.

What usually differs between two systems
  • Codes and labels for the same region, channel or product.
  • Units of measure and the period each record refers to.
  • Keys that identify the customer, and the master data that holds them.
  • Fields that one system fills and the other leaves empty.
Questions for the curated release note
  • What did the data team correct in LEGACY_NE, and on what evidence?
  • What did it leave as it was because the correction needed a business decision?
  • Would any conclusion of this morning change if LEGACY_NE outlets were removed?
  • Who owns the reconciliation from now on?
In most companies, integration is the phase that consumes the schedule, because each difference between systems requires someone with business authority to decide which version is correct, and that person is rarely in the project team.
Privacy and ethics in commercial models

Outlets are companies, but many small bars and mini-markets are run by individuals whose data is personal data under the LGPD, and models that decide who receives an offer raise questions of fairness.

Privacy · LGPD (Lei 13.709/2018)

Personal data may be processed only for a specified purpose, with a legal basis and limited to what is necessary (art. 6 and 7). The data subject may request review of decisions taken solely by automated processing that affect their interests (art. 20).

Ethics · who receives what

A model trained on an old classification reproduces it. In the case, the outlet tier was assigned by the sales team in 2023 and never reviewed, so a programme or an offer driven by tier inherits the judgement made at that time.

Question before approvingWhy it matters
What personal data does the project use, and for which purpose?It defines the legal basis and the controls required.
Who is left out by the rule or the model, and was that decided by someone?Exclusion that nobody decided cannot be defended to a regulator or to a customer.
Can a person review and reverse a decision of the model?It is a right under art. 20 and a form of the human oversight discussed in the NIST AI RMF.

Four kinds of AI assistance, from chat to agent

In the classical definition an agent perceives its environment and acts on it, and in current products the term describes a system in which a language model decides, within limits, which steps to take and which tools to use to reach a goal.

TypeWho decides the stepsExample in a beverage companyControl required
AssistantThe user, one question at a timeCopilot Chat summarising a supplier contractThe user reads and checks the answer.
Grounded assistantThe user; answers come from defined documentsA policy assistant that answers from the trade-spend policyThe document is current and has an owner.
Workflow with AIA fixed sequence written in advanceEach new outlet contract is classified and routed for approvalEach step is tested; the route does not change.
AgentThe model, within its instructions and toolsAn agent that checks orders, detects a stock-out risk and opens a replenishment requestPermissions, limits, logs and human approval for actions.

Russell and Norvig, Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach (4th ed., 2020); Anthropic, Building effective agents (2024)

Architecture of an AI agent

Every agent platform, including Copilot Studio, is configured through the same set of components, and each one corresponds to a decision that the business owner of the agent has to take.

Model

Language model

Interprets the request and writes the answer. The platform usually chooses it; the owner chooses the tasks it may handle.

Instructions

Role and rules

Text that defines the purpose, the audience, the tone and what the agent must refuse.

Tools

Actions in systems

Connectors that let the agent read or write in other systems, such as opening a request in Teams.

Knowledge

Grounding sources

Documents, sites or tables from which the answers must come, cited in each answer.

Memory

Context kept

What the agent retains within a conversation and, in some designs, between conversations.

Guardrails

Limits and filters

Topics it must not answer, content filters, data policy and the permissions of the identity it uses.

Human in the loop

Approval and escalation

Points where a person approves an action or receives the case the agent cannot resolve.

Channel

Where it is used

Teams, Microsoft 365 Copilot or a site, which defines who can reach it and with which login.

Microsoft Learn, Copilot Studio documentation; OpenAI, A practical guide to building agents (2025)

Requirements and cost drivers of an agent

Building the demo took minutes, while running it for a sales force of thousands requires the work below, which is where most of the cost of an agent sits.

DriverWhat determines itQuestion for the team
Licence and consumptionPer-user licences and usage billed per message or credit, which grows with volume and with each tool callHow many conversations a month, and what does one conversation cost?
KnowledgeDocuments that are current, versioned and have an owner who updates themWho owns the policy, and how does a new version reach the agent?
IntegrationConnectors to ERP, CRM or Teams, authentication and the permissions of the agent's identityWhich systems does it read or write, and under whose permission?
EvaluationA set of test questions with expected answers, run before each changeWhat share of test answers is correct and cited today?

Anatomy of an AI business case

The business case of the Caderno has the same fields as the one a sponsor presents to a committee, and its credibility depends less on the size of the gain than on whether the premise can be checked.

FieldWhat it contains
Problem and decisionThe business problem and the recurring decision the initiative improves
Expected valueGain in time, quality or cost, with the calculation written out
PremiseThe condition that must be true for the gain to happen
KPI and baselineThe indicator that shows the gain and its value today
RisksData, privacy or ethics, adoption by the team
EffortPeople, time, systems and what other areas must provide
Stop conditionThe result that, by a set date, ends the initiative
Expected value · Provost and Fawcett
value = p(benefit) × benefit
      − cost of acting
      − cost of running

Each term has a premise:
p    how often the action works
benefit   what one success is worth
cost      per case acted on, including
          the cases where it was useless
The stop condition is written before the start, when the sponsor can still judge it without the cost already spent weighing on the decision.
Worked example: Daniel's retention offer as a business case

The proposal is presented in the case document with a large figure and a model metric, and writing it in the fields of the previous slide shows which premises still have to be checked.

As proposed

177 outlets stopped buying after June 2026 and had generated about R$ 9.6 million of gross margin in the previous twelve months. The churn model is reported as 87% accurate, and the offer is R$ 2,000 for every outlet that the model flags, funded by the trade budget.

Premises behind the value
  • The outlets flagged are, for the most part, those that would leave.
  • Most of the outlets that leave are flagged.
  • R$ 2,000 changes the decision of an outlet that intends to leave.
  • An outlet kept brings more margin than the offer costs.
Questions before funding
  • What accuracy would a model that predicts that nobody leaves achieve?
  • How many flagged outlets would have stayed without the offer?
  • Which comparison group shows that the offer caused the retention?
  • What result, by which date, would stop the offer?
This morning's evaluation of the model answers part of these questions, and the class keeps its conclusions for the case closing on Day 3. Here the proposal serves only to show how a business case is written when its premise is fragile.
Criteria to prioritise, scale or stop an initiative

An initiative passes through gates, and at each one the sponsor asks for a different kind of evidence, which keeps a promising pilot from being scaled before its premise has been tested.

DecisionEvidence the sponsor asks forTypical criterion
PrioritiseValue, feasibility, available data, a decision owner and a sponsorHigh value with data that already exists and an owner who will change a routine
PilotA premise that can be tested in weeks, a baseline and a comparison groupSmall scope, fixed period and a KPI measured in both groups
ScaleKPI moved against the comparison group, adoption by users, unit cost within the valueThe gain holds in a second region or channel, with an operation owner
StopPremise refuted, KPI flat after the agreed period, cost per case above value, risk without mitigationThe stop condition written in the business case is met
Davenport and Ronanki recommend starting with a portfolio of pilots tied to business processes and scaling only those with demonstrated value. Prioritisation by impact and feasibility is developed in Module 6, on Day 3.

Davenport and Ronanki, Artificial intelligence for the real world, Harvard Business Review (2018); Duke, Quit (2022)
